Lithothèque

Three directions for the rock & deep-time app. Each tab is a playable sketch of one app idea — every specimen, find and date below is invented demo data, there to show what using the app would feel like.

Direction 01

The Gallery Hall

A luminous digital museum where every specimen is a small work of art. Wander themed rooms, rotate each piece, tap for the story of how deep time made it — and crack the geode yourself.

What makes it different: the museum is the product. Curated, story-first, indoors — beauty and narrative before anything else.

Day-1 PoC: one hall, 8–10 handcrafted specimens, two signature interactions (crack the geode, wake the volcano), story cards from a typed content file. Fully static.

Playable sketch — tap a specimen

Invented specimens and stories for the demo. Ages are approximate on purpose ("about 130 million years"), matching the app's honesty rule.

Direction 02

Field Log

Your pocket geologist. Photograph finds on a hike, identify them, and every walk adds to a personal collection that sits beside the museum pieces — each stone pinned to its chapter of deep time.

What makes it different: it starts outdoors. The museum is the destination; the mountain is the source. Your own finds are the collection.

Day-1 PoC: manual find entry (photo + note), rule-based identification of ~20 common Alpine rocks, a season shelf synced with the gallery. No ML required for the first version.

Direction 03

Deep Time Walk

The 4.6-billion-year ribbon becomes the main event. Scroll to stroll Earth's history; specimens are anchors along the path, and the switch from compressed to true scale is the aha moment.

What makes it different: the timescale is the product. A narrative walk first; the gallery hangs off it, not the other way round.

Day-1 PoC: one scrollable ribbon with six eras, five anchored specimens, and a linear/compressed scale toggle. One afternoon of Three.js-free, dependency-free code.

Playable sketch — scroll the ribbon, tap the pins, then switch to true scale

Invented specimen choices, real eras and approximate ages. In true-scale mode the Cenozoic — everything since the dinosaurs — is the thin last strip; all of human history fits in the final pixels.
