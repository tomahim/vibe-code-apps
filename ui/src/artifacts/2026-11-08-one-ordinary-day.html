Volume 01 uses English-language records from Britain and North America, 1660-1941. This is a limited collection, not a representative sample of the world or of social classes. Some diarists were privileged; some later became famous. The focus is ordinary activities.

An honest reconstruction

The record sets the boundary.

Each story is built around one named person and one dated diary entry. The story and moments are editorial paraphrases. Open “See the evidence” to find a short source excerpt and a direct link.

  • Documented means the cited entry says it. It does not mean the diarist was unbiased, complete or independently corroborated.
  • Not recorded marks a gap. We do not supply a plausible breakfast, bedtime, motive or conversation.
  • Only clock times explicitly present in the record are displayed as clock times. “After dinner” is not silently translated into 19:00.
  • Reading order follows the entry where possible. Events with no established place in that sequence say so.
  • The 3D desk, objects and light are an illustration. They are not evidence about possessions, rooms or weather.

These are excerpts and edited transcriptions. The source notes retain edition limits. Short quotations are for identification and commentary; the full texts remain with their source publishers.
