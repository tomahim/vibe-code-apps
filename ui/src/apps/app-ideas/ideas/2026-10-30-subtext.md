#### 1. CREATIVE CONCEPT & PROBLEM STATEMENT

- **Creative Twist / Re-imagining Angle:** Good dialogue is a lie two people agree to tell. Subtext makes the mechanism visible: you write a short scene in two columns. Left column: what is said ("Nice of you to finally come."). Right column: what is meant ("I waited forty minutes and I am not forgiving you today"). The exercise cuts both ways - write the surface first and excavate the intent, or write the intent first and find a surface line that hides it well. A contrast meter scores each pair on how far apart surface and intent sit, because the distance between them is where scenes live. Finished scenes play back as a staged reading with the subtext visible as annotations, the way a director's script looks in rehearsal week.
- **Core Problem & Target User:** Beginning writers produce dialogue where characters narrate their feelings, and the standard advice ("use subtext") is taught with famous examples but never practiced as a repeatable rep. Target user: fiction writers, screenwriters, tabletop game masters, and anyone who suspects their dialogue says exactly and only what it says.
- **Core Value Proposition:** The first daily practice that isolates the actual skill: two columns, one scene a day, and a visible measure of whether your characters are hiding anything.

#### 2. USER STORIES

- **Primary User Story:** "As a writer, I want to write each scene's dialogue and its hidden intent side by side, so that I practice making characters say less than they mean."
- **Secondary User Story:** "As a student of craft, I want to play my scenes back with the subtext annotated, so that I can feel where the gap between said and meant is working and where the dialogue goes flat."

#### 4. DAY 1 POC EXECUTION SPECIFICATION

- **Minimal Technical Setup:** React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS. Prompt deck of about twenty scene setups; the two-column editor (paired said/meant rows, add and reorder); the playback script view with a subtext toggle. localStorage only; no backend, no accounts. Desktop-first, mobile readable.
- **Core 24-Hour Demo Flow:** Open to a sample scene: two siblings clearing their late father's garage. Read it once with subtext hidden (polite small talk), then toggle the annotations and the same lines turn loaded. Then take today's prompt ("Two colleagues, one just resigned, the other knew first") and write four paired lines yourself. The "Aha!" moment: flipping the toggle on your own scene and hearing it change temperature.

#### 5. ADOPTION OPPORTUNITIES & DRIVERS

- **Instant Onboarding Hook:** The sample scene's toggle is a ten-second demonstration of the entire concept, and the first prompt produces a real scene in five minutes.
- **Growth Vector:** Same-prompt-different-scene comparisons are natural sharing material for writing groups, and the annotated script pages are built to be shown to a workshop.

#### 6. RISKS & MITIGATION

- **Craft risk:** A contrast score can be gamed and might push melodrama over nuance. Mitigation: the meter is a conversation starter, not a grade - it measures distance, not quality, and the copy says so; the reading view stays the real teacher.
- **Content risk:** Prompt cards carry the product; weak prompts produce flat scenes. Mitigation: a small curated deck at launch, expanded slowly, with user-written prompts deferred to Month 3.
- **Scope creep risk:** Full screenplay formatting, collaboration, AI critique. Mitigation: scenes cap at a page; nothing writes or judges the dialogue itself.
