#### 1. CREATIVE CONCEPT & PROBLEM STATEMENT

- **Creative Twist / Re-imagining Angle:** Newspapers bury their mistakes on page two in tiny print: "An article on Monday misstated..." Errata gives your own thinking the same treatment, with pride instead of shame. When you catch yourself wrong (a prediction that flopped, a technology you dismissed, a fact you repeated for years), you file the original claim and the correction. The app typesets it as a formal erratum in period style, assigns a severity class (Typo, Clarification, Correction, Full Retraction), and files it in your bound annual volume. A front-of-book "index of former beliefs" grows into the most honest document you own.
- **Core Problem & Target User:** Everyone is wrong constantly and nobody keeps score of themselves, so bad beliefs get quietly edited out of memory instead of examined. Writing them down feels like confession, so it never happens. Target user: curious people who follow news, tech and science, make constant small predictions, and want their calibration to actually improve.
- **Core Value Proposition:** Being wrong becomes collectible instead of embarrassing: a thirty-second ritual that turns each mistake into a beautiful artifact and, over a year, into a map of exactly where your judgment fails.

#### 2. USER STORIES

- **Primary User Story:** "As a curious person, I want to file a correction whenever I catch myself wrong, so that my mistakes become a record I learn from instead of memories I quietly lose."
- **Secondary User Story:** "As a reader of my own volume, I want to browse past errata by topic and severity, so that I can see my recurring blind spots and whether I am getting better calibrated."

#### 4. DAY 1 POC EXECUTION SPECIFICATION

- **Minimal Technical Setup:** React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS. One erratum template done in CSS (masthead, date line, two-column fine print). A small form (original claim, correction, severity) plus the bound-volume browser and the index. localStorage only; no backend, no accounts. Readable on mobile and desktop.
- **Core 24-Hour Demo Flow:** Open the app to a sample volume with three errata already filed ("Claimed RSS was dead, 2021. It was not."). Tap File an Erratum, write a claim you once made and what actually happened, pick Full Retraction, and watch it typeset itself into fine print. Save it, then open the index and see your own former beliefs listed like a table of contents. The "Aha!" moment (your mistake becoming a handsome printed correction) lands in under a minute.

#### 5. ADOPTION OPPORTUNITIES & DRIVERS

- **Instant Onboarding Hook:** The preloaded sample errata are funny and relatable; filing your first real one takes thirty seconds and immediately produces something worth showing a friend.
- **Growth Vector:** Shared errata cards are inherently social (self-deprecating honesty travels well), and the annual volume gives a natural year-end ritual worth returning for.

#### 6. RISKS & MITIGATION

- **Engagement risk:** Confessing wrongness has friction; the ritual dies if it feels like homework. Mitigation: capture stays under a minute, severity classes add playfulness, and the artifact (not the analysis) is the reward.
- **Tone risk:** The app could read as self-flagellation. Mitigation: copy throughout treats errata as a mark of seriousness, the way great newspapers treat their corrections page, with warmth and dry humor rather than guilt.
- **Scope creep risk:** Social feeds, public profiles, comment threads. Mitigation: the PoC is local and private; sharing is limited to single exported cards and deferred past Month 3.
