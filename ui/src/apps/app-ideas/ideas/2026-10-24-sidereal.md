#### 1. CREATIVE CONCEPT & PROBLEM STATEMENT

- **Creative Twist / Re-imagining Angle:** Star apps show you a perfect planetarium and make the real sky feel disappointing. Sidereal flips it: the planetarium is only the map; the product is YOUR observing life. Each session you log what you actually saw - naked-eye constellations from the balcony, the ISS pass, Saturn's rings in the borrowed binoculars - with the app's sky view used to confirm and name finds. Every log entry joins your personal sky history: a timeline of your own deepening sky, from "found Cassiopeia" in month one to "caught the Orion Nebula's core" in month eight. The app suggests tonight's realistic targets for your sky brightness, season and gear ("from your balcony, tonight: the Pleiades and Jupiter's moons"), and marks personal firsts like achievements that actually mean something because you earned them with your own eyes.
- **Core Problem & Target User:** Stargazing hooks people once (a dark sky, a bright planet) and then loses them: they don't know what to look for from home, can't tell what they found, and forget what they've seen. Target user: the balcony astronomer - curious beginners with city skies and maybe binoculars, not telescope owners with a hobby already formed.
- **Core Value Proposition:** A observing life that accumulates: realistic nightly targets for your actual sky, a log that turns glances into finds, and a personal sky history that makes you measurably more of an astronomer every month.

#### 2. USER STORIES

- **Primary User Story:** "As a beginner, I want three realistic things to look for tonight from my actual location and gear, so that every clear evening ends with something found."
- **Secondary User Story:** "As a returning observer, I want my log to grow into a personal sky history with firsts marked, so that I can feel my own progress and return to past finds."

#### 4. DAY 1 POC EXECUTION SPECIFICATION

- **Minimal Technical Setup:** React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS. A simplified but real sky model for the current season (bright planets, 30 showcase targets, Moon phase), location set manually, sky-brightness as a user setting; log in localStorage. No ephemeris backend in the PoC - targets computed from a compact built-in table.
- **Core 24-Hour Demo Flow:** Set "city balcony, binoculars" and get tonight's three: the Pleiades, Jupiter with its moons, and the ISS pass at 21:42. Tap "found it" on the Pleiades, add "hazy but the blue sisters showed", and the timeline stamps your first entry. **Aha moment:** the timeline after a simulated month - twelve entries, four firsts, and the visible arc from bright-and-obvious to genuinely-hard-won finds.

#### 5. ADOPTION OPPORTUNITIES & DRIVERS

- **Instant Onboarding Hook:** "What's above your roof tonight?" - location and gear in two taps, then three concrete targets with finder directions, no signup.
- **Growth Vector:** Sky events are natural surges (eclipses, comets, ISS flyovers, meteor showers) that the app surfaces as "tonight matters"; first-find cards are inherently shareable, and the year-in-review sky summary is a ritual.

#### 6. RISKS & MITIGATION

- **City reality:** Many users can't see much. Mitigation: target selection is honest about light pollution, binoculars unlock a realistic second tier, and the app celebrates achievable finds instead of promising nebulae a city sky won't show.
- **Ephemeris accuracy:** Wrong positions destroy trust instantly. Mitigation: PoC stays seasonal and approximate with visible "about" language; Expansion 1 uses a real ephemeris library and shows uncertainty honestly.
- **Weather dependency:** Cloudy weeks kill streaks and motivation. Mitigation: streaks count clear nights you checked, not consecutive days, and cloudy stretches surface learning content (constellation lore, how to read a star chart) so the app stays useful indoors.
- **Scope creep toward a planetarium:** The map is a means. Mitigation: keep the log and tonight list as the home surfaces; the sky view exists to name and confirm finds, not to compete with dedicated planetarium apps.
