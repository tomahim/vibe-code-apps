#### 1. CREATIVE CONCEPT & PROBLEM STATEMENT

- **Creative Twist / Re-imagining Angle:** Social deduction meets log forensics. A team of 4-8 players runs a small company for a week of game time: everyone performs normal job actions (pull reports, email files, run backups), and one hidden insider uses those same actions as cover to steal. Every action - honest or dirty - writes a line to the shared audit log, visible to everyone. The defenders' weapon is the log itself: at day's end the table reviews the trail ("why did Priya export the customer table at 7pm on a Friday?"), and the insider must keep their cover story consistent with a trail everyone can read. The insider wins by hitting their theft quota before the final vote; defenders win by naming the right person with log evidence that holds up. Rounds are short, accusation meetings are timed, and the log never lies - but it never explains itself either.
- **Core Problem & Target User:** Insider risk is the least understood security domain because it's about people and context, not malware; and social deduction games run on pure talk with no evidence layer. Target user: friend groups who love Among Us and Werewolf, and teams who want a genuinely fun way to build intuition about data access and audit trails.
- **Core Value Proposition:** A deduction game where accusations must be backed by a real, readable evidence trail - teaching data-access intuition through the best mechanic in party gaming.

#### 2. USER STORIES

- **Primary User Story:** "As a defender, I want to review a shared audit log of everyone's actions and build an evidence-backed accusation, so that catching the insider is about reasoning, not vibes."
- **Secondary User Story:** "As the insider, I want my thefts to hide inside plausible job actions, so that winning requires a cover story that survives the log."

#### 4. DAY 1 POC EXECUTION SPECIFICATION

- **Minimal Technical Setup:** React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS. Bots driven by scripted behavior trees with one insider strategy; the audit log is the single source of truth rendered from the action stream; all client-side, no accounts.
- **Core 24-Hour Demo Flow:** Day three review: you pin "exported customer table - 19:04" and "ran unusual backup - 19:11" against Priya's claimed schedule, accuse with the two lines cited, and the reveal confirms her trail beat by beat. **Aha moment:** realizing the log caught everything - the skill was reading it, and next game you read differently.

#### 5. ADOPTION OPPORTUNITIES & DRIVERS

- **Instant Onboarding Hook:** The first accusation meeting arrives fast with a log that visibly contains the answer - the table leans in immediately.
- **Growth Vector:** Social deduction spreads by word of mouth and group sessions; the security framing gives engineering and compliance teams a reason to bring it to work, and the "your table's detective score" recap begs to be shared.

#### 6. RISKS & MITIGATION

- **Teaching exfiltration craft:** The insider role involves hiding theft. Mitigation: actions stay at the level of plausible job moves (exports, shares, backups) without real evasion tradecraft, and the game's epilogue always frames how audit and least-privilege defeat these patterns.
- **Accusation without evidence ruins the genre:** Pure-vibe voting defeats the point. Mitigation: accusations must cite log lines, the UI makes citation one tap, and scoring rewards evidence quality over lucky guesses.
- **Multiplayer social friction:** Accusation games can sting. Mitigation: short timed meetings, a light corporate-satire tone, post-reveal focus on the trail rather than the player, and the solo bot mode as a low-pressure entry.
- **Bot insider too predictable solo:** Scripted theft patterns get solved. Mitigation: multiple insider strategies with randomized cover schedules, noise actions from honest bots, and difficulty via subtler signal rather than less evidence.
