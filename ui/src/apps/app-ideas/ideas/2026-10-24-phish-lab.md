#### 1. CREATIVE CONCEPT & PROBLEM STATEMENT

- **Creative Twist / Re-imagining Angle:** Security advice says "look for the signs" but never shows them on the email that is actually sitting in your inbox. Phish Lab is a dissection bench: paste a suspicious message (or forward it in), and the tool lays it out like a specimen - sender domain versus display name, lookalike characters and punycode, reply-to mismatches, link text versus real destination, urgency and authority language, attachment types - each finding pinned to the exact line it came from with a one-line explanation. The verdict is a calibrated scale ("several strong tells" to "no red flags found"), never a false binary, and every explanation links the pattern to how real campaigns use it. A practice mode flips it around: real-world phish and legitimate emails side by side, you mark the tells yourself, and the lab grades your eye.
- **Core Problem & Target User:** Phishing works because the tells are real but invisible to untrained readers, and corporate training is abstract and annual. Target user: anyone who has ever stared at an email wondering "is this actually from my bank?", plus IT-curious people and team leads who want a concrete artifact to share with family or colleagues.
- **Core Value Proposition:** Turns the email you are worried about right now into the training material, with evidence you can read, learn from, and forward.

#### 2. USER STORIES

- **Primary User Story:** "As a cautious recipient, I want to paste a doubtful email and see every suspicious element highlighted with the reason it matters, so that I can decide with evidence instead of anxiety."
- **Secondary User Story:** "As a learner, I want a practice mode that quizzes me on real phish versus legitimate mail, so that spotting tells becomes a reflex."

#### 4. DAY 1 POC EXECUTION SPECIFICATION

- **Minimal Technical Setup:** React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS. All detection is client-side rule-based analysis of pasted text (regex and string analysis over a seeded lookalike-domain and urgency-phrase list); no email leaves the browser, which is itself a privacy selling point. Practice mode ships with 10 annotated examples.
- **Core 24-Hour Demo Flow:** Paste a classic fake-bank email; watch three lines light up - the display name says "Bank" but the domain is a lookalike, the button text says one URL and the href is another, and "within 24 hours" urgency is flagged. The tally reads "4 strong tells, 2 weak tells". **Aha moment:** the user sees the exact line where the scam lives, quoted from their own paste.

#### 5. ADOPTION OPPORTUNITIES & DRIVERS

- **Instant Onboarding Hook:** The landing page runs the dissection live on one famous real phish, so the value is visible before the user pastes anything.
- **Growth Vector:** Everyone knows one person who "would fall for this" - the shareable annotated report is the natural forward, and families and team leads become the distribution channel.

#### 6. RISKS & MITIGATION

- **False authority:** A clean result could be read as "safe to click". Mitigation: the verdict is a tell tally with honest limits stated ("this checks visible content only; when in doubt, contact the sender through a known channel"), never a safe/unsafe stamp.
- **Handling real phishing content:** Pasted emails contain links to live malicious sites. Mitigation: all links are defanged in display (never rendered as clickable), analysis is local-only in the PoC, and expansion URL checks use a server-side fetcher, never the user's browser.
- **Rule decay:** Campaign tactics evolve faster than rules. Mitigation: the pattern library is content-managed and dated, each detector shows its last-updated date, and Expansion 2 tracks current campaigns from public sources.
- **Privacy of pasted mail:** Emails contain personal data. Mitigation: PoC processes everything client-side with an explicit "nothing leaves your browser" guarantee; any server feature is opt-in with clear retention terms.
