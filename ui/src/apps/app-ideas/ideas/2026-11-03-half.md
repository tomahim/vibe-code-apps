#### 1. CREATIVE CONCEPT & PROBLEM STATEMENT

- **Creative Twist / Re-imagining Angle:** Every editor knows the secret: almost any text survives losing half its words. Half turns that violence into a daily game with a hard numeric rule. Each day deals one passage (a bundled piece of competent-but-baggy prose, or your own pasted text): your only job is to cut it to fifty percent of its word count while keeping every load-bearing fact. The counter is merciless and live. Meet the target and the app offers the boss level: half again. A diff view then shows what you cut against what a reference editor cut, and the real lesson lands - you both deleted different things, and most of them did not matter. A "survivor profile" tracks which sentence types you keep sacrificing (throat-clearing openers, doubled adjectives, hedges) until sacrificing them stops being a drill and starts being your style.
- **Core Problem & Target User:** Everyone overwrites: developers' docs bury the command in three paragraphs, students pad to word counts, professionals mistake length for thoroughness. Cutting is a skill, and it has no practice format that feels like a game instead of a chore. Target user: anyone who writes for busy readers and suspects their first drafts are twice as long as their good ones.
- **Core Value Proposition:** The purest writing exercise there is, gamified by one number: hit fifty percent and keep the meaning, daily, until economy becomes your default register.

#### 2. USER STORIES

- **Primary User Story:** "As a writer, I want a daily passage to cut to half its length with a live counter, so that compression becomes a drilled reflex instead of a guilt trip."
- **Secondary User Story:** "As a learner, I want my cuts compared against a reference edit, so that I can see what a professional considered disposable and test my judgment against theirs."

#### 4. DAY 1 POC EXECUTION SPECIFICATION

- **Minimal Technical Setup:** React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS. A bundled deck of about twenty passages (150-300 words, various registers) each with a reference edit; the editing view with live word count and target; the diff comparison. localStorage only; no backend, no accounts. Desktop-first, mobile workable for short passages.
- **Core 24-Hour Demo Flow:** Today's passage: a 240-word project update email that says about 120 words' worth of things. Cut, watch the counter drop, hit 120 - the lock clicks. Open the other editor: they killed the whole second paragraph you lovingly trimmed, and kept one sentence you deleted. The "Aha!" moment: realizing the passage reads better at half, and so would yours.

#### 5. ADOPTION OPPORTUNITIES & DRIVERS

- **Instant Onboarding Hook:** The counter makes the game legible in five seconds, the first passage takes five minutes, and the diff against the reference edit is an immediate payoff.
- **Growth Vector:** Before-and-after pairs are natural sharing material, the head-to-head mode spreads it through teams and writing groups, and "your own text" mode makes it a daily utility rather than a quiz.

#### 6. RISKS & MITIGATION

- **Quality risk:** Blind cutting can butcher nuance; fifty percent is not always right. Mitigation: the reference edit models judgment (some passages resist the target, and the app says so), and the rule is framed as training weight, not gospel.
- **Content risk:** The passage deck carries the product. Mitigation: a curated launch deck across registers, your-own-text mode in Month 1 removes content dependence, and the deck grows slowly with quality over volume.
- **Tone risk:** Compression culture can sneer at style. Mitigation: the survivor profile celebrates what you chose to keep as much as what you cut, and rhythm and voice are named as things worth protecting.
