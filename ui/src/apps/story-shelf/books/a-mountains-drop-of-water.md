# A cloud above the peaks

Mira and her guide watched clouds gather above the mountains. In cold conditions, water could fall as snow and settle on the high ground. Mira imagined one snowflake joining a white blanket.

This is the beginning we choose for our drop's story. The water cycle has no single beginning. Water is always moving between places and changing form, on journeys much larger than one mountain.

# A warmer morning

When conditions grew warm enough, some snow melted into liquid water. A tiny trickle slipped between stones. Meltwater could join a stream, soak into the ground or take another path.

Mira did not need to touch the icy current to see its energy. She and her guide watched safely from the trail. The mountain was releasing some of the water it had stored as snow.

# Down through the valley

Gravity helped pull water downhill. Little flows joined larger ones, and the stream carried on through the valley. Some water rested in a lake. Some continued farther, towards rivers and eventually the sea.

Our imagined drop follows one possible route, not the route of every drop. Water can also travel underground, wait as ice, or be taken up by plants. The landscape is full of paths we cannot see.

> There is more than one way through the water cycle.
---page---
# An invisible traveller

Sunlight warmed the lake. Some liquid water changed into water vapour, an invisible gas, and entered the air. That change is called evaporation. The mist Mira could see was made of tiny liquid droplets, not invisible vapour.

Higher in the air, cooling water vapour could condense into tiny droplets or form ice crystals. Clouds are made of those droplets or crystals. The journey was changing shape again.

> A cloud is not a bag of invisible water vapour.

# No final page

From the shelter of a porch, Mira watched rain cross the valley. Water falling from clouds as rain or snow is called precipitation. Some would feed plants, some would flow away and some might rest as mountain snow.

Our drop has no final home. The Sun's energy and gravity help keep water moving. As Mira closed her notebook, the mountain's story continued: a river, a cloud, a snowflake, another beginning.
