# A valley of secrets

In Egypt lies a rocky valley where ancient kings were buried deep underground. By 1922, many people believed every tomb there had already been found. A British archaeologist named Howard Carter was not so sure.

Senu, a boy from a nearby village, carried water to Carter's digging team. For five long seasons they had searched the sand and found almost nothing. This season, Carter told them, would be their last chance.

> The valley is called the Valley of the Kings, near the city of Luxor.
---page---
# A step under the sand

On November 4, 1922, a worker brushing away sand shouted in surprise. Beneath some ancient huts, cut into the rock, was a single stone step. Then another. Then a whole staircase leading down into darkness.

Senu helped carry baskets of rubble as sixteen steps appeared. At the bottom stood a doorway, blocked with plaster and stamped with royal seals. Whatever lay behind it had not been touched for over three thousand years.

> Royal seals told ancient guards: this door must stay closed.
---page---
# Waiting for wonderful things

Carter covered the steps again and sent a telegram to Lord Carnarvon, the man paying for the dig. "At last have made wonderful discovery in the Valley," he wrote, "a magnificent tomb with seals intact."

Then came the hardest part: waiting. Carnarvon travelled all the way from England. Day after day, Senu guarded the quiet staircase and wondered. Would the tomb be empty, like so many others?

> In 1922, news and people travelled slowly. The journey from England took days.
---page---
# Wonderful things

On November 26, with Carnarvon beside him, Carter made a small hole in a second sealed door and held a candle to it. Golden light seemed to glow back. "Can you see anything?" asked Carnarvon. "Yes," whispered Carter, "wonderful things."

Senu could hardly believe the stories from inside: golden couches shaped like animals, statues, chariots, and boxes piled to the ceiling. Everywhere the explorers looked, something glittered.

> Carter really answered "wonderful things." His own diary says so.
---page---
# The boy king

The tomb belonged to Tutankhamun, a pharaoh who became king as a child and died at about nineteen. Hidden safely under rubble, his rooms held more than five thousand treasures. Recording and packing them took Carter about ten careful years.

Deep inside, in nested coffins, rested the king's famous gold mask, discovered when the coffins were opened three years later. Senu is invented, but the discovery is real. Newspapers of the time invented spooky curse tales; most of the team lived long, ordinary lives.

> True history, imagined helper. Why do you think this tomb stayed hidden so long?
