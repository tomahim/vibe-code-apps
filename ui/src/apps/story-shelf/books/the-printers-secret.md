# A door full of noise

Lina heard the workshop before she saw it. Clack, creak, swish! Inside, a printer was working beside a tall wooden press. On a table lay hundreds of tiny metal shapes. Each one carried a letter.

"One small piece can help make a whole book," the printer told her. Lina leaned closer. How could something so little hold such a big adventure?

# Letters with a second life

The printer placed the little letters together in rows. Lina noticed something strange: their faces looked backwards! When inked letters pressed against paper, the print would face the right way.

A page needed patient work. A mistaken letter had to be found and changed. But afterwards, the same pieces could be moved into new words. Lina imagined letters travelling from one story to another.

> Reusable letters are called movable type.

# The wooden giant

The printer spread ink over the type. Paper was placed carefully above it. Then the great screw press pushed the paper onto the letters. Lina waited while the printer lifted the sheet.

There they were: neat dark words, where a moment before there had been a blank page. It was not magic. It was a clever meeting of metal, ink, paper and pressure.

# More than one copy

Lina carried a dry sheet to the window. The printer could make another copy from the same arranged type, then another. Making a book still took skill, materials and time. Yet a page no longer had to be written by hand for every copy.

Across the workshop, Lina watched the little letters wait in their rows. Their work was not finished. They could carry the same words to many readers.

> What changes when more people can read the same page?

# A small beginning

As evening warmed the town, Lina pictured stories leaving the workshop and finding new homes. She had arrived looking for a secret. She left thinking about a question: what might people learn when words could travel farther?

Lina and her visit are invented. Johannes Gutenberg was real. Around 1450, his workshop in Mainz developed a European system of printing with reusable metal type. Printing already had a long history in Asia.
