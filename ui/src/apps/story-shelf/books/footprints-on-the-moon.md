# The whole world watching

On a July evening in 1969, Nora's family squeezed onto the sofa. The television murmured and flickered. Far above them, three astronauts were flying toward the Moon, and their landing was about to begin.

Nora was not alone. All around the world, about 650 million people watched the same grey pictures. In living rooms, schools and shop windows, everyone held their breath together.

# A rocket as tall as a tower

The adventure began on July 16, 1969, in Florida. The Saturn V rocket stood taller than most towers, filled with powerful fuel. When its engines roared to life, the ground itself seemed to shake.

Inside the small capsule at the very top sat Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin and Michael Collins. Their home for eight days was hardly bigger than a family car. The Moon lay four days of space travel ahead.

> Saturn V remains one of the most powerful rockets ever flown.

# Eagle lands gently

Arriving at the Moon, the astronauts climbed into a spindly landing craft named Eagle, leaving Michael Collins orbiting above in the main ship, Columbia. Down below waited the Sea of Tranquility, a plain of grey dust.

Alarms blinked and fuel ran low as Armstrong steered past a crater. Then, on July 20, came the words everyone waited for: "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."

> The Sea of Tranquility is a dark, dusty plain, not a real sea.

# One small step

Hours later, Nora watched a white-suited figure climb down the ladder. "That's one small step for a man, one giant leap for mankind," said Armstrong, pressing the very first footprint into the moon dust. Aldrin soon joined him.

For about two and a half hours, the astronauts planted a flag, set up experiments and collected moon rocks. High above, Collins circled alone, keeping their ride home ready.

> The footprints may last for millions of years, because the Moon has no wind to blow them away.
---page---
# Splashing safely home

On July 24, the astronauts' capsule fell through Earth's sky and splashed into the Pacific Ocean, where the ship USS Hornet waited to pick them up. They brought home moon rocks, photographs and a story like no other.

Nora and her family are invented, but the voyage is real. Apollo 11 carried the first people to walk on another world, and the footprints, flag and experiments they left behind are still there today.
