title: Optics, cables and reach

Moving bits costs energy that grows with distance. Inside a server, electrical traces move data centimeters. Across a rack, copper twinax cables reach a few meters. Beyond that, optics take over: a transceiver converts electrical signals to laser light, sends it through a hair-thin glass fiber, and converts back at the far end. Each conversion consumes watts, and a large cluster can contain tens of thousands of optical links.

The industry's response is co-packaged thinking: bring optics closer to the switch silicon to shorten the electrical path, and push copper as far as physics permits because it needs no optical conversion. SemiAnalysis highlights exactly this copper-first instinct in GB200-scale designs. For the facility, the lesson is that network architecture is also thermal and electrical architecture: thousands of transceivers are thousands of small heaters that the cooling system must serve and the power chain must feed, port after port, rack after rack.
