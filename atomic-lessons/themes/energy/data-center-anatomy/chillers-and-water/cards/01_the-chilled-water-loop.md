Large data centers move heat with water, not air, because water carries thousands of times more heat per unit volume. The architecture is a loop of loops. In the data hall, air handlers pass warm return air over coils of chilled water; the water, perhaps entering at 7 to 15 degrees Celsius, absorbs the heat and flows back to the plant room. There the chiller, a large refrigeration machine, extracts the heat from the chilled-water loop and pushes it into a second loop, the condenser loop.

The condenser loop carries the heat outdoors to its final destination: the atmosphere. Pumps, pipes, valves, heat exchangers and expansion tanks complete the picture, all redundant, all metered, all watched. When you hear that a data hall is water-cooled, it usually means this: air still cools the servers, but water does the heavy lifting of carrying the heat out of the building.
