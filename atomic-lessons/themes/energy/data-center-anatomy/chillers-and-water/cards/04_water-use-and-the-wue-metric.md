title: Water use and the WUE metric
archived: false
---


Just as PUE measures energy overhead, WUE, water usage effectiveness, measures water: annual site water consumption divided by IT energy, in liters per kilowatt-hour. Evaporative cooling can consume on the order of one to several liters per kWh of IT energy, which at campus scale competes with agriculture and cities for stressed watersheds.

The industry answer is deliberate trade-offs. Some operators run towers only in the hottest hours, accepting higher energy use most of the year to save water. Others choose dry coolers, essentially giant car radiators, which consume almost no water but more fan energy and cannot reach the lowest temperatures in hot climates. Still others site facilities in cool, water-rich regions, or reuse treated wastewater instead of potable supplies. There is no universal right answer: the cooling design is a negotiation among energy, water, climate and cost, which is why two equally modern data centers can look completely different.
