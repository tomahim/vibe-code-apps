title: Pumps, pipes and redundancy in the plant room

The cooling plant obeys the same reliability grammar as the electrical chain. Pumps move the loops and come in redundant sets; pipes form rings so a section can be isolated without stopping flow; valves, sensors and controllers watch temperatures, pressures and flow rates at every node. A chiller plant serving a Tier III facility is designed so any single component can be removed for service while the rest carry the load.

Two details deserve attention. First, water treatment: water systems, whether closed IT loops or open tower circuits, need treatment because they that corrode, scale and grow biology if neglected, so chemistry is monitored and adjusted continuously, because a scaled heat exchanger silently wastes megawatt-hours. Second, controls: the plant is a dynamic system balancing chiller staging, pump speeds, tower fans and economizer modes against weather and IT load, and its optimization is where much of the PUE battle is won or lost, minute by minute, by software. The plant room is a factory whose only product is cold, and it is run with factory discipline.
