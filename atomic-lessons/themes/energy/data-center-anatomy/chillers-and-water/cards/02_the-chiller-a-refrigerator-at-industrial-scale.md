title: 'The chiller: a refrigerator at industrial scale'

---


The chiller works on the same vapor-compression cycle as a home refrigerator, scaled to megawatts. A refrigerant is compressed, making it hot; it rejects that heat to the condenser water loop and condenses to liquid; it then expands through a valve, dropping in pressure and temperature; and it absorbs heat from the chilled-water loop as it evaporates, ready to be compressed again. The compressor is the heart and the main energy consumer.

Chillers are among the largest electrical loads in the facility, which is why their efficiency dominates the overhead budget. They are specified by capacity in refrigeration tons or megawatts and by efficiency at part load, because a plant rarely runs flat out. Like everything critical, they are deployed with redundancy, often with spare capacity, feeding a looped piping system so any chiller or pump can be isolated for maintenance while the plant keeps making cold. When operators talk about the mechanical plant, this machinery, plus its controls, is what they mean.
