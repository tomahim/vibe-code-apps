The cooling tower is where the data center's heat finally leaves the site. Warm condenser water is sprayed over fill material inside the tower while fans draw air through the spray. A small fraction of the water evaporates, and evaporation is powerfully endothermic: each liter that evaporates carries away roughly 2,400 kilojoules at typical tower-water temperatures of heat. The remaining water, now cooled, collects in the basin and returns to the chillers for another round.

The tower is why many data centers are recognizable by visible condensed-water plumes in suitable weather, and why they drink. Evaporated water must be replaced, and water that cycles repeatedly concentrates minerals, so some is continuously bled off. A large facility can evaporate millions of liters per day in hot weather. This consumption is the other half of the sustainability conversation, tracked by the WUE metric, and it drives the design trade-off of the next cards: towers are energy-cheap but water-hungry; dry coolers invert the bargain.
