title: 'Waste heat: from nuisance to product'

Once heat is captured in water loops, it becomes transportable and therefore usable. A growing number of facilities, especially in Europe, sell or donate their waste heat: warm water from the cooling loop feeds district heating networks, warming homes, swimming pools and greenhouses. Stockholm, Helsinki, Paris and Dublin all host such projects, and some regulators now expect heat-reuse plans with new data center permits.

The thermodynamics set the terms. Data center heat is low-grade, typically 25 to 40 degrees Celsius, so reuse usually needs heat pumps to lift it to district-heating temperatures, adding cost and complexity but delivering genuine energy reuse, a second life for watts already paid for. The vision closing the loop on this course's theme is compelling: a data center as an energy participant in both directions, buying power from the grid, selling heat to the city, and treating the byproduct of computation not as waste but as the local utility it could become.
