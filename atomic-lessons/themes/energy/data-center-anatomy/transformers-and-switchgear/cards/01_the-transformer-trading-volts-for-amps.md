A transformer is elegantly simple: two coils of wire wrapped around an iron core. Alternating current in the primary coil creates a changing magnetic field, and that field induces a voltage in the secondary coil. The ratio of the numbers of turns sets the ratio of voltages. More turns on the secondary steps voltage up; fewer steps it down. Power is conserved, minus a few percent of losses: what the secondary gains in voltage it gives up in current, and vice versa.

This device is why the grid runs on AC: only alternating current creates the changing magnetic field transformers need. In a data center, transformers appear at every boundary: stepping transmission voltage to medium voltage at the substation, and medium voltage to low voltage beside the data hall. They are big, heavy, mostly copper and iron, extremely reliable, and lately very hard to buy: lead times for large units have stretched dramatically under AI-driven demand.
