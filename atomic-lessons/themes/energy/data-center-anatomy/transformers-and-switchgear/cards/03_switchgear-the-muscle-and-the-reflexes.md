Switchgear is the collective name for the equipment that routes, protects and isolates electrical circuits: circuit breakers that trip in milliseconds when current surges, disconnect switches that isolate equipment so humans can work safely, contactors, relays and the busbars that tie them together. If transformers reshape power, switchgear decides where it may go and stops it when something goes wrong.

Its protection role is brutal and necessary. A short circuit can push current to tens of times normal in an instant, vaporizing metal and starting fires; breakers must detect and clear such faults faster than the damage spreads, and selectively, so the fault trips the smallest possible piece of the system. Medium-voltage switchgear sits at the substation and around the campus; low-voltage switchgear guards the final feeds. Every maintenance window and every redundancy scheme in this course ultimately relies on switchgear's ability to make one path safe while another keeps the servers alive.
