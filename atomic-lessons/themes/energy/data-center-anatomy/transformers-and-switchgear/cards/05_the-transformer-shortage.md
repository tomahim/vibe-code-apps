The AI build-out collided with an unglamorous fact: large power transformers are artisanal industrial products. They are built to order, wound by skilled hands, tested for weeks, and shipped on special transports. As data centers, grid upgrades and electrification all demanded them at once, lead times stretched from months to, in many cases, multiple years, and prices climbed.

This is the supply-chain side of the power story SemiAnalysis tracks across more than 200 datacenter suppliers, including electrical giants like Schneider Electric, Vertiv and Eaton. The transformer queue now shapes data center schedules the way chip supply once did: projects are sequenced around equipment deliveries, and operators place orders years before buildings rise. When you read that a campus will open in 2028, the honest translation is often: that is when its transformers arrive. The most advanced computing in the world waits on the oldest technology in the chain.
