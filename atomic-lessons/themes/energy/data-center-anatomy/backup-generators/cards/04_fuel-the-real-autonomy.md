Once running, a genset's limit is diesel. Facilities size on-site storage for extended autonomy, commonly 24 to 72 hours or more at full load, with day tanks near the engines fed from larger bulk tanks. The fuel system itself gets redundancy: multiple pumps, filters, and tank arrangements that let fuel move even when components fail.

Beyond the tanks lies logistics. Long outages turn refueling into an operation: contracts with fuel suppliers, guaranteed delivery commitments, and pre-planned tanker routes, all rehearsed before disaster season. Regions learn this the hard way after hurricanes and ice storms, when the data centers that kept running were the ones whose fuel plans survived closed roads and scarce diesel. The generator yard is therefore as much a supply-chain artifact as an engineering one: steel, fuel contracts and road access, all in service of a promise that the servers will outlast the storm.
