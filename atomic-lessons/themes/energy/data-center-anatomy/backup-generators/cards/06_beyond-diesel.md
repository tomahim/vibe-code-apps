Diesel dominates because it is proven, energy-dense and independently stored, but its emissions are increasingly unwelcome. Regulatory constraints make diesel gensets more expensive, as SemiAnalysis notes, since pollution-control equipment is required. The industry is exploring alternatives: HVO renewable diesel that drops into existing engines with far lower lifecycle emissions, natural gas generators with cleaner exhaust but pipeline dependence, and further out, hydrogen fuel cells and grid-scale batteries as ride-through and peak resources.

The realistic near-term picture is hybrid: diesel remains the emergency backbone while batteries grow from minutes toward hours and cleaner fuels erode diesel's share. Some operators now use their generator fleets and batteries to help the grid, running during grid peaks to reduce strain, which turns idle insurance assets into revenue. The last line of defense is slowly becoming part of the energy system it protects against, though nobody has yet retired the diesel yard.
