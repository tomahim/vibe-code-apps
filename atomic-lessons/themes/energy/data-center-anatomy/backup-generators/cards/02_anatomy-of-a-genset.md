title: Anatomy of a genset

A generator set, or genset, pairs a diesel engine with an alternator, the same physics as a power plant shrunk to container scale. The engine burns diesel to spin a shaft; the alternator converts rotation into AC electricity at the facility's low voltage. Around this core sit the supporting systems that make it dependable: starter batteries and motors, fuel pumps and day tanks, cooling radiators, exhaust treatment, and the controls that decide when to start and how to synchronize.

Data center gensets are built for a strange life: almost always idle, but required to perform perfectly on demand, starting in seconds after months of sleep. That duty cycle drives the maintenance culture: regular test runs under load, fuel polishing to keep stored diesel clean, block heaters so engines start willingly in winter, and inspections of every component that could balk at the critical moment. Reliability engineering here is mostly about fighting the entropy of equipment that waits.
