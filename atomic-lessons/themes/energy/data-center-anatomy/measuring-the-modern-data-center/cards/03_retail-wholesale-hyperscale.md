SemiAnalysis segments the industry by critical IT power. Retail colocation: facilities of at most a few megawatts, in cities, leasing racks to many small tenants whose real product is interconnection, cheap network links between everyone in the building. Wholesale: 10 to 30 MW facilities leasing rows and rooms, built in phases as demand signs. Hyperscale: 40 to 100 MW buildings in campuses of hundreds of megawatts, self-built by the cloud giants or built-to-suit by developers.

Ownership adds a second axis: colocation rents capacity priced per kW per month, while self-build operators construct for themselves. Hyperscalers do both, leasing where they lack scale or speed, building where they are giants. The segmentation explains the market's shape: a retail facility competes on location and network density, a wholesale facility on scalability and cost, a hyperscale campus on power procurement and efficiency. Same physics from lesson 1, three different businesses built on top.
