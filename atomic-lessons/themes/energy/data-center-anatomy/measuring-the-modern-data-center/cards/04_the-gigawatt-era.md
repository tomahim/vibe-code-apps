The AI build-out has shifted the industry's unit of ambition from megawatts to gigawatts. SemiAnalysis calculates that a cluster of about 20,000 H100 GPUs needs roughly 26 MW of critical IT power; the industry is now building 100,000-GPU clusters and planning gigawatt-scale multi-datacenter training efforts. The IEA projects global data center electricity use roughly doubling to around 945 TWh by 2030, with accelerated servers, the AI fleet, driving nearly half the increase.

At this scale, the constraints reorder. Chips were once the scarce input; now it is power: grid connections queued years out, transformers on multi-year lead times, campuses sited by transmission access before fiber or land price. The macro implications SemiAnalysis opens the series with, national security, economic growth, energy policy, all follow from the arithmetic of lesson 1: computation is electricity, and demand for computation is compounding. The data center has become energy infrastructure as much as information infrastructure.
