The Uptime Institute's Tier classification rates a facility's ability to keep running, and SemiAnalysis uses it as the frame for redundancy decisions. Tier I is a single power and cooling path with no redundancy. Tier II adds some redundant components. Tier III is concurrently maintainable: any component or distribution path can be serviced without interrupting IT, which is a performance outcome that requires checking the entire topology, not merely counting spare devices. Tier IV is fault tolerant: the facility survives any single failure without impact, with fault containment and independent paths. The standard specifies outcomes, not a fixed cost multiplier.

Uptime Institute removed expected-downtime predictions from the Tier Standard in 2009. A Tier is a topology classification, not a promise of 99.995% uptime or any fixed number of annual outage minutes. Operations, maintenance and human behavior also determine availability. A cloud service-level agreement is a different contract: its percentage, measurement window, exclusions and compensation rules apply to the service, often across several buildings. Do not infer an SLA from a building's Tier, or a Tier from a marketing claim about nines.
