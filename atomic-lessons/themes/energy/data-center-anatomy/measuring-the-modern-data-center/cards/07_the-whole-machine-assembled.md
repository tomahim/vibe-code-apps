Let us assemble it one final time. A transistor switches, billions of them per chip, turning watts into heat. Chips package into servers; servers fill racks on A and B feeds; racks join rows and fabrics of networking and storage. Meanwhile the electrical chain descends: transmission grid to substation, medium voltage to transformers, transfer switch to UPS and batteries, generators behind them, busways and whips down to dual power supplies and the 1 V regulator on the silicon. And the cooling chain rises to meet it: air through contained aisles, heat into water loops, chillers or economizers, towers evaporating into the sky, cold plates where air can no longer reach.

You now have an introductory model assembled from the accessible SemiAnalysis sections and other public sources, not a substitute for the full paid series. A data center is two rivers flowing in opposite directions: electricity flowing down to the transistor, heat flowing up to the atmosphere, engineered at every crossing for efficiency and unbroken flow. The gigawatt campuses rising today are these same rivers, wider and faster, but unchanged in kind. From one volt to one gigawatt: that is the anatomy.
