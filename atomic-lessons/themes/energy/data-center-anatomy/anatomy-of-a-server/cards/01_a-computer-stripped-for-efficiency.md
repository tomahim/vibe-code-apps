A server is a computer redesigned for a life spent in a rack: no screen, no keyboard, no speakers, just compute, memory, storage and networking packed into a flat metal chassis, managed remotely over the network. Where a desktop optimizes for the person in front of it, a server optimizes for density, reliability and serviceability by a technician who may handle hundreds of identical machines a day.

Servers come in standardized heights measured in rack units (U), where 1U is 44.45 mm. A typical general-purpose server is 1U or 2U tall; dense GPU servers run 2U to 8U because of their cooling and power needs. The chassis slides into the rack on rails, connects to power at the back and network at the front or back, and may support hot-swappable fans, drives and power supplies. The model and redundancy determine what can actually be replaced live.
