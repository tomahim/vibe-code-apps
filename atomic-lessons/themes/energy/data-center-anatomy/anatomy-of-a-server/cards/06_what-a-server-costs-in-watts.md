title: What a server costs in watts

A conventional two-socket server draws 300 to 800 W under load. A storage server, mostly drives, draws a few hundred watts. An AI server with eight H100 GPUs approaches 10 kW, and a GB200-class compute tray draws more per chassis still. These numbers decide how many servers a rack can hold: with 10 kW available, you can fill a rack with conventional servers; with the same 10 kW, a single AI server consumes the entire rack's budget.

This arithmetic is why power, not space, is the scarce resource in modern facilities. A data hall may have empty rack positions it cannot use because the electrical and cooling capacity is fully subscribed. Operators therefore plan in watts first and square meters second. When SemiAnalysis prices colocation per kW per month rather than per square meter, it is acknowledging that a server slot is really a slice of the electrical and cooling plant. The server is where the facility's megawatts finally meet the work they were built to do.
