Why are servers shaped like pizza boxes? Because the rack demands it. Standardizing on 19-inch-wide, 1U-multiple chassis lets operators mix vendors in the same rack, slide machines in and out without tools, and push air through them front to back in a predictable direction. That airflow direction is a design contract: servers inhale cold air at the front and exhale hot air at the back, which is what makes the hot aisle and cold aisle layouts of lesson 12 possible.

Cable placement, handle positions, indicator lights and latch colors are all standardized for the same reason: a technician working at speed in a noisy hall should never have to guess. Hyperscalers take this further with custom designs. Meta's Open Compute Project servers, for example, snap onto a DC busbar at the back of the rack instead of carrying their own AC-DC rectifiers, removing one conversion step per machine. The server chassis you see is a century of industrial standardization applied to computing.
