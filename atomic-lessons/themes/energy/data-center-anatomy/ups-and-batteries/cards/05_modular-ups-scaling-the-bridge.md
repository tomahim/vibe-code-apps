Early UPS systems were single monolithic boxes sized for the whole facility: expensive, hard to maintain live, and sized for load that might take years to arrive. Modern systems are modular. SemiAnalysis describes Vertiv's architecture: power cores of 200 or 400 kVA each, stacked up to ten per unit, with up to eight units in parallel, reaching a maximum around 27 MW per system.

Modularity fixes three problems at once. Capacity grows with the load, so capital follows customers instead of preceding them. A failed core is bypassed and hot-swapped while the rest carry on, turning maintenance into a non-event. And efficiency improves because cores can sleep when lightly loaded, keeping the active ones near their efficient operating point. For scale, the comparison SemiAnalysis offers is striking: a single 200 kVA core can output about as much AC power as the inverter of a Tesla Model 3. The data center UPS is a fleet of car-sized power electronics, orchestrated as one.
