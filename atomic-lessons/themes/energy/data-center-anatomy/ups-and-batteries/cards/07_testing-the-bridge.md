A UPS is only real if it has carried the load lately. Batteries degrade silently: cells lose capacity, internal resistance climbs, and a bank that looked healthy on paper can collapse in thirty seconds under real load. Facilities therefore test constantly, with monitoring that tracks every battery string and periodic discharge tests that prove the autonomy figure is still true.

Maintenance is where the static bypass earns its keep. To service a UPS, operators switch the load to bypass power, work on the de-energized unit, and switch back, all without the servers noticing. During that window the facility runs without its bridge, which is why such work follows strict procedures and why the 2N design matters so much: the other chain still protects the load while this one is open. The uninterruptible promise is not a device but a discipline of monitoring, testing and redundant paths, renewed every week of the facility's life.
