A campus drawing 300 MW cannot be served by the neighborhood distribution network; it needs a direct relationship with the transmission grid. SemiAnalysis explains that hyperscale sites are placed near high-voltage transmission lines, at 138 kV, 230 kV or 345 kV, because those lines can deliver far more power than medium-voltage distribution, and regulators sometimes cap how much power may be drawn from a given voltage level.

This shapes the map of the industry. Site selection starts with power: available capacity on nearby lines, the utility's willingness and timeline to connect, the price of electricity and the reliability of the local grid. If no adequate substation exists, the operator builds one or funds the utility to build it, a project measured in years and tens of millions of dollars. In today's AI build-out, the queue for grid connections and the lead time for large electrical equipment have become the binding constraints on how fast compute can grow.
