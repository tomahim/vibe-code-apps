How big a grid connection does a data center need? Start from critical IT power, the maximum draw of the IT equipment, then add the overhead: cooling, lighting, losses and auxiliary systems, and distinguish maximum design demand from expected energy use. Expected IT draw is reduced, not increased, by lower utilization; capacity planning must still consider peaks, growth and redundancy. SemiAnalysis notes typical power utilization of 50-60% for cloud workloads and above 80% for AI training, which cuts both ways: AI campuses use almost all the capacity they buy, while enterprise sites often run far below theirs.

The connection itself is then engineered with margin and redundancy, often with two feeds from the grid where geography allows. Capacity is contracted from the utility years ahead, which is why headlines track gigawatts of future commitments. A connection agreement specifies capacity and conditions, while proposed or announced capacity may still depend on upgrades and approvals. A headline alone does not prove the load is energized. In the AI era those promises have become some of the most contested pieces of infrastructure planning on Earth.
