A hyperscale campus typically owns an on-site substation: an industrial yard of transformers, switches, circuit breakers and busbars, often behind its own fence within the fence. Its job is to receive high-voltage power and step it down to the medium voltage, commonly 11 to 33 kV, at which power circulates around the site to the individual buildings and data halls.

The substation is where the operator stops being a customer and starts being an electricity company. Equipment here is utility-grade: oil-filled transformers the size of small houses, protection systems that isolate faults in milliseconds, redundancy so a single failure leaves the site running. SemiAnalysis shows Google and Microsoft campuses with their own substations as a matter of course. This yard of steel is also a large slice of the electrical budget and, lately, of the schedule: large power transformers have become some of the longest-lead items in the entire build.
