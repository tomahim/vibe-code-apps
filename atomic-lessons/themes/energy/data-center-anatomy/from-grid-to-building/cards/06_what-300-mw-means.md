Let us make a campus number tangible. SemiAnalysis's comparison: an average US home draws about 1.2 kW on average, so a 300 MW campus consumes like roughly 200,000 homes, a mid-sized city. At 8,760 hours a year and high utilization, that campus consumes well over a terawatt-hour annually, comparable to the output of a dedicated power plant.

For the grid, such a customer is both opportunity and strain: a huge, steady source of revenue, and a load that can arrive faster than generation and transmission can be built. This is why data center siting now involves energy strategy, including long-term power purchase agreements, interest in nuclear and renewables, and sometimes on-site generation. It is also why the efficiency lessons later in this course matter beyond one company's bill: at campus scale, a few percentage points of wasted energy equals the consumption of thousands of households.
