title: The power profile of storage
archived: false
---

Storage rarely dominates a facility's power budget, but it shapes it. An HDD draws 5 to 10 W spinning, an SSD a few watts, so a dense storage rack draws 3 to 8 kW, far below a compute rack. Multiply by the hundreds of racks a large facility dedicates to storage and the total becomes significant, and nearly constant: storage does not idle the way compute does, because data must stay readable around the clock.

This steadiness makes storage the baseline hum of the data hall's electrical plan. It also creates an unusual optimization lever: unlike compute, storage capacity can be managed in energy terms by consolidating cold data onto fewer, denser, slower media and powering down what is unneeded. As AI clusters consume more megawatts for training, operators look harder at the steady background load, and cold-tier discipline becomes part of the facility's energy story. The micro world is now complete: chips, servers, networks and storage. The next lesson starts the power chain that feeds them all.
