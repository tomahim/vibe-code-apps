Here is the cooling problem stated in one line: essentially every watt delivered to IT equipment becomes heat in the data hall. A 10 MW IT load is a 10 MW heater, the thermal equivalent of thousands of fan heaters running nonstop. There is no exhaust pipe for computation, no product leaving the building but data; energy enters as electricity and must leave as heat.

This reframes cooling as the mirror of the power chain. The same redundancy, capacity planning and cost discipline apply, just in thermal units. It also explains SemiAnalysis's observation about blast radius: cooling failures are rarer than electrical faults but more disruptive when they happen, because heat builds everywhere at once but cooling continuity can be protected with UPS-backed auxiliaries, stored thermal energy and redundant paths. A data hall losing cooling at full AI load can cross safe temperatures within minutes. The cooling chain is the power chain's counterpart, and the next three lessons dissect it.
