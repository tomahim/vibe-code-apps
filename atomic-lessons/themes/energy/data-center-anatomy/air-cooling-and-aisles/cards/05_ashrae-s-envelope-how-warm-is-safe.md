How cold must a data hall be? Less cold than tradition assumed. ASHRAE, the industry body whose thermal guidelines govern the field, recommends server inlet temperatures of 18 to 27 degrees Celsius, and defines wider allowable envelopes for modern equipment classes. Early data centers ran at refrigerator temperatures out of caution; the guidelines exist because research showed modern servers tolerate, and are warranted at, much warmer conditions.

Every degree matters economically. Raising the supply air setpoint reduces refrigeration energy directly and, more importantly, expands the hours per year when the facility can cool itself without mechanical refrigeration at all, using outside air or water-side economization, the free cooling of lesson 13. The trust placed in those guidelines is total: hyperscalers run halls in the mid-20s and let hot aisles exceed 40 degrees. The human experience of a modern data hall, a warm wind tunnel under fluorescent light, is the physical signature of the industry learning exactly how much cooling it does not need.
