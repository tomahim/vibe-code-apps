The workhorses of traditional cooling are the computer room air conditioner (CRAC) and its cousin the computer room air handler (CRAH). A CRAC uses a direct-expansion refrigerant coil: it pulls hot air from the room across a cold refrigerant coil and pushes cooled air back out. A CRAH skips the local refrigeration; its coil carries chilled water made elsewhere by a central plant, and it simply moves air across that cold coil.

Air can be delivered under a raised floor, through ducts or directly into the room, pressurizing a supply plenum so cold air can be delivered evenly to the aisles that need it. The choice between CRAC and CRAH is the choice between distributed refrigeration and a central chilled-water plant. CRAC and CRAH describe heat-exchange approaches, not strict building-size categories. Site climate, water access, capacity and lifecycle cost determine whether direct expansion, chilled water or another design makes sense. Lesson 13 follows that chilled water to its source.
