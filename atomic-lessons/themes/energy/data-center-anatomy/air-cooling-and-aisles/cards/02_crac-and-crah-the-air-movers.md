title: 'CRAC and CRAH: the air movers'

The workhorses of traditional cooling are the computer room air conditioner (CRAC) and its cousin the computer room air handler (CRAH). A CRAC is a self-contained refrigeration unit: it pulls hot air from the room across a cold refrigerant coil and pushes cooled air back out. A CRAH skips the local refrigeration; its coil carries chilled water made elsewhere by a central plant, and it simply moves air across that cold coil.

Both deliver cold air to the same place: under a raised floor or into overhead ducts, pressurizing a supply plenum so cold air can be delivered evenly to the aisles that need it. The choice between CRAC and CRAH is the choice between distributed refrigeration and a central chilled-water plant. Small and legacy sites run CRACs; large modern facilities run CRAHs fed by chillers, because central plants are more efficient at scale and concentrate the refrigeration machinery where it is easier to maintain.
