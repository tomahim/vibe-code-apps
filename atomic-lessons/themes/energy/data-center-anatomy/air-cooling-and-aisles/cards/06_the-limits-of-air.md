title: The limits of air
archived: false

Air is a mediocre heat carrier. Per unit of volume it moves little heat, so removing kilowatts means moving storm-force volumes of it, and fans to move that air themselves consume meaningful power, often several percent of the IT load. At low rack densities, this is fine. As density climbs, the airflow required per rack grows until the physics breaks: you cannot push a hurricane through a server without the fans consuming the energy you are trying to save.

The industry has watched the ceiling approach for years. Conventional wisdom puts practical air cooling limits around 15 to 25 kW per rack, stretching toward 30-40 kW with containment, rear-door heat exchangers and heroic engineering. AI racks at 60, 100, 130 kW simply exceed what air can carry. SemiAnalysis's Part 2, the cooling half of the Datacenter Anatomy series, opens from exactly this premise. When air fails, the answer is to bring a better heat carrier to the chip: liquid, the subject of lesson 14, and the reason the newest data halls are plumbed like refineries.
