Servers enforce the airflow contract from lesson 3: cold air in the front, hot air out the back. Arrange rows so fronts face fronts and you create alternating aisle types: cold aisles, enclosed by rack fronts, fed with chilled air; hot aisles, enclosed by rack backs, collecting exhaust. This widely used layout keeps supply and exhaust from mixing before either has done its job.

Without the discipline, air short-circuits: hot exhaust leaks around and over racks back into intakes, servers ingest pre-heated air, and operators respond by overcooling the whole room, wasting energy and still finding hot spots. The aisle layout is the first fix, free and architectural. Details reinforce it: blanking panels in empty slots, floor tiles placed only in cold aisles, cable cutouts sealed. The physics is simple, cold in front, hot out back, but the discipline is daily, and the energy at stake is enormous.
