title: 'Containment: building walls for air'

The natural upgrade from organized aisles is enclosed ones. Containment puts physical barriers, roofs and doors, around either the cold aisle or the hot aisle to reduce mixing between supply and exhaust. Cold-aisle containment delivers chilled air into a closed room shared only by server intakes; hot-aisle containment captures exhaust and ducts it straight back to the cooling units.

The payoff is efficiency with a multiplier. With mixing reduced, supply temperatures can rise several degrees while servers stay just as cool, and every degree of warmer supply air cuts refrigeration work. Return air arrives at the cooling units hotter, which improves heat-exchanger performance. Containment is why modern halls look like greenhouses or industrial kitchens: glass or plastic roofs over aisles, sliding doors at the ends, and a deliberate absence of the chaotic open rooms of the early internet era. The air in a contained facility is not ambient; it is engineered, aisle by aisle.
