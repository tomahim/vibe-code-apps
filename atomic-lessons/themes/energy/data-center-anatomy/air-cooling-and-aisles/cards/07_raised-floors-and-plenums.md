title: Raised floors and plenums
archived: false
---

The classic data hall delivers its cold air from below: a raised floor of removable tiles stands a half-meter or more above the concrete slab, and the void beneath is the supply plenum, pressurized with chilled air by the CRAC or CRAH units at the room's edge. Perforated tiles placed in the cold aisles let the pressurized air rise exactly where servers inhale.

The plenum doubles as a cable highway in older designs, though modern practice pushes cabling overhead to keep airflow unobstructed: every cable tray in the plenum is a dam the cold air must flow around. Many new halls abandon the raised floor entirely, delivering air through overhead ducts into contained aisles and reclaiming the slab. The pattern to notice is the same everywhere in this course: an architecture born in the mainframe era, optimized over decades, then partially re-invented when density and efficiency demanded it. Even the floor is negotiable; only the thermodynamics is not.
