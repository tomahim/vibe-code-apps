A common server rack uses a 19-inch mounting standard, not a 19-inch external cabinet width with mounting holes spaced in rack units, typically 42U tall, about two meters. It is one widely used standard, not the only one: Open Compute designs also use wider equipment spaces. Standardization is the point: compatible 1U equipment can share mounting conventions. Rail kits, rack depth, weight and cooling compatibility still need checking.

A rack is not furniture; it is infrastructure. Bolted to the floor, grounded, cable-managed and rated for a tonne or more of equipment, it provides the mechanical, electrical and network connection points for everything it holds. When capacity planners talk about a facility, they count racks and kilowatts per rack. The rack is the smallest unit of the data center that has its own power budget, its own network connection and its own place in the cooling design.
