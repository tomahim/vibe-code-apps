An NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 is not a rack full of servers; it is one computer the size of a rack. Its 72 GPUs and 36 Grace CPUs sit in compute trays linked by a rack-wide NVLink fabric of copper cables, so software can coordinate them within one high-bandwidth scale-up domain. They remain 72 distinct GPUs. The rack ships as an engineered system with its own power shelves, busbars and liquid-cooling manifolds, because such high power density requires matching electrical, structural and cooling design.

This changes the relationship between IT and facilities. A traditional rack was an empty frame the customer filled; the AI rack arrives as a pre-integrated product with hard requirements: direct-to-chip liquid cooling, high-capacity power feeds, reinforced floors and factory-specified networking. SemiAnalysis frames the Blackwell generation as the moment the industry standardized on these requirements. The rack, once a commodity enclosure, became the productized unit of AI compute, and facilities now design around the rack rather than the other way around.
