title: The rack as the unit of sale

In retail colocation, customers lease racks: a quarter rack, a half rack, a full rack, each with a committed power allocation in kW. In wholesale colocation, they lease rows or rooms measured in hundreds of kW or in MW. In both cases the rack count and density define the commercial product. A customer buying 5 kW per rack reserves less power than a 10 kW customer, even when both occupy the same physical space.

This is why density planning is business planning. Build a hall for 5 kW racks and AI customers cannot use it; build for 50 kW and the electrical plant costs more per rack whether or not customers use it. Operators therefore design in blocks that can be fitted out later, installing transformers and busway as demand signs up. The humble 19-inch frame turns out to be the atom of a global real-estate market: racks times kilowatts times months is literally the revenue formula of colocation.
