The single most important number attached to a rack is its power density in kilowatts. Legacy enterprise racks drew 3 to 5 kW. Modern designs commonly plan 10 to 15 kW, and Uptime Institute's 2025 survey found most reported maximum rack densities in the 10 to 30 kW range; this is not the average of every rack. High-performance computing pushed select rows to 30 to 50 kW. Then AI arrived: NVIDIA's GB200 NVL72 rack draws around 120 kW.

Density drives everything. It sets how thick the power feeds must be, how much air or liquid must pass through the rack, and how strong the floor must be. It also sets the value of the rack: at typical colocation prices, a 20 kW rack earns far more per year than a 5 kW one. Rising density is the story of the industry's last decade, and managing its consequences, electrically and thermally, is the story of the next.
