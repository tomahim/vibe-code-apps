title: 'Rows: racks find their neighbors'

Racks are deployed side by side in rows, and rows are arranged facing each other so that server intakes share a cold aisle and exhausts share a hot aisle. SemiAnalysis describes a typical data hall as rooms with rows of about 26 racks. The row is the practical unit of deployment: network cabling runs along the row, busway tap-offs drop power to each rack in it, and containment systems enclose whole aisles at once.

Rows also structure resilience work. Power whips, busway sections and network aggregation switches are mapped to rows so that maintenance on one distribution branch affects a known, bounded set of racks. When operators plan capacity, they think in rows of a given density: a row of twelve 20 kW racks is a 240 kW block of demand the electrical and cooling chains must serve. The row is where the rack count meets the megawatt count.
