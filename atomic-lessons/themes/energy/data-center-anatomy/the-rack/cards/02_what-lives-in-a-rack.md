title: What lives in a rack
archived: false
---

A conventional rack holds a stack of 1U or 2U servers, one or two network switches near the top, and two vertical power distribution units (PDUs), narrow strips of outlets running down the rear corners. The switches, called top-of-rack (ToR) switches, aggregate every server's network cables into a few high-speed uplinks to the facility's spine. The vertical PDUs distribute power from the two facility feeds, A side and B side, to each server's dual power supplies.

Weight and wiring matter as much as electronics. A full rack of servers can exceed a tonne, and its rear carries dozens of power cords and network cables that must not block the hot air leaving the machines. Good rack hygiene, with cables dressed to the sides and blanking panels filling empty slots, keeps cold air from short-circuiting through gaps. In the data center, tidiness is an energy-efficiency measure, not an aesthetic one.
