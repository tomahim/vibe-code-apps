The classic distribution chain uses power distribution units (PDUs): cabinet-sized assemblies that take a large low-voltage feed and break it into many protected branch circuits, each with its own breaker and metering. In legacy designs, PDUs feed remote power panels (RPPs) closer to the racks, which split circuits further into the flexible cables that finally reach each cabinet.

Think of it as a river delta: one large feed dividing into dozens of manageable channels, each protected so a fault in one rack cannot trip its neighbors. The breakers are the selective-protection principle of lesson 8 applied at room scale. Metering matters as much as protection: modern PDUs report current per circuit, letting operators balance phases, spot creeping overloads, and bill colocation customers for the capacity they actually commit. The PDU is where the facility's megawatts become individually accountable kilowatts.
