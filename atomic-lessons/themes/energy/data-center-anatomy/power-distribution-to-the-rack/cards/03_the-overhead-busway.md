The modern alternative to cable deltas is the overhead busway: a rigid metal housing containing solid copper bars, suspended above the rows, carrying hundreds of amps. SemiAnalysis describes the layout: a main room busway feeds smaller row busways, and tap-off units plug into the busway above each rack, dropping flexible cables called whips down to the rack's power strips.

Busway wins on flexibility and cleanliness. Adding or moving a rack means plugging in a new tap-off box, not pulling new cable runs through underfloor trays; the copper is overhead where air and technicians can move freely; and every tap can carry its own metering. Hyperscalers adopted busway broadly years ago, precisely because their halls reconfigure constantly. Redundancy follows the same pattern as everything else: busways come in pairs, one for the A side and one for the B side, each fed from its own UPS chain.
