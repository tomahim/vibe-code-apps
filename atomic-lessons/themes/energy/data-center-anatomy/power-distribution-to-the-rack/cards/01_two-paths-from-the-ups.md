The building has at least two important kinds of electrical load: IT equipment that computes, stores and moves data, and mechanical equipment that removes the resulting heat. This is an accounting split, not a universal wiring diagram. In one common arrangement, transfer equipment supplies both branches, while the UPS protects the IT branch. Some pumps, fans, controls or liquid-cooling equipment also need uninterrupted power, depending on the thermal design.

That distinction matters when reading a one-line drawing. Never assume all cooling is downstream of the IT UPS, or that every mechanical load can safely stop until generators start. Engineers assess restart times, stored thermal capacity and the rate at which chips heat up. At a PUE of 1.5, IT uses two-thirds of the facility energy and all non-IT overhead together uses one-third. Cooling is only part of that overhead, alongside electrical losses, lighting and other services.
