The final meters are humble but critical. A whip, a flexible armored cable, runs from the tap-off box or PDU circuit into the rack, where it lands on a vertical rack PDU: a strip of outlets down the rear corner, often smart, measuring power per outlet. Servers plug in with two cords, one to the A-side strip, one to the B-side strip, completing the dual-corded pattern from lesson 3.

This last hop is where theory meets a technician's hands. Cords must reach without strain, phases must balance across circuits, and the strip's rating must cover the rack's growth, because a rack provisioned for 5 kW and later loaded to 20 kW has a way of discovering its wiring's limits at the worst moment. In AI halls the last hop is industrial: GB200-class racks take high-current feeds that look more like factory machinery than IT kit. The journey that began at a distant power plant ends here, in a plug, behind a server, in the dark behind the cable arms.
