Everything in a data center traces back to the transistor. The electrical chain exists to deliver clean, uninterrupted power to chips. The cooling chain exists because those chips turn that power into heat. The racks, the rows, the substations and the chillers are all scaled from one number: how many watts the silicon consumes.

This is why AI reshaped the industry so quickly. When the unit of compute went from a 300 W CPU to a 700 W GPU to a 1.2 kW Grace-Blackwell module, every downstream system had to grow with it. SemiAnalysis reports that Meta demolished a data center building mid-construction because its older low-density design could not host the new hardware. Keep the transistor's bargain in mind, performance paid for in watts, and every design choice in the coming lessons will read as a rational response to it.
