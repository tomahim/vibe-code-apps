title: 'Heat density: the real challenge'

It is tempting to think of chip power as an electrician's problem, but the harder half is thermal. Power density is heat flux: watts per square centimeter. A modern GPU die dissipates heat at a flux comparable to a kitchen hotplate concentrated into a coin. Let that heat accumulate and the silicon throttles itself to survive, wasting the very power you paid to deliver; push further and the chip degrades or dies.

So the thermal chain matters as much as the electrical chain. Heat moves from the transistor junction to the chip package, to a heat sink or cold plate, to air or liquid, and eventually to the atmosphere outside the building. Every interface in that chain adds resistance, and the total resistance sets how hot the silicon runs for a given power. Chip designers and facility engineers are solving the same problem from opposite ends: one generates heat ever more densely, the other must carry it away ever more efficiently.
