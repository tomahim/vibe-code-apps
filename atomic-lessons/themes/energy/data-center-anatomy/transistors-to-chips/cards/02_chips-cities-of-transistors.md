A chip, or integrated circuit, is a single piece of silicon patterned with billions of transistors wired together. The processor in a laptop, the memory in a phone and the GPU training an AI model are all chips, differing in what their transistors are arranged to do. A CPU is built for versatile, sequential logic; a GPU arranges thousands of simpler arithmetic units side by side to do the same operation on huge batches of data at once.

Chips do not work alone. They are mounted on a package that connects their microscopic wires to the outside world, then soldered onto boards with memory, storage and networking chips nearby. In modern AI systems the package itself has become a miniature system: NVIDIA's GB200 Superchip combines a Grace CPU with two Blackwell GPUs through a high-speed link. Each GPU package includes high-bandwidth memory; this is an interconnected module, not all CPU and GPU dies on one common substrate. The design choices trade performance, memory bandwidth, energy and packaging complexity; newer does not always mean higher draw for the same task.
