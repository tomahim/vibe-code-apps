title: 'Chips: cities of transistors'

A chip, or integrated circuit, is a single piece of silicon patterned with billions of transistors wired together. The processor in a laptop, the memory in a phone and the GPU training an AI model are all chips, differing in what their transistors are arranged to do. A CPU is built for versatile, sequential logic; a GPU arranges thousands of simpler arithmetic units side by side to do the same operation on huge batches of data at once.

Chips do not work alone. They are mounted on a package that connects their microscopic wires to the outside world, then soldered onto boards with memory, storage and networking chips nearby. In modern AI systems the package itself has become a miniature system: NVIDIA's Grace-Blackwell modules combine CPU and GPU dies with stacks of high-bandwidth memory on one substrate. The trend is constant: more transistors, packed closer, fed with more power, in every generation.
