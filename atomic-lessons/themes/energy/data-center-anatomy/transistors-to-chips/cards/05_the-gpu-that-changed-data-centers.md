NVIDIA's H100, launched in 2022, became the workhorse of the generative AI boom. In its server form it draws 700 W, and servers typically carry eight of them, so a single AI server approaches 10 kW. SemiAnalysis calculates that a cluster of 20,840 H100s requires about 26 MW of critical IT power capacity once networking, storage, CPUs and overhead are included.

Its successor family, Blackwell, pushed further. The GB200 NVL72 system packs 72 Blackwell GPUs and 36 Grace CPUs into a single liquid-cooled rack drawing around 120 kW, and SemiAnalysis notes densities up to 130 kW with direct-to-chip liquid cooling as a requirement, not an option. For comparison, a traditional enterprise rack draws 5 to 15 kW. In five years the power density of the industry's flagship rack rose by an order of magnitude. That single fact forced the redesign of power distribution and cooling that the rest of this course follows.
