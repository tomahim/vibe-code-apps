title: Leak fears and how the industry answers

The first objection every engineer raises to liquid cooling is the oldest fear in computing: water near electronics. The industry's answers are layered. The IT loop runs at low pressure, often below the pressure of the surrounding air in negative-pressure designs, so a breach sucks air in rather than spraying coolant out. Quick-disconnect fittings are dry-break, sealing both sides as they separate. Leak detection cables snake through racks and under manifolds, alarming on the first drop.

Coolant choice adds another layer: the IT loop carries treated water or engineered dielectric fluids, never the building's raw supply, and the CDU keeps the two worlds apart. The record so far supports the engineering: supercomputing centers have run liquid-cooled machines for decades, and the GB200 generation is industrializing the same practices for everyone. The fear is rational and the mitigation is systematic, which is exactly how this industry treats every risk from fire to grid failure: not by denying it, but by designing it down to a managed nonevent.
