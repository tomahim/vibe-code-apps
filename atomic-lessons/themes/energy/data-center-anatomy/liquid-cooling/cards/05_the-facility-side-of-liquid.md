title: The facility side of liquid

Liquid cooling does not remove the cooling plant; it transforms it. The facility water loop now begins at the CDUs instead of the air handlers, and because liquid captures heat efficiently at the chip, the loop can run warm, 30 degrees Celsius and above, where traditional chilled water runs cold. That single change cascades beautifully: warm loops may allow more hours without compressor work, depending on weather and the specified coolant temperature, so dry coolers or towers can serve them directly, and some designs can reduce or avoid chillers for that load. Others still need them at peak ambient temperature.

Warm water also raises the value of the waste heat: 40-degree return water is far more useful to a district-heating heat pump than 25-degree air-handler runoff. And liquid cooling enables the densities themselves: halls designed for liquid can host several times the compute per square meter, which is the economic heart of the matter. Liquid cooling is not merely a thermal technology; it is what makes the 120 kW rack, and therefore the economics of modern AI infrastructure, physically possible.
