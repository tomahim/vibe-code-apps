title: 'Immersion: servers that swim'

Immersion cooling puts compatible IT equipment in a dielectric fluid rather than surrounding it with air. In single-phase immersion, the fluid remains liquid and transfers heat to a heat exchanger. In two-phase immersion, the working fluid boils at the hot components, and its vapor condenses back to liquid at a cooled surface. That phase change can move heat without a pumped primary circulation loop, though the overall system still needs heat rejection and controls.

The trade-offs are different from cold plates. Equipment compatibility, fluid handling, seals, material aging, fire safety and service procedures all matter. Technicians may need lifting equipment and a drain-down process before working on a server. Immersion can remove most server-fan demand, but it is not inherently silent or free to operate: outdoor coolers, pumps and auxiliary systems may remain. It is an alternative architecture, not an inevitable next step after every direct-to-chip design.
