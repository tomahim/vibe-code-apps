The more radical approach removes air from the equation almost entirely. Immersion cooling submerges whole servers in tanks of dielectric fluid, a liquid that does not conduct electricity. Single-phase immersion keeps the fluid liquid, circulating it to external heat exchangers; two-phase immersion uses fluids engineered to boil at low temperatures, so heat leaves the chips as vapor bubbles that condense on coils at the tank's top, an elegant passive cycle with no pumps for the primary loop.

Immersion's promises are spectacular heat capture, near-total elimination of server fans, and silence. Its costs are equally real: tanks instead of racks, cranes to lift servers out dripping, fluid that costs as much as the hardware it bathes, and an ecosystem of warranties and servicing still maturing. SemiAnalysis flags immersion as a technology its future reports explore, the frontier beyond direct-to-chip. Today, direct-to-chip owns the mainstream AI build-out; immersion waits for the densities that even cold plates cannot serve.
