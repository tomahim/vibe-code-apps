Between pure air and full liquid lies a bridge technology: the rear-door heat exchanger, a radiator with fans mounted on the back of a standard rack. Server exhaust passes through the water-filled door, which captures most of the heat before it ever enters the room, letting existing air-cooled halls host 30 to 50 kW racks without rebuilding the room.

Rear doors illustrate a general truth about this transition: most of the world's data centers exist already, and their owners need liquid's benefits without gutting their buildings. The retrofit path runs from rear-door exchangers, to direct-to-chip on new high-density rows with the rest of the hall still on air, to purpose-built liquid halls. SemiAnalysis's warning to the industry is precisely about this stragglers' risk: facilities unable to deliver high-density liquid cooling will miss the performance and cost improvements of the GB200 generation and be left behind in the AI arms race. Plumbing has become a competitive moat.
