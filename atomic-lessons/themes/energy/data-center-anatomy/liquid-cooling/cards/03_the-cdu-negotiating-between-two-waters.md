Between the rack and the building sits the coolant distribution unit (CDU), the device that makes liquid cooling safe and practical at scale. The CDU is a heat exchanger and pumping station: it circulates a clean, treated coolant through the IT racks on one side, and exchanges that heat with the facility water loop on the other, keeping the two fluids separate.

Separation solves three problems. The IT loop stays clean and chemically controlled, whatever the building water contains. The CDU maintains the IT loop's pressure and temperature precisely, keeping supply above the dew point so condensation never forms on cold pipes inside live racks. And it localizes risk: valves, leak detection and pressure control can limit a technology-loop leak. The heat exchanger alone does not guarantee containment. A rack-scale CDU might serve a handful of high-density racks; row-scale units serve many. The CDU is the customs post of liquid cooling: everything crosses its border, under its rules.
