The metric prefixes do the heavy lifting at data center scale: a kilowatt (kW) is a thousand watts, a megawatt (MW) a million, a gigawatt (GW) a billion. A server draws hundreds of watts, a rack 5 to 130 kW, a data hall a few MW, a hyperscale building 40 to 100 MW and a campus hundreds of MW. The industry now discusses gigawatt-scale AI clusters, the power of a large nuclear reactor dedicated to computing.

These figures drive the business model. Colocation operators lease capacity priced per kW of critical IT power per month, because space is cheap compared to the electrical and cooling plant behind each kW. SemiAnalysis notes a cluster of about 20,000 NVIDIA H100 GPUs needs roughly 26 MW of critical IT power, and 100,000-GPU clusters are being built. When you read that a company secured a gigawatt of capacity, you now know exactly what was bought: the ability to deliver power, every second, to compute.
