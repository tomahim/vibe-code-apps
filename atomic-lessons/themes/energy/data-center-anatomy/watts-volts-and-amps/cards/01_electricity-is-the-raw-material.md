A data center is, at its core, a machine for turning electricity into computation. Every video streamed, every AI model trained and every photo stored is paid for in watts. SemiAnalysis defines a data center as a purpose-built facility that delivers power to IT equipment efficiently, safely and at the lowest possible total cost over the hardware's life.

The scale is hard to grasp without a comparison. An average American home draws about 1.2 kW on average. A single rack of servers can draw 10 kW or more, and the newest AI racks exceed 100 kW. A hyperscale campus can reach 300 MW, which we can compare to about 250,000 homes at an assumed 1.2 kW average load. This is an order-of-magnitude analogy, not a local household statistic. To understand how such a facility works, we start with the three quantities that describe electricity itself: voltage, current and power.
