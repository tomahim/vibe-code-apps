title: Power versus energy
archived: false
---

Power and energy are related but different. Power (watts) is a rate, like speed. Energy (watt-hours) is an amount, like distance. A 1 kW device running for one hour consumes 1 kWh of energy, and your home electricity bill charges for kWh, not kW.

Data centers live in both units at once. Their capacity is quoted in power, megawatts of critical IT load, because that is what the electrical equipment must deliver every second. Their consumption is quoted in energy, terawatt-hours per year, because that is what the grid must produce and what the operator pays for. The IEA estimates data centers consumed about 415 TWh in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity, and projects around 945 TWh by 2030 in its base case. One terawatt-hour is a billion kilowatt-hours. Keeping power and energy straight is the difference between understanding a capacity figure and an electricity bill.
