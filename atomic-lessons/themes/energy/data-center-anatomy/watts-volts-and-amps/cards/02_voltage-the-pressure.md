Voltage, measured in volts (V), is the electrical pressure that pushes charge through a circuit. The usual analogy is water in a pipe: voltage is the pressure in the pipe. A phone charger delivers 5 to 20 V, a European wall outlet 230 V, and the transmission lines crossing the countryside carry hundreds of thousands of volts.

Data centers care about voltage at every scale. The utility may deliver power at 138 kV or more, the building distributes it at medium voltage such as 11 kV or 33 kV, the data hall steps it down to a few hundred volts, and a processor core runs on roughly 1 V. Each step down exists because high voltage moves power efficiently over distance, while low voltage is safe and usable near equipment. Managing that descent, from transmission line to transistor, is a large part of what a data center's electrical system does.
