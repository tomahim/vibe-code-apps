Gretzky was not the biggest or the fastest skater, so he won with anticipation. He liked to start plays from behind the opponent's net, a spot nicknamed his office, where he could see every player on the ice and decide whether to pass or to open space for a teammate.

He also perfected a trick at the blue line. He would race up the boards, then brake so teammates could catch up and take position. Harry Sinden said this made the late man into the zone the most dangerous man. His father had drilled him in the saucer pass, a flip that skims over a stick or skate and lands flat on a teammate's blade. Those assists are why his total is the highest in NHL history.
