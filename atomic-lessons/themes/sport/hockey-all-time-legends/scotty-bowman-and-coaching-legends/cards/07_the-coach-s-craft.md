Britannica says Bowman became known as much for his mind games as for his line changes, often switching players' positions and threatening trades to motivate them. He was controlling and uncompromising and often drew the ire of players and fans, but his methods produced results.

His career, Blake's dynasty and Tikhonov's Soviet teams point to a few common ingredients: strong teams, the ability to adapt, and a clear plan for how to win. Bowman's 1,244 wins remain the NHL record.

Sources: [Britannica Kids, Scotty Bowman](https://kids.britannica.com/scholars/article/Scotty-Bowman/476833), [NHL Records, Scotty Bowman](https://records.nhl.com/coaches/87), [NHL.com on Toe Blake](https://www.nhl.com/news/this-date-in-nhl-history-june-8-280871318), [Canada's Sports Hall of Fame, Toe Blake](https://halloffamers.sportshall.ca/?hector_toe_blake=&language=EN) [NYT on Bowman's 1991 Hall of Fame election](https://www.nytimes.com/1991/03/28/sports/sports-people-pro-hockey-two-named-to-hall.html) and [BBC Sport, Viktor Tikhonov](https://www.bbc.com/sport/ice-hockey/30176309). These traits matter for any coach, at any level of the game, and they explain why all three men are in the Hall of Fame.
