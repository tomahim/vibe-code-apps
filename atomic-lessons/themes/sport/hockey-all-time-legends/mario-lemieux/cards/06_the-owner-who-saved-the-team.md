The Penguins fell into bankruptcy in the late 1990s. Lemieux, who was owed deferred salary, converted that debt into a large share of the team and became its owner, rescuing the club. NHL.com credits him with saving the franchise twice: first as the player who led them to two Cups, and then as the owner who kept them going.

The Hockey Hall of Fame waived its normal waiting period and inducted him in 1997. The Penguins raised a number 66 banner to the rafters. Yet on December 27, 2000, after 44 months away, Lemieux skated onto the ice in Pittsburgh against the Toronto Maple Leafs as both owner and player.
