In the 1940s Richard played right wing with Elmer Lach at centre and Toe Blake on left wing. The trio was called the Punch Line. Richard shot left-handed from the right side, and he was described as hockey's most dangerous player from the blue line in.

His scoring style was fierce. Writer Trent Frayne said Richard scored goals from all angles, sometimes while lying on his back with defenders holding his stick and pulling his sweater. Goalie Glenn Hall remembered his eyes as flashing like a pinball machine when he came flying toward the net. Richard could carry a checker, or two, on his back right to the crease.
