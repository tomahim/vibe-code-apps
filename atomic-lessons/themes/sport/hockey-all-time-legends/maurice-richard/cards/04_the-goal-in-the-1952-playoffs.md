One of the most famous goals in NHL history came in 1952. In Game 7 of the semifinal against Boston, Richard had to leave the game with a concussion. He came back out of the locker room later and scored the winning goal against the Bruins.

Stories like this made fans see him as more than a scorer. He was intense, and rivals knew that he could be provoked. A magazine writer in 1955 noted that opposing coaches targeted him for years, because a player like him can lose control after a night of being tripped, slashed and insulted. Yet the same fire drove his comebacks. He was named to the First or Second All-Star Team 14 times.
