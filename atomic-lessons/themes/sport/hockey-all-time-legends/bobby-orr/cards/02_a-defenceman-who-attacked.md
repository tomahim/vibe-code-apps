title: "A defenceman who attacked"

Before Orr, defencemen were mostly big, slow players who cleared opponents from the front of the net and passed the puck up the ice. Orr played at a staggering speed instead, darting and spinning from behind his own net to the opponent's goal. Don Cherry, who coached him in his last two Boston seasons, compared seeing him to a horse trainer seeing Secretariat.

Orr was not a one-way player. He also returned quickly to break up rushes, and he played a rugged game: in his rookie season he had 102 penalty minutes in 61 games, and in 1969-70 he became the first player to pass 100 points while also being assessed 100 penalty minutes. The Norris Trophy winner Doug Harvey had controlled a game's pace, but Orr did it at a far higher speed.
