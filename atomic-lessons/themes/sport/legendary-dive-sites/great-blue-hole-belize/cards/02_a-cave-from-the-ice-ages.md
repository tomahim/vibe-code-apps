The Blue Hole began as a cave on dry land. It formed during several phases of the Quaternary glaciation, when so much water was locked up in ice that sea level was far lower than today. Rain slowly dissolved the limestone, as it does in any karst landscape, and carved out a large cavity.

Stalactites found inside the hole tell the story of changing sea levels. Analysis shows that they grew in several phases, first in air when sea level was low and later underwater. A stalactite can only grow in air, so the oldest growth marks a period when this space was a dry cave. When the ice melted and the ocean rose again, the cave flooded, and the roof collapsed to leave the open circle we see now.
