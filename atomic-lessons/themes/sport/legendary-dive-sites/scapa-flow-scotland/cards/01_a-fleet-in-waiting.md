When the First World War ended with the Armistice of 11 November 1918, the Imperial German Navy's High Seas Fleet was interned at Scapa Flow, a sheltered natural harbour in the Orkney Islands of Scotland. The ships were held there while negotiations took place in Paris over what should happen to them. In total, 74 German ships were interned, including battleships, cruisers and destroyers.

Command of the interned ships was held by Rear-Admiral Ludwig von Reuter. The ships were only lightly guarded by the British, and the crews were gradually reduced as men were sent home. Under the Armistice the Germans were forbidden to destroy their ships, so the fleet sat in the Flow, with its future undecided, for more than half a year.
