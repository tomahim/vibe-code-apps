As the plates pull apart, tension builds in the crust between them. That tension is released through major earthquakes at intervals of roughly ten years, which cause cracks and fissures to form in the Þingvellir valley. The valley itself is a rift valley, a strip of ground that sinks as the plates separate.

The earthquakes of 1789 opened several fissures in the Þingvellir area, and one of them cut into an underground spring. Boulders and rocks falling into the widening cracks formed caves within the fissures, which is why the dive site includes tunnels and chambers as well as an open crack. Silfra is not a lasting, finished feature: the plates keep moving, and the shape of the fissure keeps changing.
