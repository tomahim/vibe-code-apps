Þingvellir is a UNESCO World Heritage area, and it is also the site of the Althing, Iceland's first parliamentary assembly. The first two-week meeting took place in June 930 CE, and this date is often taken as the birth of the Icelandic state. The assembly met here until 1798.

So a dive at Silfra connects three stories: the geology of two plates moving apart, the hydrology of a glacier's meltwater, and the political history of a nation that met in the open air on the rift valley floor. Visitors on land can walk between the two plates, and the park authorities explain Silfra as one of its main nature attractions.

Learn more: [Thingvellir National Park - Silfra](https://www.thingvellir.is/en/education/nature/silfra/)
