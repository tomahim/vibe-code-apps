The Yucatán Peninsula is a huge slab of limestone. When rain falls on this porous rock, it does not collect in rivers on the surface. Instead it drains through the stone like water through a sponge. Over millions of years, slightly acidic water dissolved channels in the limestone, creating a maze of tunnels, rooms and underground rivers.

This kind of landscape is called karst. Rainwater picks up carbon dioxide from the air and soil, which forms weak carbonic acid, and the acid slowly eats the rock. The same process is seen in Florida's springs. The result in Mexico is a region with almost no surface rivers but a vast network of water below the jungle.
