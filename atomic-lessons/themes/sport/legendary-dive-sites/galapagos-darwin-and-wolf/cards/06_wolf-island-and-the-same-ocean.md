Wolf Island, another remote island in the north, is also known for schools of scalloped hammerhead sharks, and it belongs to the same reserve. Wolf Island sits in the far north of the archipelago, not far from Darwin. Both islands are part of the Galápagos Marine Reserve, so the rules that protect them apply across a very large area of ocean. Neither is a place for casual visits.

The richness of the area is linked to the meeting of ocean currents described earlier. As with Blue Corner and Sipadan, a steep underwater slope in moving water is a good candidate for a gathering place for wildlife, although scientists continue to study exactly why animals choose particular spots.
