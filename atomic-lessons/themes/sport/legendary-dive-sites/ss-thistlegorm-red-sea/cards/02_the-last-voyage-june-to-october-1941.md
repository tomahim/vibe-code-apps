The Thistlegorm left Glasgow on 2 June 1941, heading for Alexandria in Egypt. Because of German and Italian activity in the Mediterranean, her convoy travelled the long way round, via Cape Town in South Africa, and then north up the east coast of Africa into the Red Sea.

Her cargo was supplies for the Allied forces in Egypt: lorries, motorcycles, Universal Carrier armoured vehicles, rifles and ammunition, radio equipment, aircraft parts and even Wellington boots. On deck she carried two steam locomotives intended for Egyptian railways. A collision in the Suez Canal stopped the convoy from reaching Alexandria, so the ship waited at anchor at a spot called Safe Anchorage F, in the Red Sea, through September and into October.
