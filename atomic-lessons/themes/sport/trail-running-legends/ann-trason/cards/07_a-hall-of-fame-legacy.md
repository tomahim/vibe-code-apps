In 2020 Ann Trason was inducted into the American Ultrarunning Hall of Fame. By then many people considered her the most successful female ultrarunner of all time. Her record at Western States, her 20 world records and her 14 wins make a strong case, and Ultrarunning Magazine named her Ultrarunner of the Year 12 times.

Her story also shows how an athlete can succeed after setbacks. She missed finishes early in her Western States career, and after she stopped racing in 2004 she found out that she had been running for years with an undiagnosed torn ACL. She returned to ultrarunning in 2013, at age 53, to enjoy running in the mountains rather than to race for wins.

Younger women who later set Western States records, including Greenwood and Dauwalter, raced on a course that Trason had already made famous.

Source: [Ultrarunning History, Ann Trason](https://ultrarunninghistory.com/hall-of-fame/hall-of-fame-ann-trason).
