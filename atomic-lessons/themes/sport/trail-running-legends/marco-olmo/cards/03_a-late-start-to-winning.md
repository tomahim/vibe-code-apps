By 1996 Olmo was almost 48 years old, an age when most runners have stopped racing. He began to win major ultra distance events, and kept doing so for years.

This is very unusual. In many sports, performance peaks in the twenties or early thirties. Ultra running often rewards experience and consistency more than raw speed, and Olmo showed how far those qualities can go. His story encouraged many older runners to take up trail running.

Ultra running rewards patience, pacing and mental strength, which often keep improving with age. Many top ultra runners are in their thirties, and some are older, but few have won major titles at Olmo's age.
