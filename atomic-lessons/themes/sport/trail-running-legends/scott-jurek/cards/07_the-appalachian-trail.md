In 2015 Jurek began an attempt to break the supported speed record for the Appalachian Trail. The old record was 46 days, 11 hours and 20 minutes for the route of about 2,168 miles. On July 13, 2015, he finished and beat the record by about three hours.

The finish at Katahdin created controversy, when rangers at Baxter State Park cited his party for being too large, for alcohol and for littering. Two charges were dismissed and Jurek paid 500 dollars for the alcohol. In 2014 he had also become, with Rickey Gates, one of the first North Americans to run the Bob Graham Round in under 24 hours.

A supported record means the runner has a crew who can provide food, water and logistics along the way. Even so, covering the Appalachian Trail's roughly 2,200 miles in under 47 days means running about 47 miles every day.

Source: [Ultrarunning History, Scott Jurek](https://ultrarunninghistory.com/hall-of-fame/scott-jurek/).
