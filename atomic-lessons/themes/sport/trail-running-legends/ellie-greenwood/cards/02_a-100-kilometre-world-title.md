In May 2010, at her first attempt at the distance, Greenwood won the Elk Beaver 100k on Vancouver Island in a course record of 7:36, which was a 100K trail world record. That earned her a place on the British team at the 2010 IAU 100 km World Championships.

There she battled with the race leader, 2006 champion Lizzy Hawker, and took gold in 7:29:05. Britain also won the team event and the jointly held European Championships. In 2010 she also set course records at the Canadian Death Race and the Knee Knackering North Shore Trail Run.

A 100 kilometre world title is decided on a flat road course, so it measures pure endurance and pace. Winning one in 2010 and again in 2014, after also winning a hilly 100K on a trail, shows that she was strong on both surfaces.
