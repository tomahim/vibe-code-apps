LeMond changed the equipment of professional cycling as much as its geography. His 1989 time trial made aerodynamic handlebars standard kit overnight, and he won his Tours of 1989 and 1990 on carbon fibre frames, then a novelty, which he later marketed through his own LeMond Bicycles company.

He was also one of the earliest and loudest voices against doping. When the EPO era transformed the peloton in the 1990s, he said publicly that performances no longer made physiological sense, and he kept saying it for years, at real cost to his businesses and friendships. Time proved him largely right, and it added a second legacy to his palmarès: the champion who warned the sport about itself.
