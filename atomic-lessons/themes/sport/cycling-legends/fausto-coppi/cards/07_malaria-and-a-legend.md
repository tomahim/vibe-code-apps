title: Malaria and an immortal legend

In December 1959 Coppi travelled to Burkina Faso, then Upper Volta, for an exhibition race, and caught malaria. The illness was misdiagnosed back in Italy and treatment came too late. He died on 2 January 1960, aged forty, and Italy mourned him like a head of state.

His legacy is everywhere in Italian cycling. The highest point of each Giro d'Italia is called the Cima Coppi in his honour, his home town renamed itself Castellania Coppi, and his duel with Bartali still frames how Italians talk about rivalry. More than sixty years after his death, many historians and fans still consider him the greatest Italian cyclist of all time.
