title: A first Giro at twenty, then the hour

Coppi won the Giro d'Italia in 1940 at just twenty years old, announcing himself as Italy's next great champion in his very first attempt. Two years later, in November 1942, he took on the hour record at the Vigorelli velodrome in Milan and covered 45.798 kilometres, a mark that would stand for fourteen years.

Both achievements came under the shadow of war, which would soon stop his career entirely. The hour record in particular made his reputation as a complete athlete: it demands sustained power, aerodynamic discipline and mental endurance, with no rivals to draft and no tactics to hide behind. When the war interrupted racing, Coppi was already the man expected to rule whenever the sport returned.
