In 1949 Coppi achieved what no rider had managed before: he won the Giro d'Italia and the Tour de France in the same season. His Tour was a masterpiece, sealed with a legendary stage over the Izoard and the Vars where he rode away alone, and he finished in Paris as the first Giro-Tour double winner in history.

He repeated the double in 1952, confirming it was no accident. Those two seasons define the Coppi legend: an elegant rider who won the hardest stage races with time to spare and seemed to float uphill while rivals ground their gears. When journalists of the time ranked cyclists, they often stopped trying to compare and simply called him the champion of champions.
