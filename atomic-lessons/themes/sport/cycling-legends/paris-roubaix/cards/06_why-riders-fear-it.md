title: Why riders fear it
archived: false
---
Many great riders have openly hated Paris-Roubaix. Bernard Hinault won it in 1981 and still called it absurd, arguing that the cobbles destroyed bikes and bodies. He had a point: every edition leaves a trail of broken collar bones, shattered wheels and ruined seasons.

And yet they keep coming back, because the reward matches the risk. The old saying holds that Roubaix is not won by the strongest rider but by the strongest survivor. No other race on the calendar combines speed, violence and beauty quite like it, which is why a film about the 1976 edition is simply called A Sunday in Hell.
