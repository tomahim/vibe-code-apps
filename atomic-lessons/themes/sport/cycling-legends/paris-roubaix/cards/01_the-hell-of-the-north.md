Paris-Roubaix is the oldest of the cobbled Monuments, first run in 1896. Its famous name, the Hell of the North, came later: after the First World War, organisers toured the devastated route through northern France and found landscapes so ruined that a journalist's phrase - l'Enfer du Nord - stuck forever.

The race is also called the Queen of the Classics and la Pascale, the Easter race, from its traditional April date. From 1896 to 1967 it really did start in Paris; since 1977 it has set off from Compiègne, north-east of the capital. It has been run every year except during the World Wars and the 2020 pandemic, and it remains the one race almost every professional both fears and dreams of winning.
