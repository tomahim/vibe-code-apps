The Giro's theatre is the mountains, and its cathedrals are the Alpine and Dolomite passes: the Passo dello Stelvio, the Passo Pordoi, the Passo di Gavia, and brutal late additions like the Mortirolo and the Monte Zoncolan. Since 1965 the highest point of each Giro has carried a special name: the Cima Coppi, in honour of Fausto Coppi.

The Stelvio, at 2,757 metres, is the most famous of all, with its walls of hairpins above Trafoi and Bormio. The 1988 Giro gave the mountains their most mythologised day: a snowstorm on the Gavia, riders climbing in a blizzard, and Andy Hampsten emerging through the white to take the pink jersey and, eventually, the race. The Giro without suffering in the high mountains would not be the Giro.
