The modern Giro keeps producing collapses and ambushes that the Tour rarely matches. In 2018 Chris Froome, minutes down, attacked 80 kilometres from the finish on the gravel of the Colle delle Finestre and rode alone over the Cima Coppi to take the race. In 2024 Tadej Pogačar won on his debut by nearly ten minutes, one of the biggest margins of the modern era.

Why is the Giro like this? Partly the terrain, with steeper and rougher climbs; partly the weather, snow and rain in May; partly a culture that rewards audacity over control. Grand Tour racing's most chaotic three weeks happen in Italy, and fans would not want it any other way.
