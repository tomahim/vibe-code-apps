title: Coppi, Bartali and the golden age

The Giro's golden age belonged to two men. Gino Bartali won it three times, and Fausto Coppi five times, a record he still shares. Their rivalry, the devout champion of the countryside against the modern star, divided Italian families and filled the Gazzetta's pink pages for a decade.

Coppi's Giro wins in 1940, 1947, 1949, 1952 and 1953 trace the arc of his legend: the twenty-year-old prodigy, the post-war return, the years of the Campionissimo. The 1949 edition, which he won before going on to take the Tour, contains his most celebrated raid, a stage-long solo through the Alps from Cuneo to Pinerolo. When Italians say they grew up on cycling, this is the cycling they mean.
