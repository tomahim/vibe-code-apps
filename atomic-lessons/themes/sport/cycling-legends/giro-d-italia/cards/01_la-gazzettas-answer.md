The Giro d'Italia was born in 1909, directly inspired by the Tour de France. The pink newspaper La Gazzetta dello Sport wanted its own race to sell copies, and the first Giro set off from Milan: eight stages covering 2,448 kilometres around Italy.

The first edition was decided on points, not time, and was won by Luigi Ganna, a mason from Lombardy, who also won three of the eight stages and collected 5,325 lire. Only 49 riders finished. The race was an immediate national success, and it has been run by the Gazzetta's owners ever since, making the Giro the second-oldest Grand Tour and the emotional centre of Italian sport each May.
