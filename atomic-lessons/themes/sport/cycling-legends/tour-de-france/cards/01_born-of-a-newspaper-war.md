title: Born of a newspaper war

The Tour de France was created in 1903 to sell newspapers. L'Auto, a struggling French sports daily edited by the former cyclist Henri Desgrange, needed a sensation to crush its rival, and a stage race around the whole of France was the answer. Sixty riders set off from outside Paris for a 2,428-kilometre loop raced in just six enormous stages, some of them through the night.

Maurice Garin, a chimney sweep turned professional, won that first Tour. The race doubled L'Auto's circulation and killed off its competitor within a year. The formula has barely changed since: whoever has the lowest total time over about three weeks of racing wears the yellow jersey into Paris and wins the Tour.
