The Tour was transformed when it left the plains. In 1910 the race went into the Pyrenees for the first time, over tracks so rough that the organiser's telegram famously called the riders' survival into question; stage winner Octave Lapize shouted that the officials were assassins as he walked up the Aubisque. The Alps followed in 1911.

The big climbs became the race's cathedrals. The Tourmalet has appeared more than eighty times. Alpe d'Huez, first climbed in 1952, packs hundreds of thousands of fans onto its twenty-one hairpins on race day. In 2026 Tadej Pogačar climbed it in 35 minutes 27 seconds, a record. Tours are rarely won in the valleys: they are won where the road points at the sky.
