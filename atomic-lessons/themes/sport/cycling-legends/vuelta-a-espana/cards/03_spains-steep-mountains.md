If the Tour has its long Alpine cols and the Giro its Dolomite giants, the Vuelta has walls. The race specialises in summit finishes on absurdly steep climbs, none more feared than the Alto de l'Angliru in Asturias, whose final ramps tilt beyond twenty percent. The Lagos de Covadonga, the Bola del Mundo and the Sierra Nevada have all hosted decisive days.

These climbs changed Grand Tour tactics. Because gradients are so extreme, gaps of seconds become minutes, and the Vuelta is famous for late collapses and comebacks. A leader who looks safe can crack on one Asturian wall and lose everything. The race's reputation as the most unpredictable Grand Tour was built on those slopes.
