Every Grand Tour has its mythology, and the Vuelta's is built on drama. In 1985 Spain's Pedro Delgado lost the race in his home mountains before roaring back to win it the following year; Bernard Hinault and Miguel Indurain both used the Vuelta as a springboard or a crown, with Indurain never quite winning it despite five Tours.

The 2011 edition became infamous years later when winner Juan José Cobo was found to have doped and the title passed to Britain's Chris Froome, retrospectively making him the first British Grand Tour winner. In 2015 Fabio Aru beat a crashing field in the red jersey. And in 2026, leader Tadej Pogačar crashed out on stage eight, his first-ever Grand Tour abandon. The Vuelta rarely lets its stars leave without a story.
