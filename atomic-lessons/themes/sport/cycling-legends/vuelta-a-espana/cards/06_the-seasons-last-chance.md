Since 1995 the Vuelta has closed the Grand Tour season, and that slot defines its character. Riders who crashed or fell ill in May or July come to Spain for redemption; young riders make their names; Tour champions sometimes arrive chasing the rare Tour-Vuelta double.

Its late slot once flowed straight into the World Championships, making the Vuelta the perfect tune-up, though that rhythm now changes every fourth year when the Worlds move to August. The race's heat, steep climbs and end-of-season fatigue combine into something unique: a Grand Tour where form is fragile and almost every edition keeps its mystery into the final week.
