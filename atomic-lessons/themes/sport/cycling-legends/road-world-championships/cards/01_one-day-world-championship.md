The UCI Road World Championships are unlike almost everything else in cycling. Instead of trade teams, riders race for their national teams; instead of a season or a stage race, the title is decided on a single day. The first world championships were held in 1921 for amateurs only; the first professional championship came in July 1927 at the Nürburgring in Germany, won by the great Italian Alfredo Binda.

A women's road race was introduced in 1958, individual time trials for all categories in 1994, and a mixed team relay in 2019. The championships move to a different country every year, and the courses vary wildly: some favour sprinters, some climbers, some classic-men. That is the point - the rainbow jersey must be won in every kind of terrain.
