Indurain spent his first professional years working for others, chiefly Pedro Delgado, Reynolds' leader and the 1988 Tour winner. Riding as a domestique taught him the craft of stage racing: when to spend energy, when to hide, how a three-week race is actually won.

His own results grew steadily. He won Paris-Nice in 1989 and 1990, and took mountain stage wins at the Tour. The team recognised that his time trialling was close to unbeatable and his climbing was more than sufficient to defend what the clock won him. By 1991, Reynolds was ready to ride for him, and the Tour de France was about to enter the Indurain era.
