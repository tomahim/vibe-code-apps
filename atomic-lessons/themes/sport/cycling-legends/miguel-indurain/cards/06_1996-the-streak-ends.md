title: '1996: the streak ends'

Indurain came to the 1996 Tour de France chasing a record sixth consecutive victory. He had already won the Dauphiné that spring and looked ready. But cold, wet weather in the first week and a rare off-day on the road to Les Arcs broke his rhythm, and Denmark's Bjarne Riis rode away with the race. Indurain finished outside the top ten.

In January 1997, still only thirty-two, he announced his retirement. He gave a simple reason: after fourteen years as a professional he no longer had the will to train with the necessary intensity. There was no farewell tour, no long decline. The quiet giant simply stopped, leaving the sport with the strange feeling of a ruler who had abdicated.
