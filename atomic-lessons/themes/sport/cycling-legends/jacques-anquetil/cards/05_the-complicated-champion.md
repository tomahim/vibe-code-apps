Anquetil was strikingly honest about the realities of his era. Asked about doping, he answered that you do not ride the Tour de France on mineral water, a remark that shocked some and simply stated what many knew. Amphetamines were part of professional cycling's culture in his day, and he argued that professionals faced conditions amateurs could not judge. He died in 1987, at fifty-three, of stomach cancer.

His private life was equally unconventional and was chronicled in detail by the French press. What matters for his sporting story is simpler: he was the modern, calculating professional, open about treating racing as a job, at a time when the public preferred heroes who pretended to ride on pure heart.
