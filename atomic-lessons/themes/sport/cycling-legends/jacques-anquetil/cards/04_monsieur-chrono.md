Anquetil's weapon was the time trial, and his nickname was Monsieur Chrono: Mister Stopwatch. While rivals attacked on instinct, he rode to a plan, measuring his effort against the clock with a precision nobody else could match. On flat and rolling time-trial stages he was close to unbeatable, and he built his Tour victories by taking minutes where others took seconds.

His style divided opinion. Fans who loved panache preferred attackers like Poulidor, and Anquetil never hid that he raced to win, not to entertain. Yet his 1965 double shows his range: he won the week-long Critérium du Dauphiné Libéré and then, with barely a pause, the 557-kilometre Bordeaux-Paris classic the very next day, an almost impossible pairing that even his critics still admire.
