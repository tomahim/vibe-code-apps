Jacques Anquetil was born on 8 January 1934 in Mont-Saint-Aignan, near Rouen in Normandy. He got his first bicycle from his father at four, and grew up in the war years, when the family grew strawberries and the boy rode the produce to market. Racing was an escape from farm work, and he was good at it from the start.

He turned professional in the early 1950s and quickly specialised in the discipline that would define him: the individual time trial. Riding alone against the clock suited his smooth, economical style and his cold calculations. He won the Grand Prix des Nations, the era's most prestigious time trial, a record nine times over his career, and France began to talk about the quiet Norman as the heir to its Tour de France hopes.
