The 1964 Tour de France produced Anquetil's hardest defence. Raymond Poulidor, the darling of the French public, matched him in the mountains, and on the Puy-de-Dôme the two climbed side by side, elbows touching, neither able to drop the other. Anquetil kept yellow and won his fifth Tour in Paris by 55 seconds.

The rivalry defined both men. Anquetil got the records; Poulidor got the affection, and never won the Tour at all. Their contrast - the calculator against the romantic - shaped how France watched cycling for a decade. It also sharpened Anquetil's legacy: his five Tours were won against a rival strong enough to make every one of them a genuine fight.
