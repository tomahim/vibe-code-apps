Milan-San Remo is called the sprinters' classic, but that undersells the argument at its heart. The course is flat enough that a bunch finish is always possible, yet the Cipressa and Poggio give puncheurs and climbers just enough road to dream. Every March the same tactical war is fought: sprint teams control, attackers gamble.

The numbers favour the sprinters - most editions end in some kind of bunch sprint - but the race's legend is written by the exceptions. That tension is the point: a seven-hour race balanced on two little hills, where one acceleration can beat a hundred kilometres of teamwork.
