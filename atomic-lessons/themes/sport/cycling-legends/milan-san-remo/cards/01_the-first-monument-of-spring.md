Milan-San Remo is the traditional start of cycling's spring: the first of the five Monuments each year, held on a Saturday in March. The first edition ran in 1907, organised by the Gazzetta dello Sport, in brutal cold; only fourteen riders finished, and the winner was the Frenchman Lucien Petit-Breton, who covered the 286 kilometres at about 26 km/h.

Italians call it La Classicissima, the classic of classics. The concept has not changed in over a century: ride from Milan across the Lombardy plain, over the Apennines at the Passo del Turchino, then down the Ligurian coast to Sanremo. It is the longest one-day race in professional cycling, and one of the hardest to win.
