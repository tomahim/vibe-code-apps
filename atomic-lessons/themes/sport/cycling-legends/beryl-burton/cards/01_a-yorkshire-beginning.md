Beryl Burton was born Beryl Charnock in 1937 in the Halton area of Leeds, in Yorkshire, England. A serious illness as a teenager left her told to take care with exercise; instead she discovered cycling through her husband Charlie, a keen club rider, and found she was ferociously good at it.

She started with club time trials, the very British institution of racing alone against the clock on open roads, usually at dawn. There was no professional structure for women and almost no money in it. Burton worked ordinary jobs, raised a family, and trained before and after work. Within a few years she was competing internationally, and British cycling had never seen anything like her.
