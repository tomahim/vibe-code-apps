Burton's most famous ride came in 1967 in a 12-hour time trial. Her target was the women's record, but as the hours passed it became clear something bigger was happening. She caught and passed the fastest men on the road, including the holder of the men's record, and finished with 277.25 miles.

That distance beat the men's 12-hour record of the time by 0.73 miles, and no man bettered it until 1969. For two years the outright British 12-hour record, men's or women's, belonged to Beryl Burton. No other woman has ever held a men's national endurance record outright. The story is often retold with a detail she loved: as she passed the men's record holder, she offered him a liquorice allsort.
