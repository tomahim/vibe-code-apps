His nickname, Le Blaireau, the Badger, matched his temperament: he associated himself with the animal for its aggressive nature, and he embodied it on the bike. Nowhere did that show more than the 1980 World Championship at Sallanches, on a course often called the hardest in the event's history.

Hinault had quit that year's Tour with a knee injury while leading, and arrived angry. He broke away about 80 kilometres from the finish on a circuit so steep that most of the field simply gave up, and he rode alone to the rainbow jersey. It was the world title that completed his claim to be the best all-round rider of his generation, won in the style of a rider who refused to let injury decide his season.
