Unlike many Grand Tour specialists, Hinault hunted the one-day Monuments too. He won Liège-Bastogne-Liège twice, including the 1980 edition that became legend: snow and freezing wind cut the field to pieces, and Hinault rode away alone to win by nearly ten minutes, finishing with fingers so frostbitten that he suffered from them for years. Only about one rider in eight finished.

He also won Paris-Roubaix in 1981, despite a crash and despite despising the race, plus the Giro di Lombardia twice and the Amstel Gold Race and Flèche Wallonne. Five Monument wins sit alongside his ten Grand Tours: the mark of a champion who believed a leader should be able to win any race on the calendar.
