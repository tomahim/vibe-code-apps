Merckx earned his famous nickname early. The daughter of a teammate heard her father explain that Merckx would not let anyone else win, and she started calling him the Cannibal. The name stuck because it fit: he did not save his efforts for the races that mattered most but attacked everywhere, in small stage races and criteriums as readily as in the Tour de France.

In 1968 he moved to the Faema team and won the Giro d'Italia, his first Grand Tour. In 1969 he won his first Tour de France in outrageous style. On stage 17, already in yellow, he rode away alone over the Tourmalet and stayed clear for roughly 140 kilometres to Mourenx, then told journalists he hoped he had done enough for them to consider him a worthy winner. He finished that Tour with six stage wins plus the general, points, mountains and combination classifications.
