Eddy Merckx was born on 17 June 1945 in Meensel-Kiezegem, Belgium, and grew up in Woluwe-Saint-Pierre near Brussels, where his parents ran a grocery store. He tried several sports as a boy, but cycling won him over: he got his first bicycle at three or four and raced for the first time in 1961, taking his first victory at Petit-Enghien that October.

He piled up about 80 wins as an amateur, a tally that made Belgian professional teams take notice. On 29 April 1965 he signed with Solo-Superia and turned professional. A year later, after switching to Peugeot-BP-Michelin, he won Milan-San Remo, the first of his many victories in the five Monuments of cycling. He was twenty years old, and the sport had just met the rider who would dominate it like no one before or since.
