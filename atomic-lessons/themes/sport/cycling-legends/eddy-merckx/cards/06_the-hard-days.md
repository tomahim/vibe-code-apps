Even Merckx was not untouchable. Leading the 1969 Giro d'Italia after stage 16 to Savona, he was told a doping control had come back positive for fencamfamine and was thrown out of the race, in tears on live television. He always denied doping in that affair, and the episode remains one of the most controversial in the race's history.

His clearest defeat came at the 1975 Tour de France. On the climb to Puy-de-Dôme a spectator punched him in the side, leaving him with severe bruising, and later in the race he crashed and broke his cheekbone. Riding in pain and unable to eat solid food, he still finished second overall behind Bernard Thévenet. Those hard days matter for his story because they show the limit he finally met: after 1975 the Tour victories stopped, though he kept winning races until his retirement in 1978.
