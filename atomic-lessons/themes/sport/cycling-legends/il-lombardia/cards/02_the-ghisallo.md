The race's soul is a hill above Lake Como: the Madonna del Ghisallo. At its summit stands a small church, the sanctuary of the Madonna del Ghisallo, declared the patroness of cyclists by Pope Pius XII in 1949. Its walls are covered with the bikes and jerseys of champions, and next door stands a full cycling museum.

The climb itself is long and steady rather than savage, but its position in the finale makes it decisive: riders who escape over the Ghisallo can hold off chasers all the way to the finish in Como or Bergamo, depending on the year's route. Later additions like the Muro di Sormano, a wall with gradients over twenty percent, have only deepened the race's reputation as the climbers' Monument.
