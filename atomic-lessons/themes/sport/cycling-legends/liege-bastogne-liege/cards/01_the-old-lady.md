Liège-Bastogne-Liège is the oldest of cycling's five Monuments, which is why everyone calls it La Doyenne, the Old Lady. It was first run in 1892 as an amateur event, over 250 kilometres from Liège down to Bastogne in the Ardennes and straight back again. The first professional edition followed in 1894.

The course logic has never changed: out and back through the steep, forested hills of the Belgian Ardennes. The early editions were epics of endurance; Léon Houa won the first three. Through more than a hundred editions the race has been interrupted only by the World Wars, and it closes the spring classics season each April as the last and hilliest of the Monuments.
