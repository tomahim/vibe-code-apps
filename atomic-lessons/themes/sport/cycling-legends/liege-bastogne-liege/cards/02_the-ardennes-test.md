La Doyenne is about 250 kilometres of constant up and down. There are no cobbles and no single giant mountain: instead, a dozen short, steep côtes wear the field down one by one. The decisive names are the Côte de La Redoute, with its double-digit gradients through the houses of Aywaille, and the Côte de la Roche-aux-Faucons, the last launchpad before the run into Liège.

Modern editions have made the Redoute the race's signature: Tadej Pogačar has repeatedly attacked there to win. Because the climbs come one after another, the race suits Grand Tour riders more than any other classic; it is the Monument where the stage racers come out to fight the one-day specialists, and usually beat them.
