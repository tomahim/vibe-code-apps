Ask old Belgian fans about La Doyenne and one edition comes first: 1980, the year of the snow. A blizzard hit the Ardennes, temperatures plunged, and rider after rider climbed off. Of the 174 starters, only about twenty-one finished.

Bernard Hinault, who had nearly abandoned himself, rode away alone and won by almost ten minutes, his fingers so frostbitten that they troubled him for years. The 1980 edition is the extreme proof of the race's character: La Doyenne is never easy, but when the Ardennes weather turns, it becomes survival. Hinault called it one of his hardest days on a bike, which, from the Badger, meant something.
