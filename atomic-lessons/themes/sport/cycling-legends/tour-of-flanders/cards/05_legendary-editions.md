Ask Belgians for the greatest Ronde and you will start an argument. Eddy Merckx's 1969 win, a 70-kilometre solo through cold rain, is often cited as his greatest single day. The 1985 race in brutal weather, the 2010s duel between Tom Boonen and Fabian Cancellara, and van der Poel's recent battles with Pogačar all have their defenders.

What unites the great editions is the same ingredient: the race is hard enough that the strongest rider can win alone. Unlike Milan-San Remo, where the strongest often lose, the Ronde almost always crowns a worthy winner. That reliability is its pride: Flanders' Finest is where legends are proven, not made by luck.
