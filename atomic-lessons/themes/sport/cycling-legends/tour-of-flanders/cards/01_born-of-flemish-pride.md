The Tour of Flanders, the Ronde van Vlaanderen, was first held in 1913, the creation of the Dutch-language sports paper Sportwereld and its young cycling writer Karel Van Wijnendaele. It was as much a cultural statement as a bike race: a showcase for Flemish cycling at a time when French-speakers dominated Belgian public life.

The first edition was won by twenty-five-year-old Paul Deman, who took the sprint of a six-man group after more than twelve hours in the saddle. The race has run every year since 1919, the longest continuous sequence of any cycling classic, and its nickname says everything about what it means: Vlaanderens Mooiste, Flanders' Finest. On Ronde Sunday, Belgium simply stops.
