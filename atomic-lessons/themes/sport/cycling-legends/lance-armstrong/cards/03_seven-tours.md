From 1999 to 2005, riding for US Postal and then Discovery Channel, Armstrong won the Tour de France seven consecutive times, more than anyone in history. He dominated the time trials and the mountain finishes, protected by a famously drilled team that set a suffocating pace on every climb.

The sporting impact was enormous, especially in the United States, where his cancer comeback turned the Tour into mainstream news. Each July added to the myth: the look back at Jan Ullrich on Alpe d'Huez in 2001, the ride across a field to avoid a crash in 2003, the record-breaking seventh win in 2005. All of it would later be reclassified. At the time, it looked like the perfect story.
