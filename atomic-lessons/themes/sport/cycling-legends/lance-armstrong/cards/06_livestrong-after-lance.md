One part of the story outlived the scandal. Livestrong, the foundation Armstrong created after his diagnosis, separated from its founder and continued its work supporting people affected by cancer. For many survivors, the yellow wristband meant something real that the doping revelations did not erase.

The distinction matters for how the legend is told. Armstrong's cancer survival was genuine and his foundation's impact measurable; his seven Tours were fraudulent. Both things are true at once, and the tension between them is exactly why his story is still taught, debated and filmed. Few athletes force a sport to ask such hard questions about what it is willing to believe.
