Armstrong's era was cycling's dirtiest. The 1998 Festina affair had exposed systematic doping with EPO, a hormone that boosts endurance and was nearly undetectable at the time. Suspicion followed Armstrong from his first Tour win in 1999, and he denied doping for more than a decade, suing or bullying many who said otherwise.

Former teammates gradually broke ranks. The US Anti-Doping Agency's 2012 investigation gathered sworn testimony from eleven of them, describing organised doping on the US Postal team: EPO, blood transfusions, testosterone and cortisone, coordinated and concealed over years. USADA called it the most sophisticated, professionalised and successful doping program that sport had ever seen.
