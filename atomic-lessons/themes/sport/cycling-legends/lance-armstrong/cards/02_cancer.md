In October 1996, aged twenty-five, Armstrong was diagnosed with advanced testicular cancer that had spread to his lungs and brain. The disease was potentially fatal; doctors gave him less than a fifty percent chance of survival. He underwent brain surgery and an aggressive course of chemotherapy that he chose partly to protect his lungs for a possible return to racing.

He survived, and the experience reshaped him. In 1997 he founded the Lance Armstrong Foundation, later known as Livestrong, which went on to raise hundreds of millions of dollars for cancer support and made the yellow wristband a global symbol. His return to professional cycling in 1998 was, on its own terms, already one of sport's great comebacks, whatever came later.
