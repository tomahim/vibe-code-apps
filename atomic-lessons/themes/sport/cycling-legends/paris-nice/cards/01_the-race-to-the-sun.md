Paris-Nice has been held annually since 1933, making it one of the oldest stage races in cycling. Its idea is simple and brilliant: start in the cold and rain of the Paris region in early March, then race south for a week until the riders reach the sunshine of the Mediterranean. Hence the nickname every cycling fan knows: the Race to the Sun.

The race was created in the depths of the off-season to give professionals a serious early target, and it found a permanent slot as the first major European stage race of the year. Eight days, one direction, and a finish line in Nice: the format has survived almost a century of calendar upheaval.
