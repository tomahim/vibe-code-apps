title: Eight days south

The modern Paris-Nice runs over eight days, usually opening with a prologue or an individual time trial near Paris, then heading south through a mix of flat, hilly and windy stages. Echelons in the wind-swept plains north of the Rhône valley have decided the race as often as mountains have.

The finale is tradition itself: a last stage in Nice or, in many editions, a decisive uphill time trial on the Col d'Èze overlooking the city and the sea. Winning on the Col d'Èze, with the Mediterranean glittering below, is one of the sport's classic images. The race covers no great Alpine passes, yet its blend of time trials, wind and punchy hills makes it a complete test.
