The first edition was won by Patrick Renard, a runner from the marathon, according to French sources. His reward was a shepherd's bag, the first prize that referred to the local heritage of the plateau. It became a tradition that prizes should honour the region's culture.

The race is named after the Knights Templar, who once had commanderies on the Larzac plateau. The organisers use the Templar paths and villages as the backdrop for a sporting event. That link between history and landscape is one reason the race feels different from a purely athletic competition and why it attracted a lot of attention from the start.
