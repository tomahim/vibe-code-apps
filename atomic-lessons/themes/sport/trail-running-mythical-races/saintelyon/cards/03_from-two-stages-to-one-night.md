An Outside article on the race's history explains that in the following years the format evolved. Seven pioneers were allowed to make the whole trip in a single stage, with a start at midnight. As participation grew, a clock was added and the event became a running race. Today it is described as the oldest ultra in France and the oldest nature race in the country.

This is why the SaintéLyon is a night race. The midnight start of the early single-stage runs became the modern format, with several waves starting between 11:30 pm and 12:30 am in the past, and a 23:30 start listed for the 2026 edition. The headlamp became the symbol of the race.
