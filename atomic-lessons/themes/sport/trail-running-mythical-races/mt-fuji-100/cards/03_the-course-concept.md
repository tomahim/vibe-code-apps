The official concept page says the race explores the trails encircling Mt. Fuji, spanning 100 miles, around 160 km. It connects the rich culture and communities of the area, and it is held day and night, without breaks for sleep or rest.

The organisers say they want to highlight the significance of self-challenge and show the appeal of trail running as a sport. They also want to share the culture and natural beauty of Mt. Fuji, a valuable Japanese treasure, with the world and future generations. The course concept is explained with the idea of Kishotenketsu, a four-part structure of storytelling.
