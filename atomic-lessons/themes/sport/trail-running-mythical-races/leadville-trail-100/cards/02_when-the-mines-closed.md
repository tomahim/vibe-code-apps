By the early twentieth century Leadville was already in decline. The closure of the Climax Mine in the 1980s was a particularly heavy blow, leaving the town with the highest unemployment rate in the country, according to the race's own history. With almost the whole town out of work, Leadville struggled to find a new identity and a new source of income.

This is the important context for the race. It was not created for glory or for sport alone. It was created because a town needed a plan, and someone believed that visitors could help. That makes Leadville a rare example of a race born from an economic problem, with tourism as the solution.
