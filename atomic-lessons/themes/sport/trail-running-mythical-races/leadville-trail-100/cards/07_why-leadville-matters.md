Leadville is a lesson in motivation. Ken Chlouber and Merilee Maupin did not create the race to win records. They created it to help their neighbours. That gave the event a deep local meaning that still shapes its culture, from the scholarships to the volunteers.

It also became a model. The French founders of the Templiers race, as you will see later in this path, used Leadville and Western States as their examples. Together, those two US races showed that a 100-mile mountain race could be a community festival, a competition and a personal test at the same time. It is a good reminder that a race can be both a sporting event and a community project.
