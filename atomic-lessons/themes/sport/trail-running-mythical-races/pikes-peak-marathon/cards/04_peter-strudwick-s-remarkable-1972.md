The Pikes Peak history page keeps a "fun fact" that shows who the race attracts. In 1972, Peter Strudwick did the Ascent in 4 hours 20 minutes 29 seconds and the full Marathon in 7 hours 2 minutes 28 seconds. What makes it remarkable is how he was born. After his mother caught rubella, also called German measles, Peter was born with legs that ended in stumps just past the ankles, a left arm with one thumb and a finger, and a right arm ending at the wrist.

The Ascent is the up-only race, and the Marathon adds the descent, so Strudwick completed both formats. His story is one of the reasons the race is remembered as a challenge between people and the mountain, not only between runners.
