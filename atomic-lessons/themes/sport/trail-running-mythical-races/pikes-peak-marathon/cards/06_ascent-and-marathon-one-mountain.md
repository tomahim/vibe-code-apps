Pikes Peak hosts two linked events. The Ascent is the race to the summit. The Marathon is the full out-and-back, where runners climb to the top and then run down the same mountain to finish where they started. The race results from 1993 show why the split matters: Carpenter took 2:01:06 to climb but only 1:15:33 to descend, so the climb is much slower than the descent even for the winner.

The descent is not easy, though. After hours of climbing at altitude, legs are tired, and a steep downhill on rocky trail punishes them even more. That is why mountain runners treat Pikes Peak as two races in one. You need a strong climb and the discipline to save legs for the way down.
