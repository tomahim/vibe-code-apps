title: "The mountain that turned Pike back"

The race is named after the peak, and the peak is named after Zebulon Pike, who tried to climb it and failed. According to the race's own history, Pike was turned back by harsh weather. The snow was waist deep, his men were not dressed for it, and they had run out of food. Many people claim he said nobody would ever reach the summit, but the history says it is generally accepted that he meant on that day, under those conditions.

That story still describes the mountain well. Pikes Peak reaches about 4,300 metres (14,115 feet), so the weather at the top can be very different from the weather in town. The race organisers record heat waves, lightning strikes and even blizzards in the race's history, which is why runners must be ready for fast changes.
