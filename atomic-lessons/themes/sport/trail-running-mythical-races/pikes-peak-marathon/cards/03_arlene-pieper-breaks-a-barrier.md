Women were allowed to enter from the very first race in 1956, but in the early years they were not always allowed to run the full marathon. In 1958 Arlene Pieper stopped at the top of the peak and was disqualified. The next year, on Friday, August 7, 1959, she ran the whole race, up and back down.

According to the race archive, that made her the first woman to officially finish a marathon in the United States. The result matters for the history of the sport as a whole, not only for Pikes Peak. Women's distance running was still barely accepted in the 1950s, and a mountain race in Colorado gave one runner the chance to put an official finish on the record years before road marathons opened to women.
