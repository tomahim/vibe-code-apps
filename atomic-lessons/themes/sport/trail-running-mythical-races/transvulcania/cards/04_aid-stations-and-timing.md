The race has a table of kilometre points with altitudes, distances, elevation gain and cut-off times. Examples are Los Canarios at 715 metres, Las Deseadas at 1,827 metres, El Pilar at 1,445 metres and El Reventon at 1,415 metres.

Time limits help organisers manage safety and logistics on a route with big changes in altitude. The official page also lists the mandatory basic equipment, so runners know what to carry. Transvulcania is a point-to-point race, so aid stations and cut-offs must work for a long linear route with limited road access. The altitude column of the table makes the profile easy to read before race day.
