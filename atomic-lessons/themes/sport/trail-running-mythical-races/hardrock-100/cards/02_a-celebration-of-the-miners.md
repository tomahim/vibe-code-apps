A basic principle of the course was to use old mining trails as much as possible. The founders wanted the event to be a physical and psychological challenge for runners and also a celebration of the miners who made the area what it had become.

They imagined it as a "Post Graduate" run. Entry was limited to runners who had finished several certified 100-mile races or who, in the opinion of the run management, were capable of finishing what was meant to be the most mountainous trail run in the United States. That requirement still shapes the race's culture, which treats experience and respect for the mountains as essential.
