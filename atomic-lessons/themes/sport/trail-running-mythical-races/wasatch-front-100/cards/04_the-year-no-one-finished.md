The early years were hard. The history lists 1981 as "The Year No One Finished" and 1982 as a "Suffer Fest" in which only three of 19 starters survived. The race had not yet become the organised event it is today, and the course was long, steep and remote.

The race slowly grew into the foundation of Utah ultrarunning. The official history describes 1983 as the year Wasatch "came of age", and later annual reports were published in Ultrarunning magazine, whose founders Fred Pilon and Peter Gagarin attempted the 1982 race and dropped at mile 73. Gagarin finished in 1988, so the magazine and the race grew up together.
