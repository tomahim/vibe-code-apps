An island race has natural advantages. The terrain changes quickly with altitude, from beaches to high ridges within a few kilometres. Gran Canaria's ravines, pine forests and villages give organisers many different landscapes to choose from.

This lesson is about the ideas behind Transgrancanaria, and similar ideas appear in other islands you will see in this path, such as Reunion and La Palma. Runners can experience several landscapes in one outing, which is a strong reason for the popularity of island ultras.

The Spanish encyclopedia says the race was created in October 2003 to show the beauty and the contrasts of the island. The route moves from the north coast across the interior to the south, through ravines, pine forests and volcanic landscapes. That is the main reason this race belongs in the island group of the path, next to Transvulcania.
