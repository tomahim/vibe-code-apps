The inspiration for the Barkley in 1986 was the 1977 escape of James Earl Ray from Brushy Mountain State Prison. Ray, the convicted assassin of Martin Luther King Jr., spent more than two days in the rugged Cumberland Mountains trying to get away. His escape became folklore.

Ultrarunning History traces how the mountains, the mines, the prison and the escape all played a part in the birth of the Barkley. The prison was a four-story wooden building in a valley with mountains on three sides, and a park ranger says the mountains are more of a prison than the prison. There is one way in and one way out.
