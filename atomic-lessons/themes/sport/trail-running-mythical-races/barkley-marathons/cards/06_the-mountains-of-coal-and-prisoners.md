The Barkley's land has a hard history. The state purchased 13,000 acres, used inmates to build Brushy Mountain Prison and then put prisoners to work in the mines. Conditions were harsh and many prisoners died, and the article says more than 400 bodies are buried on the prison property.

Escapes were common between 1922 and 2009, with hundreds recorded, though the story that the prison was escape-proof is false. The mountains did make escape difficult. This is the setting that gave the race its tone: a place where terrain can defeat even determined people. Understanding that background explains why the Barkley feels different from a race in a purely natural park.
