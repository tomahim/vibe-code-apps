title: "What Western States gave the sport"

Western States turned a one-person experiment into a template. A famous trail, a fixed time limit, medical monitoring and volunteer aid stations became the standard recipe for the 100-mile race. Other races took the same approach, and the French founders of the Grand Trail des Templiers, for example, openly modelled their race on Western States and Leadville, which you will see later in this path.

It also set a human tone. Early finishers included a 22-year-old and runners in their fifties, and the 30-hour award celebrated finishing within a day and a half instead of winning. The message was that the 100-mile distance is a personal challenge first. That spirit is the reason Western States is still called the original.
