In 1972, a group of 20 U.S. Army infantrymen attempted to hike the trail nonstop, starting one day ahead of the Tevis Cup ride on July 28. They were members of the 6th Battalion of the 67th Air Defense Artillery Regiment from Fort Riley, Kansas, and were guided by local horseman Jim Larimer. Seven of the soldiers completed the distance on foot in under 48 hours, and the Auburn Journal called it the first "Auburn Endurance March."

That was a hike, not a run, but it proved that the route could be done on foot. It set up the next idea, which was whether someone could run most of the trail inside the horses' 24-hour limit.
