In 1974, Tevis veteran Gordy Ainsleigh joined the horses of the Tevis Cup on foot. He wanted to see if he could complete the course while running the majority of it. According to the official Western States history, on August 3, 1974, he joined the 198 Tevis Cup horses and, 23 hours and 42 minutes later, he arrived in Auburn.

That proved a runner could cover the whole trail within the 24-hour time limit. The idea had been encouraged by Drucilla Barner, the first woman to win the Tevis Cup. The 1974 result is recorded as one starter and one finisher, a 100 percent finish rate. It is the moment most people call the beginning of 100-mile racing.
