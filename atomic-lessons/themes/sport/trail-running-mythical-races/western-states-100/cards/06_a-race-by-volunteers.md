The first Western States races relied on communities, doctors and volunteers. Early runners were responsible for their own supplies except water, and the organisers moved their gear for them. The Run organisation later became its own entity, now called the Western States Endurance Run Foundation, and the race still depends on volunteers at its aid stations.

This model shaped the rest of the sport. Many later 100-mile races, including some you will meet in this path, copied the idea of a community event built on volunteers and medical checks. Western States is also still run on the last weekend in June, with the start in Olympic Valley. Demand exceeds the 369 entrants allowed each year, which is why entry is competitive.
