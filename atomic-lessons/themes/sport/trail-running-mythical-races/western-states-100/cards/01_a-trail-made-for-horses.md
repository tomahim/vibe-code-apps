The Western States Trail runs through the Sierra Nevada of California from Tahoe City toward Auburn. In 1955 Wendell T. Robie and five horsemen rode it to prove that horses could cover 100 miles in one day. That ride led to the Western States Trail Foundation and the Western States Trail Ride, also called the Tevis Cup.

So the trail was already a legend before anybody ran it. The Tevis Cup had a 24-hour limit, and for years the question was whether a person on foot could do what horses did. The answer would change the sport of running. A race that is now famous around the world began as a question about the endurance of horses and humans on the same trail.
