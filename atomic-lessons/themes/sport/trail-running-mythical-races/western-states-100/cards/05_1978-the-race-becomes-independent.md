In 1978 the Western States Run took a big step. After a year-long effort by what the history calls the "Gang of Four" - Phil Gardner, Mo Livermore, Shannon Weil and Curt Sproul - the race was held in June, a month earlier than the Tevis Cup. It grew to include 21 aid stations and six medical checks, supported by volunteers, the Placer County Sheriff's Communications Reserve and the Search and Rescue Unit.

63 people ran the race that year. The first woman to finish, Pat Smythe, crossed the line in just under 30 hours (the official pages list 29:34 in one place and 29:24 in another). In 1979, 143 runners from 21 states and three foreign countries tried the course. From then on, interest outgrew the number of places available.
