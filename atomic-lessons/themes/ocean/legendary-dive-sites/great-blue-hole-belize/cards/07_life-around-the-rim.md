The most alive part of the Blue Hole is its edge. The shallow reef rim lies close to the surface, slopes down, and then drops as a near-vertical cliff. This rim supports a community of corals and tropical fish.

Species recorded there include groupers, squirrelfish, angelfish, butterflyfish and midnight parrotfish. Reef sharks also visit, including nurse sharks, Caribbean reef sharks and blacktip sharks, and smaller residents such as sea anemones, Pederson's cleaner shrimp and neon gobies live on the reef. So the famous centre is mostly empty, deep water, while the shallow reef at the edge holds the visible wildlife. If you picture the hole as an aquarium full of fish, the real picture is the opposite: a deep, quiet void ringed by a busy reef.
