One detail from the stalactites surprised the scientists. Some of them were tilted, and all were leaning in a consistent direction. Stalactites hang straight down under gravity, so a lean means the rock itself moved after they formed.

That tilt shows that the plateau under Lighthouse Reef was shifted by geological movement at some point in the past, and then stayed in its new position for a long time. In other words, the Blue Hole records two kinds of change: the rise and fall of the sea, and slower movement of the ground. Scientists use features like these as natural archives, reading the ledges and the stalactites the way a historian reads layers in a document.
