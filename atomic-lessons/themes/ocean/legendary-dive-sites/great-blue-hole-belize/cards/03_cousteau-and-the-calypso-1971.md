The Blue Hole became famous through Jacques Cousteau. In 1971 he brought his research ship Calypso to Lighthouse Reef to chart the depths of the hole. He declared it one of the top five scuba diving sites in the world, and his work put it on the map for the public.

The expedition did more than take photographs. Its investigations confirmed that the hole was a typical karst limestone formation, formed in at least four stages as sea level rose. They found ledges on the walls at depths of about 21 m, 49 m and 91 m, and they retrieved stalactites from submerged caves. Those stalactites were the proof that these spaces had once been above water.
