The Blue Hole became famous through Jacques Cousteau. In the early 1970s he brought his research ship Calypso to Lighthouse Reef to chart the depths of the hole. His expedition put the Blue Hole on the map for the public. The lesson is that a famous place often becomes known through one person's work, and that the stories told about it are worth checking against the science that came later.

The expedition did more than take photographs. Its investigations confirmed that the hole was a typical karst limestone formation, formed in several stages as sea level rose. They found ledges on the walls, and they retrieved stalactites from submerged caves. Those stalactites were the proof that these spaces had once been above water.
