In the caves of the Riviera Maya fresh water sits on top of a layer of salt water that extends under the whole peninsula. The boundary between the two is called the halocline. Salt water is denser, so it stays below the lighter fresh water.

When a diver crosses the halocline, the two kinds of water mix and bend the light, and visibility drops, as if looking through a heat haze or a mirage. It also affects buoyancy, because salt water is denser than fresh water. This is a simple demonstration of physics in action. Cave divers study these effects in training, because buoyancy and visibility problems matter much more when there is rock overhead.
