The caves are more than a dive site. They are part of the aquifer, the underground store of fresh water beneath the peninsula. Because rain drains straight through limestone, anything that goes onto the surface can end up in the water below.

This is why protecting cenotes matters for both nature and people. A diver who touches a stalactite can break it, and a surface pollutant can reach the cave system within the aquifer. The fossil evidence found in these caves, from mastodons to the skeleton of Naia, is also fragile and needs protection. A legendary dive site is sometimes a library, and the right way to use a library is to read carefully and not tear out pages.
