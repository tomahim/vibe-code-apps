In 2007 and 2008 divers from the Tulum cave exploration project and Global Underwater Explorers explored a section called Hoyo Negro, Spanish for black hole. They found the remains of a mastodon, and a human skull.

The skeleton belonged to a teenage girl, now called Naia, who lived about 12,000 years ago. At that time sea level was much lower and the cave was dry, so she probably fell into a deep pit. After the last ice age the rising sea flooded the passages and preserved the bones along with the bones of extinct animals. Naia is one of the oldest well-preserved human skeletons in the Americas, and she helps scientists understand where the first Americans came from.
