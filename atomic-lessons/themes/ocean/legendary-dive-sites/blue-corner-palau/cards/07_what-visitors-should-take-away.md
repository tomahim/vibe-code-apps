Blue Corner shows how geography, ecology and policy connect. The reef wall and the plateau concentrate currents and food. The food supports sharks and many other fish. And a national government decided that the animals and the diving they attract were worth protecting.

Conditions at these sites are demanding and change with the tide, which is why they are visited with local guides. For the rest of us, the lesson is simpler: when a place is famous for a particular animal, it is often because the physical shape of the reef and the movement of the water create the conditions. Protect the shape and the flow, and the wildlife follows.
