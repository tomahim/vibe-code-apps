The shape of the reef matters. The top of Blue Corner is a sloping plateau at about 15 to 25 m deep, with steep drop-offs on both sides. Small coral heads, sandy patches and natural channels run across the shelf, and to the west the wall plunges into very deep water.

When ocean currents hit a point of reef like this, they speed up and carry plankton and small fish across the plateau. Larger fish gather where the food is concentrated. Predators such as grey reef sharks patrol the edge and use the current to hold position with little effort. The same current is what makes a visit demanding, because it can change with the tide, and local guides plan every dive around it.
