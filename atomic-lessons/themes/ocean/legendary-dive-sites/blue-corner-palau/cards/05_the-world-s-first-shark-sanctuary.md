On 25 September 2009 Palau's President Johnson Toribiong told the United Nations General Assembly that his country would create the world's first shark sanctuary. The plan banned all commercial shark fishing in Palau's territorial waters.

His declaration pointed out that sharks are top predators and play an important role in keeping marine ecosystems healthy, and that many species were threatened. The announcement shows how a country with famous dive sites can turn what divers value into national policy.

The declaration gave figures to explain the urgency: about 38% of sharks and related rays were threatened or near threatened with extinction, scientists lacked data on another 35% of species, and roughly 100 million sharks were killed each year, largely for their fins. The Pew Environment Group welcomed it as the first international shark sanctuary.
