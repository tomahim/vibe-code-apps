title: "Meltwater with a thirty-year journey"

Silfra is fed by groundwater that began as meltwater from Langjökull, Iceland's second largest glacier, about 50 km to the north. A few thousand years ago, lava flows from the Skjaldbreiður volcano blocked the river that once carried this water straight into the lake. The meltwater ponded and then seeped into the porous lava rock, forming an aquifer.

The water travels slowly through the lava, taking from about thirty to a hundred years before it emerges from springs in the fissures. The lava acts as a giant natural filter, so the water that arrives is exceptionally clear and drinkable. The name Silfra means silver, and refers to the shine and clarity of the water.
