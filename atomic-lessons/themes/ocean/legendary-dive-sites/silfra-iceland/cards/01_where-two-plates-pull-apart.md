Silfra is a rift in Iceland's Þingvallavatn lake, inside Þingvellir National Park. It lies on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, the boundary where the North American plate and the Eurasian plate move away from each other. Iceland is one of the few places where this ridge rises above the ocean, so you can see a plate boundary on land.

The plates drift apart by about 2 cm per year. That is roughly the speed at which fingernails grow, but over millions of years it widens an ocean. Silfra is one of about 100 fissures in a stretch about 5 km wide between the two plate edges, and it is one of the largest and deepest of them.
