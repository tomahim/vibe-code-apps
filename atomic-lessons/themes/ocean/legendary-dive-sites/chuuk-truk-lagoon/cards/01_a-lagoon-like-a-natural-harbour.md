Chuuk Lagoon, formerly known as Truk, is an atoll in the central Pacific Ocean, part of Chuuk State in the Federated States of Micronesia. A protective reef encloses a very large natural harbour.

That huge sheltered space is the reason for its history. Ships could anchor safely behind the reef, with only a few channels in and out. The name Chuuk means mountain in the Chuukese language, and the lagoon was known mainly as Truk until late in the twentieth century. Today tourism, especially scuba diving among the wrecks, is the main industry of the islands. That shelter is why the lagoon matters to this story. A harbour with a reef for a wall is easy to defend and hard to enter, which is exactly what a navy wants from a base far from home.
