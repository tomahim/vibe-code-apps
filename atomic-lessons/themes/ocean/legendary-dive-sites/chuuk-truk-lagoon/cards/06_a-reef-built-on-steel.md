Coral and sponges have grown over the ships over the decades, which turns each wreck into an artificial reef. The coral-encrusted wrecks attract manta rays, turtles, sharks and many reef fish. In 2007 an Earthwatch team recorded 266 species of reef fish, and in 2006 a rare coral, Acropora pichoni, was identified.

This makes Truk both a historic site and a living habitat. The two roles can conflict. Divers who touch coral, or who take souvenirs, damage both. For this reason, the usual rule at wrecks of this kind is to leave everything as found, so that archaeologists, descendants and later divers can see the same evidence.
