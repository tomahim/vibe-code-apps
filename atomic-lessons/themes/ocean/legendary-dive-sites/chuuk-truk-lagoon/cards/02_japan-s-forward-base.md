Japanese forces occupied Micronesia in 1914 and set up Truk as a base as early as 1939. It was used as an anchorage for the Imperial Japanese Navy's 4th Fleet, and during the Second World War it became the navy's main base in the South Pacific, with port facilities, airfields and shore batteries. Fortification began in earnest in late 1943.

The base also served as a supply hub. Merchant ships carried fuel, ammunition, vehicles and other cargo to Truk, and aircraft were repaired and distributed from there. The Americans understood that attacking Truk would hurt Japanese logistics as well as warships, and it could protect their planned invasion of Eniwetok in the Marshall Islands.
