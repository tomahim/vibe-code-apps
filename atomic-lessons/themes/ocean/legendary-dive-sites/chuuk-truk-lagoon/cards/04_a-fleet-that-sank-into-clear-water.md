After the war the wrecks stayed where they sank. Many lie inside the lagoon, scattered around the islands of Dublon, Eten, Fefan and Uman, some in shallow water. The lagoon water is clear, so sunlight reaches many decks.

In 1969 the American William Brown and Jacques Cousteau's team explored the lagoon. After Cousteau's 1971 documentary about its ghostly remains, Truk became a draw for wreck divers from around the world, who called the ships the Ghost Fleet of Truk Lagoon. Because the ships were sunk in a narrow window of time, in a sheltered lagoon, the collection is unusually complete. It is hard to find anywhere else 50 or 60 wrecks in such conditions.
