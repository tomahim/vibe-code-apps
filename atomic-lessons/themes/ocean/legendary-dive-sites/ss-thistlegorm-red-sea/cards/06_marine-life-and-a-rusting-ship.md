A wreck becomes a reef over time. Common animals around the Thistlegorm include tuna, barracuda, batfish, moray eels, lionfish, stonefish, crocodilefish, scorpionfish and sea turtles. Steel in warm salt water attracts coral, sponges and fish, which use the structure for shelter and hunting.

At the same time the ship is slowly being destroyed. Natural rusting is breaking down the hull, and the dive boats that depend on the wreck also damage it when they moor to weak parts. In December 2007 an environmental group installed 32 mooring buoys and drilled holes in the hull to release trapped air, but by 2009 none of the moorings remained because they were too light for the currents and moored ships. Boats moor to the wreck again today.
