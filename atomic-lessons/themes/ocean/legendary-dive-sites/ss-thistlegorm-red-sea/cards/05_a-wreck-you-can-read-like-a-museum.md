The Thistlegorm is popular because so much of her cargo is still on board. The bomb explosion blew away much of her midship superstructure, which makes the wreck open and accessible. Her depth is around 30 m at the deepest, shallower than many famous wrecks.

In the holds divers can see trucks, motorcycles, Wellington boots, rifles, aircraft parts such as Westland Lysander wings and Bristol Blenheim bomber tailplanes, and Universal Carriers. The two steam locomotives that were carried on deck were blown off the ship by the blast, and now lie on either side of the wreck. Each object is a snapshot of what the Allied forces in Egypt needed in 1941, preserved in place by the sea.
