Jacques Cousteau located the wreck in the early 1950s, using information from local fishermen. His divers brought up items including a motorcycle, the captain's safe and the ship's bell, and he described the dive in his book The Living Sea. Then the site was forgotten, except by local fishermen.

In the early 1990s the town of Sharm el-Sheikh began to develop as a diving resort, and recreational diving on the wreck restarted after the dive boat Poolster visited, using information from an Israeli fishing boat captain. The lesson is that even a famous wreck can disappear from public memory. A wreck of about 4,900 tons is easy to lose, because without a precise position the sea is enormous and the visible surface shows nothing.
