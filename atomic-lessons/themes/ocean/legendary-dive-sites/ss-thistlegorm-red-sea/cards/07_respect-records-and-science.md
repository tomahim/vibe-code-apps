The Thistlegorm is also a war grave, because nine people lost their lives in the attack. This is why divers are asked to look but not to take items from wrecks like this one. Once an object is removed, the context that makes the wreck valuable is gone.

The wreck keeps teaching in new ways. In 2007 The Times named it one of the top ten wreck diving sites in the world. Detailed images like this are an important record, because the ship is rusting and parts are collapsing.

The same wreck can be a memorial, a classroom and a dive site. Each use works only if visitors treat it with care, because a hull that has lasted decades can still be damaged by one careless anchor.
