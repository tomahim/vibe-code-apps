The Thistlegorm is also a war grave, because nine people lost their lives in the attack. This is why divers are asked to look but not to take items from wrecks like this one. Once an object is removed, the context that makes the wreck valuable is gone.

The wreck keeps teaching in new ways. In 2007 The Times named it one of the top ten wreck diving sites in the world, and in 2021 a photographer named Simon Brown won a Royal Photographic Society science prize for an orthophoto of the whole wreck, assembled from 15,005 merged frames. Detailed images like this are an important record, because the ship is rusting and parts are collapsing.
