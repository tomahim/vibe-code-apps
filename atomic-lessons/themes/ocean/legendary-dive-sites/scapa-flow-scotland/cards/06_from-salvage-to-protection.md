Seven of the remaining wrecks, three battleships and four cruisers, were scheduled as protected monuments under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Areas Act 1979. Divers are allowed to visit them, as long as no damage occurs and nothing is removed.

In 2025 the protection was extended to the whole site, when the Scottish government designated it a Historic Marine Protected Area, which took effect on 1 November 2025 ([Scottish Government order](https://www.gov.scot/publications/scapa-flow-historic-marine-protected-area-order-2025/)). This change reflects a shift in attitude. A century ago the wrecks were seen as scrap with value. Today they are seen as heritage, and the rules say that the best way to value them is to leave them where they are.
