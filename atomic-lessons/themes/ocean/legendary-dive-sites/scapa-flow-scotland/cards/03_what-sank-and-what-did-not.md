title: "What sank, and what did not"

Of the 74 German ships at Scapa Flow, 52 sank. British guard ships were able to beach some of the others, so that they did not sink. The beached ships were later shared out among the Allied navies, but most of the sunken ships were left where they lay at first, because salvaging them was judged not worth the cost.

During the afternoon, 1,774 Germans were picked up from boats and the water. They were treated as prisoners of war for breaking the armistice. A British admiral told Reuter that his action was dishonourable. Whatever the judgement, the event left a huge collection of ships on the bottom of a sheltered bay.
