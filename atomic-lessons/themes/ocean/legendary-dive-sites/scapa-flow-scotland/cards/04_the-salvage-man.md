After the war there was plenty of scrap metal available, so at first the sunken ships were mostly left on the bottom. An entrepreneur named Ernest Cox bought 26 destroyers from the Admiralty for £250, along with the large ships Seydlitz and Hindenburg, and began raising them.

He lifted 24 of his 26 destroyers over about a year and a half, using a purchased German floating dock, and then began work on bigger vessels. His new technique was to have divers patch the holes in the submerged hulls so that the ships could be lifted. Over the following decades, many wrecks were salvaged and towed away for scrapping. By the end of this industrial phase, only the deepest and most difficult wrecks remained.
