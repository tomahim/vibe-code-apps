The Galápagos Islands lie about a thousand kilometres from the mainland of Ecuador. The surrounding Galápagos Marine Reserve covers around 133,000 square kilometres, and the islands were declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1978. The reserve is one of the largest marine reserves in the world.

The marine life is unusual because several kinds of ocean current come together here. Three ocean currents meet, and the mixing supports a wide range of animals, from small coloured fish to large mammals. Marine iguanas, which are the only lizards that feed in the sea, live alongside sea lions, blue-footed boobies and giant tortoises on land, and sharks and rays in the water.
