The waters around Darwin's Arch are famous for large marine animals. Scalloped hammerhead sharks gather in big schools, and the area also has manta rays, big-eye jacks, bonito, yellowfin tuna, dolphins and large schools of other open-ocean fish. Whale sharks, the largest fish in the sea, are also seen there.

Other species include Galapagos sharks, eagle rays, green and hawksbill turtles, silky and whitetip reef sharks, barracuda and black jacks. These are mostly pelagic animals, which means they live in open water and travel long distances, so they cross the reserve rather than living on one reef. The whale sharks arrive from early July, so the season matters. Seeing any of these animals depends on currents, season and luck.
