In the north of the archipelago lies Darwin Island, a small uninhabited island of 2.33 square kilometres with a height of 168 m. It is named after Charles Darwin, the English naturalist whose studies in the Galápagos helped him to form his theory of evolution by natural selection.

Tourists are not permitted to set foot on the island. Its main visitors are birds, such as the sooty tern, which breeds there, and the many fish that gather offshore. It is a small piece of land, but the waters around it are some of the most productive in the reserve. The Galápagos Islands and their surrounding waters are one of the most unusual ecosystems in the world, and they are rich in biodiversity.
