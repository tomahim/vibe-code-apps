A kilometre to the south-east of Darwin Island stood Darwin's Arch, a natural rock arch with a bridge-like shape formed by erosion, standing out in the open ocean.

The arch was popular with photographers and cruise-ship tours. It was also a landmark for divers, because the surrounding water was a gathering place for large animals. People visited the arch for years, and it became one of the best-known images of the Galápagos, but it was always the same kind of structure as any sea stack: rock, shaped by waves, with no guarantee of lasting.

For a diver, an arch like this is more than scenery. It breaks the current and creates a place where fish and sharks gather. Understanding how such a landmark forms makes it easier to see why the water around it is so busy.
