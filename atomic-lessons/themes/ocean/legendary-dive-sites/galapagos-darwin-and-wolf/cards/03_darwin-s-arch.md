title: "Darwin's Arch"

A kilometre to the south-east of Darwin Island stood Darwin's Arch, a natural rock arch with a bridge-like shape formed by erosion. It was about 43 m high, 70 m long and 23 m wide. It stood on a rocky submerged plateau nicknamed the theatre, with a viewing platform on the ocean side at 18 m.

The arch was popular with photographers and cruise-ship tours. It was also a landmark for divers, because the surrounding water was a gathering place for large animals. People visited the arch for years, and it became one of the best-known images of the Galápagos, but it was always the same kind of structure as any sea stack: rock, shaped by waves, with no guarantee of lasting.
