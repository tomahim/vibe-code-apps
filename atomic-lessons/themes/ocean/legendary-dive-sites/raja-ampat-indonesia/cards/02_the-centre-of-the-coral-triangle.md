Raja Ampat is part of the Coral Triangle, an area of Southeast Asian seas considered to contain the richest marine biodiversity on Earth. The Coral Triangle lies roughly west-southwest of the Philippines, east-northeast and southeast of Borneo, and north, east and west of New Guinea, including the seas in between.

Within it, Raja Ampat is called the crown jewel of the Bird's Head Seascape, which also includes Cenderawasih Bay and Triton Bay. The region has more than 600 species of hard corals, about 75% of the world's known species, and more than 1,600 species of reef fish. Comparing similarly sized ecosystems elsewhere, its biodiversity is arguably the richest.
