Raja Ampat is an archipelago off the north-west tip of the Bird's Head Peninsula on the island of New Guinea, in Southwest Papua province, Indonesia. It spans roughly 610 islands, atolls and takas, of which only 34 are inhabited, with four main islands: Misool, Salawati, Batanta and Waigeo.

The name means four kings. Local mythology tells of a woman who found seven eggs, and three of them hatched into kings who ruled the islands, with a fourth settling at Waigama. The islands lie just south of the equator, so the water is warm all year.

The scale matters for the story. A scattered archipelago has many reefs, channels and bays, and each can hold a different community of corals and fish. That variety is part of why the area is so rich.
