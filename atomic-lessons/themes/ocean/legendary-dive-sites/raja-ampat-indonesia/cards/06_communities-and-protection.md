Later English explorer William Dampier gave his name to Dampier Strait, between Batanta and Waigeo. But the islands were home to people long before.

Today local communities take part in conservation. In the north-east of Waigeo villagers protect nests and move eggs of leatherback, olive ridley and hawksbill turtles, with support from the local government and non-governmental groups. Large parts of the archipelago are now protected as marine parks. Protection works best when the people who live nearby are partners.

The lesson is that a marine park on a map is only a start. Rules are easier to keep when local families have a reason to protect the reef, such as fishing, tourism work and pride in a place that is theirs. Remote sites like this one have few officials, so the community is often the first line of care.
