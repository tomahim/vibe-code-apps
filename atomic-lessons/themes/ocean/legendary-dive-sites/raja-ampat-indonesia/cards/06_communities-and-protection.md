The islands have been home to people for a very long time, and today local communities take part in conservation. In the north-east of Waigeo villagers protect sea turtle nests and move eggs, with support from the local government and non-governmental groups. Large parts of the archipelago are now protected as marine parks. Protection works best when the people who live nearby are partners.

The lesson is that a marine park on a map is only a start. Rules are easier to keep when local families have a reason to protect the reef, such as fishing, tourism work and pride in a place that is theirs. Remote sites like this one have few officials, so the community is often the first line of care.
