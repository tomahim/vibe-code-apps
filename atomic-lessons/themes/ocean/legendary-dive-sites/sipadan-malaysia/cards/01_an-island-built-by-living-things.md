Sipadan is the only oceanic island in Malaysia. It sits in the Celebes Sea off the east coast of Sabah, on Malaysian Borneo, and it rises about 600 m from the seabed. An oceanic island is one that grew up from deep ocean, not one that was once part of a continent.

Sipadan has an unusual structure. It was formed by living corals growing on top of an extinct volcanic cone, and that process took thousands of years. The volcano supplied the base, and the corals did the rest, adding limestone layer by layer near the surface where sunlight reaches them. So the green island you can stand on is the top of a coral-covered mountain whose lower slopes plunge into deep water.
