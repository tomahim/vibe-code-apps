Sipadan also has a place in international law. For years Malaysia and Indonesia both claimed the island. The dispute was brought to the International Court of Justice, which at the end of 2002 awarded Sipadan and the nearby island of Ligitan to Malaysia.

The Court based its decision on effective occupation, meaning the administration shown by Malaysia's predecessor, the United Kingdom, and the absence of any stronger title. The Philippines had asked to take part in the proceedings, on the basis of a claim to northern Borneo, but the Court refused the request in early 2001. For the dive world the ruling settled which government was responsible for managing the reef. The case is a reminder that even a tiny island can matter greatly when the surrounding reef is valuable.
