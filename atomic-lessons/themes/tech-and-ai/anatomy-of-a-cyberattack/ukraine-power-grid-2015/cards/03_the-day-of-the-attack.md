On the day of the attack, the CISA alert says multiple people operated the breakers remotely, using remote administration tools or the control system's client software over a VPN with legitimate credentials. The attacks at the three companies came within 30 minutes of each other.

The attack shows why defenders watch the use of legitimate tools. An operator login that comes from an unusual place, or breaker commands at an unusual time, can be a sign of an intruder, even when no malware is involved.

The attackers also tried to slow recovery. According to the alert and E-ISAC/SANS analysis, they used the destructive tool KillDisk, corrupted the firmware of serial-to-Ethernet devices, scheduled disconnections of backup power supplies, and in one case flooded a call centre. Operators were forced to switch to manual operation. Sources: [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-alerts/ir-alert-h-16-056-01) and [E-ISAC/SANS](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/3891751/SANS-and-Electricity-Information-Sharing-and.pdf).
