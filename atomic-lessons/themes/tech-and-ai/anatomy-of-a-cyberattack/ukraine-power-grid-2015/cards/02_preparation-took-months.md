The outage was not the first sign of trouble. The CISA alert says the BlackEnergy malware was delivered by spear phishing emails with Office attachments, and that the attackers used legitimate credentials. The alert also says the role of BlackEnergy in the outages was unknown, so it should not be described as the cause.

The outage actions themselves used remote access to existing control tools. This is a common pattern: attackers prefer legitimate tools because they blend in with normal work. It means defenders have to watch who uses which tool and when, and not only look for unknown malware.
