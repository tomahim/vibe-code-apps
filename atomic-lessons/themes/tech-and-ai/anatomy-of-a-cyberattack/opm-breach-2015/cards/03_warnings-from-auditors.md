The committee found that OPM leadership failed to heed repeated recommendations from its own Inspector General, failed to respond sufficiently to the growing threat of sophisticated cyber attacks, and failed to prioritise resources for cybersecurity. Its recommendations included moving toward zero trust, modernising legacy information technology, reducing the use of Social Security numbers, and empowering and holding accountable agency chief information officers.

This is a classic pattern: a known list of weaknesses exists, but fixing them competes with other priorities and budgets. The recommendation to modernise legacy systems reflects that old systems are hard to monitor and protect with modern tools, so organisations postpone hard work. A risk list without owners and deadlines does not protect anyone. Source: [House Oversight Committee release on its OPM report](https://oversight.house.gov/release/committee-releases-year-long-investigative-report-opm-data-breaches/).
