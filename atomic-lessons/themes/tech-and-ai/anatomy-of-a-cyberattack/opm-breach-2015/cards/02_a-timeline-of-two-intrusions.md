The House Oversight Committee's report of September 2016 described a longer story than the 2015 headlines. The committee found that the data breach was preventable, and that breaches of OPM in 2014 were likely connected to, and possibly coordinated with, the 2015 breach. A Congressional Research Service brief says the 2014 breach reportedly targeted files on people who had applied for top-secret clearances.

The CRS brief also reports that the hackers reportedly used compromised credentials assigned to an employee of KeyPoint Government Solutions, a federal background-check contractor working on OPM systems. Treat that as a reported detail. The committee's own summary focuses on leadership and security failures rather than on a single technical step.
