Symantec counted infections from more than 40,000 external IP addresses in over 155 countries, and reported in 2010 that about 60 percent of infected computers worldwide were in Iran. Its dossier says the threat targets a specific industrial control system likely in Iran. Researchers and journalists widely link it to the uranium enrichment plant at Natanz, but that is an inference, not an official statement. Beyond that, exact numbers and delays are estimates from analysts and journalists, not figures confirmed by a neutral audit.

The worm also spread far beyond its target. Because it travelled through USB drives and networks, it infected many computers in other countries, although it did little on machines without the specific setup. This showed the risk of releasing self-propagating code: it cannot be fully contained, and others can study and copy the techniques once it is public.
