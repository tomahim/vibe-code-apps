The defenders' assumptions failed. The facility relied on isolation, but removable media provided a route across it. Certificates that should have proved authenticity were stolen. The monitoring screens trusted the same controller data the malware controlled.

There was also a human factor. Every strong control, such as limits on USB drives or signed code checks, needs staff to follow it under daily pressure. Attackers look for the gap between the policy on paper and the habits in practice.

A related point is that attackers study the target's real processes. Strong controls need regular testing, such as exercises that try to bring a removable drive across the boundary, because a rule that is never tested may not work on the day it matters.
