Stuxnet spread mainly through removable USB drives and local networks, which let it cross into facilities that had no direct internet connection. Symantec's analysis found that it used several previously unknown Windows vulnerabilities, called zero-days, as well as stolen digital certificates from two Taiwanese hardware companies, Realtek and JMicron, to make its drivers look trusted.

That combination was expensive and rare. Zero-days are valuable, and signing certificates are normally closely guarded. It suggested a well-resourced team. Once on a machine, the worm checked whether it had found the software used to program the target Siemens controllers. If it had not, the dossier says it did little.
