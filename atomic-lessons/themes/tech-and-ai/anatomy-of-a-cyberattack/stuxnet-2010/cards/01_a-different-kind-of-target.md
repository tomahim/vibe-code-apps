Most malware targets data or money. Stuxnet targeted physical machinery. In June 2010 a Belarusian security firm, VirusBlokAda, reported an unusual Windows worm, and analysts at Symantec and others then spent months studying it. Their conclusion was that the code looked for one very specific industrial setup, built around Siemens control systems.

Industrial control systems run factories, power plants and pipelines. Programmable logic controllers, or PLCs, are small computers that read sensors and switch motors and valves. Such systems were often kept off the internet, which many operators believed was enough protection. Stuxnet showed that an "air gap" is a policy, not a force field, because people carry files and devices across it.
