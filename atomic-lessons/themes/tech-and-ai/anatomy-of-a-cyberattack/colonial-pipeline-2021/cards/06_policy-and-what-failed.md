CISA and the FBI published guidance for preventing and responding to DarkSide ransomware, which is the main public reference for defenders. The sources cited here do not cover every policy response, so check the latest official guidance for current requirements.

What failed was basic. A legacy remote access profile that should not have been in use let the attackers in, and Reuters reported that it lacked multifactor authentication. The ransomware then disrupted business systems that operations depended on. Attribution to DarkSide comes from CISA and the FBI, and individual affiliates were not named in these sources. Sources: [CISA Alert AA21-131A on DarkSide ransomware](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa21-131a), [Blount's Senate testimony](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/imo/media/doc/Testimony-Blount-2021-06-08.pdf), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/colonial-pipeline-ceo-tells-senate-cyber-defenses-were-compromised-ahead-hack-2021-06-08/) and [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/department-justice-seizes-23-million-cryptocurrency-paid-ransomware-extortionists-darkside).
