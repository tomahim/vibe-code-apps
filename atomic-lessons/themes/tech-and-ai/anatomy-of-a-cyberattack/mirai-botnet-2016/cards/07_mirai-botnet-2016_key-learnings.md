1. Ship secure by default. Unique passwords, closed services and updateable software prevent mass infections better than asking each user to fix them.

2. Small devices can cause large harm. Many weak devices together can produce attack traffic that disrupts major services.

3. Avoid single points of failure. Using several DNS providers and having capacity to absorb floods reduces the damage of a single attack.
The guilty pleas concern Mirai and click fraud botnets, and the sources cited here do not tie them to the Dyn attack.
