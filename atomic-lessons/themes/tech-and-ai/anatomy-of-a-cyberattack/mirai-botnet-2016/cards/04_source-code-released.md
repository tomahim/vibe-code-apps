On 30 September 2016 the Mirai source code was released online, according to the USENIX paper, and other operators could then run their own botnets with it.

On 13 December 2017 the US Department of Justice announced that Paras Jha, Josiah White and Dalton Norman had pleaded guilty on 8 December to charges tied to the Mirai and click fraud botnets. The sources cited here do not connect those cases to the Dyn attack, so do not assume they were the Dyn attackers.

Public release of attack code is a recurring pattern in security history. It lowers the skill needed for copycats, which is why defenders treat such a release as a reason to patch and harden quickly instead of waiting to see who uses it.
