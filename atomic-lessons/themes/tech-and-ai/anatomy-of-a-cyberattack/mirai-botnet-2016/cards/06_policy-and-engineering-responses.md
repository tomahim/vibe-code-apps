The USENIX paper discusses technical interventions, such as removing default credentials, patching, and cooperation between device makers, network operators and researchers, and notes that no single fix covers all devices.

Engineers can take concrete steps today: generate a unique credential for each device at manufacture, close services that are not needed, provide a way to update the software, and document how long updates will be supplied. These steps are simple, and they address the root cause that Mirai used.

For engineers and for the companies that employ them, the lesson is secure by default. A device should arrive with a unique password, minimal open services and a way to update. Source: [Antonakakis et al., Understanding the Mirai Botnet (USENIX Security 2017)](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-antonakakis.pdf).
