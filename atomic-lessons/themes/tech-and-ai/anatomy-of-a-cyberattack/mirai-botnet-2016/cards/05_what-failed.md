The failure was first at the manufacturer and second at the buyer. Devices shipped with predictable passwords and open services, and many received no updates. Owners had no easy way to notice that a camera or router was part of an attack, since it kept working. 

There was also a concentration risk. Many sites depended on one DNS provider, so one successful attack on it made many sites hard to reach even though they were not the target.

Responsibility is shared. Manufacturers decide the defaults, owners decide whether to change them, and network operators can sometimes notice unusual traffic. A weak point at any of these steps can be enough for an attacker to build a large botnet from small devices.
