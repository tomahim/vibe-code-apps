The breach did not break the mathematics of encryption. It broke the operational security around the CA. Fox-IT reported that the most critical servers contained malicious software that can normally be detected by antivirus, that the separation of critical components was not functioning or not in place, and that all CA servers were members of one Windows domain. The attacker had obtained domain administrator rights, so the attacker could reach the systems that sign certificates.

A fraudulent certificate is powerful only when combined with a way to intercept traffic, which is called a man-in-the-middle position. Fox-IT counted around 300,000 unique IP addresses that checked the status of the rogue Google certificate, with more than 99 percent of them in Iran. It warned that email and login cookies of those users could have been intercepted. In that kind of attack, the victim sees a normal padlock while a third party reads or alters the traffic.
