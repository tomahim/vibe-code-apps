Several controls failed together. Fox-IT concluded that the network setup and procedures at DigiNotar were not sufficiently secure to prevent this kind of attack. Critical servers carried malware that standard antivirus could detect, components were not separated, and all CA servers shared one Windows domain, so one stolen administrator position reached all of them. The report adds that the servers, although physically very securely placed, were accessible over the network.

The wider system also failed, since any CA could issue a certificate for any domain and domain owners had little visibility. Fox-IT did not name the attackers. It found hacker tools and custom scripts, and said the pattern of victims suggested a goal of intercepting communication in Iran. That is an inference about purpose, not proof of identity.
