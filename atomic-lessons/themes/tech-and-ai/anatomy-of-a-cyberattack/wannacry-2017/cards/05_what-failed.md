The failures were mostly in basic security. Patches were available but not installed. Old software stayed in use, and the Department of Health had been warned a year earlier without giving a formal written response.

The cost of fixes is real, because shutting systems down for patching can affect care. These constraints must be planned for in advance, with inventories, isolated networks for hard-to-patch devices and clear authority to make decisions in an emergency.

Basic security is not simple at scale. An inventory of devices, an agreed owner for patching, tested backups and a plan for working without IT are all slow, unglamorous tasks, yet they decide how bad a worm outbreak becomes.
