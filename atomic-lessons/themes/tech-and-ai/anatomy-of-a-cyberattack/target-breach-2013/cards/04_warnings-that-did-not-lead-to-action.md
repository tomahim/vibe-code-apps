Target had invested in security, including a monitoring product from FireEye and a security operations team. The Senate report, citing a Bloomberg Businessweek report, says the attackers installed their malware on 30 November and that this triggered alerts from the FireEye system. According to those reports, Target's security team neither reacted to the alerts nor allowed the software to delete the malware automatically. The report concludes that Target appears to have failed to respond to multiple warnings.

The case shows that detection alone is not defence. A tool that raises an alert helps only when someone has the authority, time and procedure to act on it. These are findings of committee staff based on public reports, not a court ruling.
