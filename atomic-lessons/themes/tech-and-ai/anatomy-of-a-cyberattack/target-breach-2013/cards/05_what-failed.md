The failures were mostly about process, not novelty. Vendor access was reportedly too broad, sensitive parts of the network were not well isolated, alerts were not acted upon, and card data was exposed in memory on terminals. Each of these steps has a known countermeasure.

The cost was large. Target's chief executive left in May 2014, according to a Target board statement. In May 2017, Target agreed to pay $18.5 million to settle claims from 47 US states and the District of Columbia.

The practical value of the report is that it turns a long incident into a list of checkpoints. A team can compare its own network against each stage and ask whether it would notice, block or at least log the activity at that point.
