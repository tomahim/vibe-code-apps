After the initial access, the attackers moved from less sensitive areas of the network to areas storing consumer data. The Senate staff report concluded that this suggests Target failed to properly isolate its most sensitive network assets.

The criminals then installed memory-scraping malware on point-of-sale terminals. Card data exists briefly in memory in readable form when a payment is processed, even if it is encrypted elsewhere. According to the report, the stolen data was sent out over FTP to external servers, at least one of them in Russia, and the report names application whitelisting, which lets only approved programs run, as a defence that could have helped. Sending data out is called the exfiltration stage, and it is another point where unusual traffic could have been noticed.
