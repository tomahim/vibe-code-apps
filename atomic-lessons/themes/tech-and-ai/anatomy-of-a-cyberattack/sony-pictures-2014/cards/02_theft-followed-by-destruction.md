title: "Theft followed by destruction"

The FBI determined that the intrusion consisted of the deployment of destructive malware and the theft of proprietary information as well as employees' personally identifiable information and confidential communications. In plain terms, the attackers both took data and ran software that made Sony's computers inoperable.

The two steps served different goals. Theft gave the attackers material to pressure the company, and the destructive malware made recovery costly and visible. For a defender, the time between entry and destruction is an opportunity: large transfers of data out of a network, or a program that disables many machines at once, are unusual events that monitoring can be designed to catch. These are lessons drawn from the case, since the FBI statement does not describe Sony's internal systems.
