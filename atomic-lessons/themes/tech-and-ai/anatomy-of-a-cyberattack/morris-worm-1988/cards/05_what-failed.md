Several things failed together. Software shipped with a risky debug option enabled, a network service accepted more data than its buffer could hold, and passwords were easy to guess. Machines trusted each other without checking much, so one compromised host exposed its neighbours. There was no agreed way to alert the whole community or to coordinate a response.

The author's design also failed on its own terms. The FBI says that shortly after the attack the programmer despaired that it had spiraled out of control and asked a friend to relay an anonymous message with advice on removing it, but the message reached few people in time because the network was so damaged. A technical mistake and a communication breakdown combined into a much larger incident than any single flaw.
