At about 8:30 p.m. on 2 November 1988 a program was released from a computer at MIT. Within 24 hours it had hit an estimated 6,000 of the roughly 60,000 connected computers, according to the FBI. Systems at universities, NASA and national laboratories slowed to a crawl, and electronic mail was delayed for days.

Administrators were not even sure what they were facing at first. A student at Berkeley wrote that the network was under attack, and teams at several universities began taking the program apart to understand it. Some institutions wiped their machines, while others disconnected from the network for as long as a week to stop reinfection.
