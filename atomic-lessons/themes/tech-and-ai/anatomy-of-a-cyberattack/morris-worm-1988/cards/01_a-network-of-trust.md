In November 1988 the internet was a small network of universities, research labs and military sites, roughly 60,000 computers connected through a few shared protocols. It was built by people who mostly knew each other, so machines often trusted one another by default. Nobody had designed it with an attacker in mind, and the World Wide Web did not exist yet.

That environment explains why the incident hit so hard. Programs such as the mail system and the "finger" directory service ran with wide access on Unix machines, and many accounts used short, guessable passwords. The worm did not need genius-level tricks. It needed a network where one weak machine could be used to reach the next one, and that is exactly what it found.
