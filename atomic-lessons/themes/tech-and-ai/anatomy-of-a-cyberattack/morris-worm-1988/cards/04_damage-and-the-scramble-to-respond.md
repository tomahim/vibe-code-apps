The worm did not destroy data, yet it caused real harm. Military and university work stalled, and the FBI notes that damage estimates started at about $100,000 and rose into the millions. Some institutions wiped their systems, and others disconnected from the network for as long as a week, according to the FBI.

Spafford's account says that researchers at Berkeley developed an interim set of steps to halt the spread, including a preliminary patch to the mail program, and that the suggestions were published on mailing lists and Usenet. The response relied on experts sharing advice and not on any organised emergency system, and the FBI notes that a standing emergency response team was created only days later. That gap is the lesson: a fix is worth little if there is no agreed way to share it quickly.
