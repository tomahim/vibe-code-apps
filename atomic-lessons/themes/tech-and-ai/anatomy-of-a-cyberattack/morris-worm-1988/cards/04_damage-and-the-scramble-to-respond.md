The worm did not destroy data, yet it caused real harm. Military and university work stalled, and the FBI notes that damage estimates started at about $100,000 and rose into the millions. The bigger cost was uncertainty: nobody knew who to call, who was in charge, or how to share a fix when the network itself was clogged.

Security-minded engineers at Berkeley, MIT and elsewhere worked through the night, analysed the code and published patches and advice. Communication was hard because email, the usual channel, was slow or down. People used phones and ad hoc mailing lists. The response worked, but it worked through personal networks rather than any organised system, which showed a gap that the field had not yet filled.
