1. Trust by default is a weakness. When machines, accounts or services trust each other without checks, one compromise can spread across the whole network.

2. Safety measures can be the failure. The worm's reinfection rule let copies pile up until machines stalled, which exposed it. Systems that copy themselves need strict limits.

3. Response needs a plan before the crisis. The community fixed the problem through individual effort. The lasting defence was creating a standing team to coordinate alerts, which is why incident response centres exist today.
This is a history lesson about a 1988 incident. The specific flaws were fixed long ago, and the lessons apply to any connected system.
