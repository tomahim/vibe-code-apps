Central banks keep accounts at other banks to settle international payments. Bangladesh Bank held a US dollar account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. Payments are sent as authenticated messages over SWIFT, a messaging network used by banks worldwide. SWIFT does not move money itself. It carries instructions that banks then execute.

On 4 and 5 February 2016, which was the start of a weekend in Bangladesh, attackers sent nearly three dozen payment requests to the New York Fed, attempting to transfer $951 million, according to BAE Systems and Reuters. Reporting also notes that Friday is the Bangladeshi weekend, which delayed the discovery.
