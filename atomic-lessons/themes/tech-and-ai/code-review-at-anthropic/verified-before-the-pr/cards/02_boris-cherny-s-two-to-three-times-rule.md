title: Boris Cherny's two-to-three-times rule

Boris Cherny, who created Claude Code, has called giving Claude a way to verify its own work probably the most important thing for getting good results out of it. He estimates that verification makes the final result two to three times better.

His point is that verification looks different in every domain. It might be as simple as running a bash command and reading what comes back, running a test suite, or testing the app in a browser or a phone simulator. What matters is that the agent closes a loop against reality instead of grading its own homework. When you work with coding agents yourself, the practical question to ask after every task is the same one: what can this agent run that would prove the change works, and did it show me that output?
