title: The cost of a needless interruption

Pulling a senior engineer out of deep work to catch a routine mistake costs far more than an automated review. The interruption tax is real: context switches destroy hours, not minutes, and the routine mistake was findable by a machine anyway.

So the question is never whether humans review, but where their review buys something machines cannot provide. The team lists five responsibilities people still carry: deciding whether to build something at all, reviewing risky code, reviewing architecture that sets a pattern, reading the evidence behind a change, and owning the outcome. The rest of this lesson walks through each one. Notice that none of them is “read every line.” The human contribution has moved up the stack, from inspection to judgment.
