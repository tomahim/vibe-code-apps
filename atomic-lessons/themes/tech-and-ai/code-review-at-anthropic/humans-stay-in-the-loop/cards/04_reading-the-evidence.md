The fourth human job is verifying the verification. Someone reads the evidence behind a change: the test output, the command that ran and what it returned, a screenshot, the reviewer's findings. The article's test for whether you are doing this job is sharp: if you cannot say what the checks would miss, ask for a real review rather than a quick approval.

This is the seatbelt on the whole automated system. Every layer below produces claims: tests passed, the reviewer found nothing blocking, security is green. Those claims are only as good as the checks themselves, and checks have blind spots by construction. A human who can articulate what the current checks would miss is doing the one review no layer can do for itself. A human who cannot is rubber-stamping, and the process would be more honest without them.
