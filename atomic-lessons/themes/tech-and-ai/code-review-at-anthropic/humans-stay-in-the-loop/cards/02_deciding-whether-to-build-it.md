The first human job is direction: taste and scope, the question of whether a change should exist. One team that declared its review bottleneck solved still kept human review for changes from outside the team, so that someone asks “should we even do this?”

Agents make starting work nearly free, which makes this judgment more valuable, not less. When code is cheap, the expensive mistakes are the features that should never have been built: the maintenance they demand, the complexity they add, the direction they commit you to. No automated reviewer reads a diff and knows your product strategy or your team's appetite for supporting a clever idea forever. That knowledge lives in people, and the review process reserves a place for it, especially for changes that arrive from outside the team that will own the consequences.
