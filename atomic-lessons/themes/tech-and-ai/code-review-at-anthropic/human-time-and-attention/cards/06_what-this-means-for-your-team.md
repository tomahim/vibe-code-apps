The transferable idea is not any single tool but the accounting. Start by deciding what your scarce resource actually is. For most teams adopting coding agents, it becomes senior attention: the people who can judge architecture, spot subtle breakage, and say no to a change that should not exist.

Then instrument both sides of the cost. Count how much of that attention each change consumes today, and count what escapes into incidents. Only then does automation have a target: move the routine 80 percent of checks off the humans, watch the guardrail metrics, and tighten or loosen based on what they say. Teams that skip the measurement step end up either over-reviewing trivial agent output or under-reviewing the risky changes buried inside it.
