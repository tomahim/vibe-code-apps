Review's second job, spreading codebase understanding, survives the machines. Nobody keeps a complete mental model of Claude Code, and nobody needs to: engineers ask Claude for an explainer of exactly the slice they are about to change, or a small artifact that teaches it their way.

An explainer for the module you are about to change teaches a lot; reading trivial diffs line by line teaches almost nothing. Open channels keep ambient awareness alive without mandatory diff reading.

Adoption is a checklist you can start this week: verification and instructional files before the pull request, CI plus AI and security review with risk-matched depth and round caps on it, written risk tiers with human-only approvals for sensitive paths, and merge queue, flags, staged releases, incident evals, and measured human time after it. Approving stays a human call in the product, while internal low-risk merges keep pushing that line.
