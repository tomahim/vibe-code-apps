title: When more prompt is not the answer
archived: false
---


The natural instinct when an agent misbehaves is to add another paragraph to the prompt. Sometimes that is right. But instructions pile up until the prompt is a sprawling wish list that dilutes attention, contradicts itself and still misses the real fix. An agent that struggles with one specialized document workflow probably needs a reusable skill, not a permanently lengthened prompt read on every task.

There is also a subtle economy to always-on text. Every sentence there is paid for on every call, in attention and in context budget that could hold the actual task. Harness engineering is partly the discipline of moving procedures out of the prompt and into the cheapest component that will reliably trigger: skill, script, subagent or hook. The result is a harness that stays lean where it is always read, and deep where depth is actually needed. [Further reading: Appendix D of the [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224) lists the component files and their activation rules.]
