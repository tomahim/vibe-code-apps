It helps to separate an AI agent into two layers. The first is the model, the neural network that supplies the intelligence: reading, reasoning, writing. The second is everything wrapped around it that turns that intelligence into a working colleague: the instructions it follows, the tools it may call, the memory it keeps, the procedures it knows, and the runtime that executes its choices. The Harness² researchers compress this into a short formula: Agent = Model + Harness.

The word "harness" is borrowed from the straps and fittings around a horse: the horse supplies the power, but the harness directs it into useful work. A strong model with a poor harness behaves like a brilliant employee locked in a messy office with no filing system and vague instructions. A modest model with a well-designed harness can outperform it. The paper's experiments confirm this: a weaker model with an improved harness beat a stronger model stuck with its original one.
