title: Organizational failures, not intellectual ones
archived: false
---


Watch a failing agent closely and a pattern appears. It reads too much irrelevant material and drowns its own context window. It picks the wrong tool, or calls the right tool with the wrong arguments. It does its steps in an unhelpful order, burns its step budget early, or forgets a constraint it discovered twenty minutes ago. Imagine a research assistant asked to summarize a policy change: it has search, a document reader and a note file, yet it quotes an outdated draft because nothing tells it to check dates first.

The tempting response is to reach for a bigger model. Sometimes that helps. But the Harness² paper treats these as failures of organization, not cognition. If the agent's instructions never say "check the publication date before relying on a source," no amount of extra reasoning power guarantees it will do so. The leverage sits in how the work is organized, and that organization lives in the harness, not in the model's weights.
