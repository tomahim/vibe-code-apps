title: The wastefulness of starting over
archived: false
---


Imagine a consultant who solves a client's problem brilliantly, then walks out the door leaving no notes. The next client with a similar problem gets the same long, expensive discovery process from scratch. That is what per-task harness refinement looks like without memory: every task pays the full cost of diagnosing weaknesses, testing edits and learning what works, and none of it compounds.

Harness² calls the fix harnessing-level recursion. The idea is that the refinement process itself generates valuable experience: how a weakness was diagnosed, which changes were tried, and what their execution revealed. Throwing that away is the waste. The system's second feedback loop exists to capture it, so that improving harnesses is itself a skill that improves. This is what separates Harness² from methods that refine each task in isolation or blindly carry a tailored harness forward.
