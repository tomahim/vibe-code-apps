The library has a strict door policy: lessons are retained only when supported by execution contrasts. Selecting an output alone does not establish that a lesson will transfer. If the evidence is insufficient, reflection adds no new entry at all. This guardrail matters because the library's whole value is trust; a library polluted with plausible-sounding but unverified lessons would steadily mislead every future task in the domain.

The Medium article names the corresponding failure mode experience contamination: a lesson inferred from limited evidence gets generalized too broadly to future tasks. The design mitigations follow from the door policy. Lessons record their applicable situation, so later tasks can judge relevance. Workspace facts stay anchored as facts about a specific task. And procedure is written for the domain, so reuse pulls general method rather than one task's specifics.
