The framework's answer-free selection is a research design, not a guarantee. When an application needs high reliability, the sensible move is to add checks that do not come from the same model: external validators where they exist, real test results where tests exist, and human review for consequential changes. These do not replace the contrast. They cover what the contrast cannot see.

A useful way to apply this is to place a review step between a composed candidate harness and its adoption: automated checks, regression tests on representative tasks, a safety review, and a human approval for changes that touch anything hard to undo, such as payments, data deletion or the agent's own rules. Self-improvement then looks less like an unsupervised agent rewriting itself and more like a disciplined release process that happens to be proposed by a machine. This is general engineering practice for this lesson, beyond what the paper itself tested.
