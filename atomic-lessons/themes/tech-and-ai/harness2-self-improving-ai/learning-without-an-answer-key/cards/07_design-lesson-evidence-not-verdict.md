title: 'Design lesson: evidence, not verdict'

The mindset to take away is epistemic hygiene: keep separate what you know from what you infer. A trajectory shows the agent never checked its output; that is knowledge. That the new harness "solves" the task; that is inference. Harness²'s design keeps the two apart: composition decisions cite observations, experience-library lessons are retained only when supported by execution contrasts, and the adopted star explicitly does not claim optimality.

For your own projects, the rule generalizes. Whenever there is no answer key, build the feedback loop on observable procedure: did the agent check its work, consult the right sources, respect the budget, finish the deliverable? Grade the method, not the outcome, and reserve outcome judgment for the rare moments a real referee exists.
