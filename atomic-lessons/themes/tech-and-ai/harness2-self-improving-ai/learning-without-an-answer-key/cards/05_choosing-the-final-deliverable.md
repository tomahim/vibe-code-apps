When the recursion budget k is greater than one, refinement produces several candidate deliverables, and one must be returned. Harness² uses an agentic selector with three careful constraints. It compares anonymized deliverables against the task request and source materials, so it cannot favor a candidate because of which harness produced it. It must return an existing output; it may not synthesize a new, blended answer. And it adds no harness revision of its own.

The paper marks the chosen harness with a star but insists the star means adopted, not verified optimal. That honesty is important: selection is another judgment made without an answer key, subject to the same biases as everything else. At k=1, both recursion forms reduce to a single contrastive step and selection is bypassed entirely, which is one more reason the single-step configuration is the cleanest to reason about.
