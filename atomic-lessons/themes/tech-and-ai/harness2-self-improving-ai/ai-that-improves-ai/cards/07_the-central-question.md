The paper states its research question in one sentence: can recursive self-improvement improve both the current harness and how future harnesses are improved? Everything in Harness² follows from answering yes with a concrete mechanism. The execution harness is the system being improved; the improvement harness is the mechanism that improves it. During a task, the improver stays fixed; after the task, the improver itself gets updated with what the refinement taught.

Hold onto that two-loop picture, because the next lessons walk through it piece by piece. First the inner loop: the Propose-Probe-Compose step that improves the harness for the task at hand without any answer key. Then the two ways to scale it, parallel and sequential. Then the outer loop: the experience library that makes the whole system better at improving itself over time. [Further reading: the [paper's introduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224) lays out this two-challenge framing.]
