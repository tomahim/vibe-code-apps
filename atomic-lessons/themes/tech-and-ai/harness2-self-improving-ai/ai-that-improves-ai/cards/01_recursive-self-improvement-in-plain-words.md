title: Recursive self-improvement in plain words

Recursive self-improvement, RSI for short, means a system that participates in its own development. The paper's definition: a system turns execution outcomes and internal reflections into persistent changes to itself, and those changes also shape how later improvements are generated, evaluated and retained. In plain words: the system does not just get better at the task, it gets better at getting better.

The word "recursive" is the key. A one-shot improvement is not RSI. Recursion means the improvement process itself is affected by previous improvements, so progress can compound instead of resetting every time. Think of a new employee who not only learns to do the job, but also keeps refining their own onboarding notes, so each month they learn faster than the month before. The ambition of RSI research is to make progress cumulative in exactly that sense.
