Wolfram argues that some processes require following their steps to know the result. He uses this idea to discuss limits of pattern-based shortcuts.

Some processes have no general shortcut that predicts their result without effectively following the computation. Wolfram calls this computational irreducibility and uses it to argue against assuming that pattern recognition can replace all explicit calculation. Think of a program whose future state depends on many earlier updates: seeing several past runs may suggest patterns but not settle every new case. This is Wolfram's interpretive framework, not a proved diagnosis of every ChatGPT error. The practical point is to distinguish a forecast from an executed computation.
