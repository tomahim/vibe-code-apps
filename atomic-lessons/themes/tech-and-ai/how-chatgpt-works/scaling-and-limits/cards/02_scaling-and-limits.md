title: "Memorizing versus calculating"

Remembering examples is different from carrying out a new sequence of exact steps. A fluent answer can hide this distinction.

If you have seen the answers to several multiplication problems, you may remember them without knowing a general multiplication procedure. A new problem with unfamiliar numbers exposes the difference. A language model may learn many patterns around arithmetic and still give an incorrect product if it relies on a plausible continuation. The essay uses this contrast to separate language-like behavior from exact computation. It is possible for a model to represent useful algorithms, but a fluent sentence alone does not show that the required steps were carried out correctly.
