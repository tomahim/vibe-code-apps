Architecture defines the broad arrangement of computation before training begins. The same broad design can sometimes work across different tasks.

Before any examples are shown, builders decide whether a network will use an image-focused design, a transformer for sequences, or another architecture. That choice fixes broad paths along which information can flow. Training then fills in many numerical weights inside the design. An architecture that cannot represent a needed relationship may keep failing even after more practice. Conversely, a flexible architecture can be useful for several tasks. The essay emphasizes that these choices come from accumulated experimentation as much as from a complete theory of why they work.
