title: "Similarity has limits"

One word may have several meanings, and a single fixed vector cannot fully express every use. Contextual representations help distinguish uses.

Consider the sentence “The crane lifted the beam” and then “The crane stood by the marsh.” A fixed representation for the printed word “crane” cannot by itself say whether it refers to machinery or a bird. Contextual representations can incorporate the neighboring words and make the two uses different. A simple map of isolated word vectors remains a helpful teaching picture, but it leaves out this context dependence. Always ask what was embedded: a vocabulary item, a token in a sentence, or an entire passage.
