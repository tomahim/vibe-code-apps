**1. Embeddings make text usable as numbers.** A token can be mapped to a learned vector rather than treated only as printed letters or an arbitrary ID. Networks can compare and transform those vectors as part of their prediction task.

**2. Similarity comes from use and context.** “Alligator” and “crocodile” may appear in similar settings, while “turnip” usually does not. Those patterns can put related representations near each other without anyone writing a formal dictionary definition for every word.

**3. Meaning is not one fixed point.** “Crane” can mean a bird or a machine, depending on surrounding words. Contextual representations help separate those uses, while simple two-dimensional maps remain illustrations of a richer space.
