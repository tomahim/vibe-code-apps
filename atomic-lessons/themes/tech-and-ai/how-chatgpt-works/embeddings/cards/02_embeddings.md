title: "Nearby words, nearby vectors"

Words used in similar contexts can receive related representations. Nearness is useful but does not give a complete definition of meaning.

The words “alligator” and “crocodile” can occur in similar sentences about rivers, scales, and wildlife. A learning task may therefore place their representations near each other. “Turnip” tends to appear in different contexts, so it may be farther away. This is an illustration from the essay, not a promise that every semantic relationship has a simple distance. Words can be related in several ways at once: “bank” and “river” share a setting, while “bank” and “credit” share another meaning.
