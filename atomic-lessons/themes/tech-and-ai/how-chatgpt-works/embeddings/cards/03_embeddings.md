The surrounding words provide clues about how a word is used. Similar surroundings can help a model learn useful relationships.

Suppose a model sees “The ___ swam across the river” in many examples. The words that fit the blank help reveal which terms behave similarly in context. This does not require a person to label every word with a formal definition. The learning task supplies indirect evidence through surrounding words. That evidence is powerful but imperfect: a fictional story or an erroneous webpage can place words in unusual contexts. Embeddings reflect patterns in the text used to learn them, including its gaps and biases.
