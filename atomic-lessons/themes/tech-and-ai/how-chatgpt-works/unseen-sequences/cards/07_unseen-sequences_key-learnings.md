**1. Language creates new combinations.** A phrase such as “a green bicycle under a purple moon” can be understandable even if no dataset contains it verbatim. Exact phrase lookup cannot cover that open-ended space.

**2. A model estimates unseen continuations.** It shares learned patterns across examples, so it can assign probabilities after a novel prompt. Those numbers are computed estimates, not counts for the complete new phrase.

**3. Plausible text still needs checking.** Generalization can produce a useful sentence about an invented product or scene. When the sentence claims something about an actual product or place, evidence beyond the model's fluency is needed.
