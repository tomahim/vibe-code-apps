title: "Generalization in plain language"

---
Generalization means using learned patterns on a new case. It can be useful, but a plausible prediction can still be wrong.

A child who learns that “the red kite flew” is grammatical can also understand “the blue kite flew” without seeing that sentence before. A language model's generalization is different from a child's understanding, but the analogy illustrates reuse of patterns across examples. It can extend a familiar structure to a new noun or adjective. That ability is valuable for creative text and routine explanation. It does not guarantee success on every new context, especially when the new case demands a precise calculation or obscure fact.
