title: "One word, several meanings"

---
A word such as crane can name a bird or a machine. Surrounding context can help separate those uses.

In “The crane lifted the steel beam,” nearby words favor the machine sense; in “The crane spread its wings,” they favor the bird. Plotting sentence-level or contextual representations can reveal those uses as separate regions even though the printed word is identical. This is a concrete reason context matters for semantic analysis. It also warns against asking what a single word “means to the model” without saying which context or representation is being examined. Multiple senses are ordinary in human language, not an exceptional flaw.
