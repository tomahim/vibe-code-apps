title: "Analogies as directions"

Some relationships between words appear as geometric patterns in embeddings. The patterns are suggestive rather than universal laws.

Some embeddings make relationships look like directions: a movement from one term to another resembles a movement between a different related pair. This can be a striking visualization of learned regularities. Yet not every analogy forms a clean parallel, especially after a low-dimensional projection. If a chart shows a suggestive line, test many examples before announcing a general law. Wolfram uses such plots to ask whether language has an underlying geometry that might be described more simply, not to establish that one diagram explains all reasoning.
