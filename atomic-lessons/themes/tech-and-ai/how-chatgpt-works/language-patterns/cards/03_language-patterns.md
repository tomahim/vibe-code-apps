The letter after q in English is very often u. This shows why the previous character changes the next character's probability.

Seeing q makes u much more likely in ordinary English than it is after most other letters. A table of single-letter frequencies cannot express that conditional relationship, but a table of letter pairs can. The lesson is broader than this one spelling rule: the next symbol's likelihood depends on recent context. That dependence is visible in ordinary words, where “th” is common and many other pairs are rare. The analogy prepares us for word prediction, though a modern language model uses much richer context than one preceding letter.
