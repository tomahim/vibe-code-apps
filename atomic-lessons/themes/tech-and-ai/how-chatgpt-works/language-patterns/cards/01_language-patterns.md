title: "Count letters in a sample"

A text sample lets us estimate how often each letter appears. A larger and more representative sample usually gives more stable estimates.

Take two short articles, one about cats and one about dogs, and count each letter. The counts will be similar in broad strokes, but the topic words change the details: “dog” increases the letter o in the second sample. With more varied text, the estimates usually become more stable. This simple exercise shows what a probability estimate needs: observations and a defined unit to count. It also shows why a small or biased sample should not be mistaken for the whole language.
