title: "How a paragraph emerges"

---
A paragraph is the result of many successive token choices. Local predictions can produce a coherent-looking whole, although coherence alone does not prove accuracy.

Take a short answer of fifty tokens. The model has run its next-token calculation about fifty times, each time reading the available context and adding one selection. Some choices establish a subject, others complete grammar, and later choices may refer back to earlier ones. The finished answer can look like a unified thought because learned language patterns constrain each step. Yet a smooth paragraph can still contain a wrong date or invented citation. The mechanism explains how prose is formed; it does not verify that every sentence is true.
