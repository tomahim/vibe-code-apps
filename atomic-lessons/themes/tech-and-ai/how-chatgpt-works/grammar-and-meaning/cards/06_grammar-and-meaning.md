title: "Fluent is not necessarily true"
archived: false
---
Correct-looking grammar and coherent wording can coexist with factual error. Checking the claim requires evidence beyond its style.

An answer can maintain subject-verb agreement, use elegant transitions, and still invent an event. The model's command of syntax makes the false claim easier to read, which can increase misplaced trust. Meaning also differs from truth: “A dragon lives under this bridge” is intelligible even if it describes fiction. When a response asserts a real-world fact, check the source or calculation behind it. The lesson's structural insight is valuable, but the practical habit is to evaluate what the sentence says, not only how smoothly it says it.
