Human language contains recurring arrangements of words and phrases. Training text gives a model many examples of these regularities.

A text collection contains repeated patterns such as subjects before verbs in many English statements and phrases nested inside larger phrases. A language model can learn these regularities because they help predict later tokens. It need not be given a complete grammar textbook to produce many well-formed sentences. Still, a statistical pattern is not an explicit proof of grammatical correctness in every edge case. The essay treats language structure as one reason the prediction task is more manageable than counting every possible sentence separately.
