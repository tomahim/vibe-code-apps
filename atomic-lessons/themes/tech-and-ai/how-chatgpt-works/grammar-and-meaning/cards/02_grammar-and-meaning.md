title: "Syntax versus meaning"
archived: false
---
A sentence can follow grammar rules yet be nonsensical or false. Syntactic fit is only one constraint on a useful answer.

“The blue idea ate breakfast” follows a familiar sentence pattern, but its ordinary meaning is strange. “Paris is the capital of Germany” is grammatically smooth and meaningful as a claim, yet factually false. These examples separate three checks: does the word order fit, does the sentence express a sensible idea, and is that idea true? A model may do well at the first while failing the third. This is why reading an answer for fluency cannot replace checking its content when the stakes are high.
