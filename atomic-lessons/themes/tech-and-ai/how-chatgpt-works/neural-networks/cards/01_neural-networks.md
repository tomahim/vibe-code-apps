An artificial neuron combines several numerical inputs into one output. It is a simplified mathematical component, not a biological brain cell.

A toy artificial neuron might receive two numbers, multiply each by a weight, add the results and a bias, then apply an activation function. If the inputs represented brightness at two pixels, changing the weights would change which brightness patterns produce a strong output. Real networks have many such operations arranged together. The biological metaphor is useful for picturing connected units, but the artificial component is a mathematical calculation. Understanding that simple calculation makes the scale of a language model less mysterious, even though the full system remains complex.
