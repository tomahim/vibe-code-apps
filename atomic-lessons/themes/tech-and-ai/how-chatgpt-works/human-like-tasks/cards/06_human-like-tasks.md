For a human-like task, a useful test is whether outputs usually match what people would say. Such agreement is empirical, not a mathematical guarantee.

Suppose a digit model gets 98 of 100 ordinary handwritten examples right. That is useful evidence, though we would still test different handwriting styles and poor scans. We cannot prove it will agree with people on every possible image without a complete theory of human visual judgment. The essay's point is empirical: networks can often learn functions whose behavior resembles human classification on practical cases. Language generation raises a larger version of the same question because its outputs have many more possible forms.
