Some images do not have an obvious label even for people. Agreement with human judgments matters more than pretending every boundary is perfectly defined.

Imagine a hastily written symbol that could be a 1 or a 7. Two careful readers may disagree, so there is no simple pixel rule that settles the intended label. A model may consistently choose one based on patterns in its training examples. We can ask whether that choice usually agrees with people, and where it fails. This keeps evaluation honest: some errors reflect a model weakness, while others reveal that the task itself has fuzzy boundaries. Similar ambiguity appears when judging whether generated text is “meaningful.”
