The core mechanism is repeated next-token prediction by a trained neural network. Large-scale learning and later tuning make its outputs more useful.

The central account begins with lots of human-created text and a network trained to predict what token comes next. Repeating that prediction from a prompt can produce coherent responses. Architecture, scale, data, and later assistant-focused training help explain why the result is useful. The surprising part, emphasized by Wolfram, is how far this seemingly local objective reaches into human-like writing. His further ideas about semantic grammar and computational irreducibility offer interpretations and research questions. Keep the observed mechanism separate from those broader explanations.
