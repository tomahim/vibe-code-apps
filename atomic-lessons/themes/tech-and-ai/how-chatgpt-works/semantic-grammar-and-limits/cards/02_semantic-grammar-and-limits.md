title: "Plausible versus factual"

A statement can sound meaningful and still be false in the world. A language model's text alone cannot settle that distinction.

“I bought two pounds of apples” is a plausible sentence, but a model cannot infer from its wording alone whether you really bought them. It could appear in a shopping diary, a fictional story, or a false statement. Plausibility concerns whether a claim fits familiar concepts and patterns; factuality concerns the world. The essay argues that ChatGPT is strong at forming the former. When a task requires the latter, a receipt, database, observation, or reliable source is more appropriate than the sentence's natural tone.
