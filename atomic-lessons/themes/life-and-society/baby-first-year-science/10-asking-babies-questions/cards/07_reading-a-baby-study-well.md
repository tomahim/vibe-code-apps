A short checklist distills this lesson. First: what was the proxy - looking, sucking, turning, brain signal - and what inference connects it to the claim? Longer looking means discrimination; it does not by itself prove understanding. Second: how many babies, and how many dropped out? Infant studies lose a large share of participants to fussiness and sleep, which can skew samples.

Third: has it been replicated, ideally by independent labs or a consortium like ManyBabies? Fourth: is the claim about detection or about rich knowledge? Babies detecting a difference is strong evidence; babies understanding hidden objects is an interpretation layered on top.

These are the questions reviewers and good journalists apply. They are also why this course kept citing ranges, consortia and converging methods: in a field where the subjects cannot speak, humility is a methodological requirement, and the best findings survive it.
