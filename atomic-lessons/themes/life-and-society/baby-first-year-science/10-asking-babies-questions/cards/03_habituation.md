title: "Habituation: boredom as an instrument"

Babies, like everyone, get bored. Show a newborn the same checkerboard repeatedly and looking time drops trial by trial - a process called habituation. Then change something and watch: if looking time rebounds (dishabituation), the baby registered the change. If not, the difference was invisible to them.

Habituation turned boredom into a precision instrument. It revealed that young infants discriminate speech sounds from any language (lesson 5's starting point), distinguish colors, notice numerical changes in small sets, and remember what they have seen. The rate of habituation itself became a measure: faster habituation in infancy modestly predicts later cognitive scores, one of the few infant measures with that property.

The logic appears everywhere in this course's evidence: whenever a study concludes a baby can tell A from B, the proof was usually that a bored baby perked up for one and not the other.
