Psychology's replication crisis did not spare infancy research. Some celebrated violation-of-expectation results proved hard to repeat, and critics noted small samples and flexible analysis choices in the older literature. The field's response has become a model in itself: ManyBabies, a global consortium where dozens of infant labs run the same pre-registered experiment on the same question with large, diverse samples.

ManyBabies projects have tested, for instance, whether infants prefer infant-directed speech (the preference replicated, robustly) and re-examined classic claims about infant social evaluation, sometimes confirming, sometimes shrinking the original effects.

For readers of baby science, the lesson is methodological maturity: single small studies in any one lab should be held lightly, while converging results across methods and consortia deserve confidence. This course has leaned on findings of the second kind - preferential-looking basics, the crying curve, perceptual narrowing, prenatal voice learning.
