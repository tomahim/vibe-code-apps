title: "Violation of expectation: measuring surprise"

The most famous - and most debated - infant method asks what babies expect of the world. The logic: babies look longer at events that violate their expectations. Show a solid screen rotating through space where a hidden box should block it, and even young infants stare, suggesting they expected the box to remain solid and present.

Renée Baillargeon's studies from the 1980s used this logic to argue that infants only months old expect objects to persist when hidden, to be solid, and to need support - conclusions that pushed knowledge of objects far earlier than Piaget had claimed. Her 1985 drawbridge study with Elizabeth Spelke and Stanley Wasserman is the classic.

The method carries a standing caution: longer looking shows that babies notice a difference, but interpreting why - impossibility, novelty, or mere perceptual oddity - requires care. That debate, and its modern replication chapter, is the next card ([Baillargeon, Spelke & Wasserman, 1985](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/infantlab/articles.old/baillargeon_spelke_wasserman1985.pdf.pdf)).
