title: "The problem: a subject who cannot say"
archived: false
---
Developmental science faces a unique obstacle: its subjects cannot follow instructions, answer questions, or stay still. A newborn cannot tell you whether two faces look different or whether a voice is familiar. Every fact in the previous nine lessons - vision acuity, voice preference, flavor learning - had to be extracted indirectly.

The solution is to find behaviors babies already produce and wire them to experimental questions. Babies look at things, suck when interested or soothed, turn their heads, and show cardiac and brain responses to surprise. Each behavior became an instrument.

This lesson is about those instruments: preferential looking, habituation, violation-of-expectation, conditioned sucking and head-turns. Understanding them changes how you read any claim about what babies know: the conclusion is always an inference from a proxy, which is both the strength and the limitation of the field.
