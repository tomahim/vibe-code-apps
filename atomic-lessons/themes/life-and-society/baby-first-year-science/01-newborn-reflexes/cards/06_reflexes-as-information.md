Within the first day of life, and again at early checkups, clinicians run through a standard set of reflex checks. They stroke the cheek for rooting, press the palm for grasp, elicit the Moro, hold the baby upright for stepping, and watch sucking and swallowing during a feed. Each probe targets identifiable neural pathways.

The power of these checks is that they need no cooperation from the baby. An infant cannot answer questions or follow instructions, but reflexes report directly on the nervous system's wiring. Presence, strength and symmetry on both sides of the body are the basic data points, always interpreted alongside muscle tone, alertness and feeding.

This is also where this course's rule applies: educational content is not diagnosis. A reflex that seems weak during one sleepy afternoon can look entirely typical the next day. Interpretation belongs to professionals who see the whole child, not to a single observation or a milestone chart.
