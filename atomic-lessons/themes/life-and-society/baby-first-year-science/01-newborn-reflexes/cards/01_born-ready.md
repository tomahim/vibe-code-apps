A reflex is an automatic, involuntary response to a specific stimulus, and newborns arrive with a whole set of them. Touch a baby's cheek and the head turns; press a finger into the palm and the fingers close around it. No learning is required: these circuits are wired into the brainstem and spinal cord before birth.

Early reflexes are often described as survival tools. Rooting and sucking help a newborn find and take milk, grasping echoes our primate ancestry, and the Moro response throws the arms out when a baby feels suddenly unsupported. Some reflexes clearly serve feeding and protection; others look like leftovers from earlier stages of development.

For parents and researchers, reflexes are also a window into the nervous system. Because they are automatic, their presence, strength and symmetry give clinicians a standardized way to check that a newborn's brain and nerves are working as expected in the first days of life.
