Reflexes are the opening chapter, not the whole story. Within weeks, babies begin converting raw reflexive material into controlled action. The automatic head turn of rooting becomes deliberate looking toward a breast or bottle. The grasp that clings to your finger becomes reaching, then the precise pincer grip used for peas and cereal pieces.

The transition is gradual and messy. Movements are jerky before they are smooth, and skills appear in bursts separated by plateaus. A baby may rock on hands and knees for weeks before crawling, or cruise along furniture for months before letting go. These plateaus are normal features of learning, not stalls.

This is also where caregiving context enters: floor time, positions to play in, and objects worth reaching for all feed the transition. The next lesson follows the big motor sequence from head control to first steps, and the one after asks how practice itself builds those skills.
