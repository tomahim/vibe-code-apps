title: "Why reflexes fade: control moves upstairs"
archived: false
---
Newborn reflexes are organized low in the nervous system, mostly in the brainstem and spinal cord. Over the first months, as the cortex and its connections mature, many of these automatic patterns weaken or disappear. Rooting fades around four months, the palmar grasp around five to six months, and the Moro typically by six months.

Fading does not mean the circuits are deleted. The responses are gradually integrated into voluntary behavior. The automatic grasp becomes deliberate reaching and releasing; the startle machinery persists in adults as the jump you make at a sudden bang. Development layers flexible control on top of older automatic machinery rather than throwing the machinery away.

This integration is also why timing matters more than drama. A reflex that lingers far beyond its usual window, or vanishes very early, is one signal professionals weigh when assessing development, alongside many others. A reflex by itself is never a verdict about a child's future.
