title: "Rooting and sucking: built for feeding"

---
The rooting reflex is the one parents meet first. Stroke the corner of a newborn's mouth or cheek and the baby turns toward the touch, mouth open, searching for a nipple. Pair that with the sucking reflex - anything touching the roof of the mouth triggers rhythmic sucking - and a minutes-old baby can already perform the two hardest parts of feeding.

These reflexes are strongest in the first weeks and most reliable when the baby is awake and calm. They gradually come under voluntary control: by roughly four months, rooting fades as purposeful head-turning and reaching take over. The baby no longer needs an automatic search program because looking, leaning and grabbing do the job better.

There is also a quieter partner: swallowing, which must coordinate with sucking and breathing. This suck-swallow-breathe rhythm is so demanding that many premature babies need feeding support until it matures, one reason feeding is a standard checkpoint before hospital discharge.
