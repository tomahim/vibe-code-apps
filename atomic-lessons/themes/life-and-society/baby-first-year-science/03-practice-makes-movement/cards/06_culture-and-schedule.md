title: "Culture changes the schedule"

If practice shapes timing, caregiving customs should shift the calendar - and they do. Classic cross-cultural studies described communities in Jamaica and parts of Africa where daily handling routines include stretching, stepping and sitting exercises, and where babies sat and walked earlier on average than Western norms predicted. Where swaddling or cradle-boards limit movement for parts of the day, some skills arrive later on average.

Equipment plays a role too, in the opposite direction. Baby walkers with wheels give mobility without building the balance and strength walking requires; the American Academy of Pediatrics has long discouraged them because of injury risk, and studies associate their use with slightly later walking rather than earlier.

The scientific point is not that one culture is right. It is that the developing system is genuinely plastic: daily experience moves the dials within the wide healthy ranges from lesson 2. Milestones are built from what babies practice all day long.
