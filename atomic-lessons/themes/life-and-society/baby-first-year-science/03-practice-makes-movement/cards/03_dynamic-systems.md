title: "Dynamic systems: skills assembled on the spot"

Thelen's alternative, dynamic systems theory, treats each motor skill as a solution assembled from many components: muscle strength, balance, limb proportions, motivation, the surface underfoot, even the bulk of a diaper. Walking appears when all the components reach a workable combination, not when a walking gene switches on.

This reframing explains observations the program story could not. It explains why milestone timing varies with body build, why skills emerge in bursts after plateaus, and why the same baby can look competent on a firm floor and wobbly on a squishy mattress. Change any component and the assembled skill changes.

It also explains why development is both orderly and individual. The components mature in a loosely common order, which produces the famous sequence, but each baby's combination is their own. The sequence is real; the schedule is not.
