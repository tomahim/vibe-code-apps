title: "Reading brain claims about babies"

Infant neuroscience invites overclaiming, so a few reading rules help. Rule one: a brain image or EEG waveform is a measurement, not an explanation; saying a region lit up during peekaboo does not explain peekaboo. Rule two: correlation in time is not mechanism - showing that responsive parenting and brain measures track together does not prove how one builds the other, however plausible.

Rule three: beware precision inflation. EEG timing is precise but localization coarse; fNIRS localizes cortex but only the surface; MRI shows anatomy superbly but awake function poorly. A claim that outruns its method's resolution deserves skepticism.

The durable findings rest on convergence: sensitive-period effects confirmed across cataract cases, animal work and perceptual studies; practice effects shown in behavior and supported by plasticity biology. In this field as in lesson 10, humility plus convergence is what makes knowledge.
