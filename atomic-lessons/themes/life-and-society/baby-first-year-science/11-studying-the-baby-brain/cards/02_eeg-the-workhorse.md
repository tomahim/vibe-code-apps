title: "EEG: the workhorse of infant brain research"
archived: false
---
Electroencephalography is the oldest and still most used window into the infant brain. A soft sensor net or cap records the brain's electrical rhythms through the scalp. It is silent, motion-tolerant by scanning standards, cheap enough for large studies, and requires no sedation - babies can sit on a parent's lap watching stimuli.

EEG's superpower is timing. Event-related potentials (ERPs) resolve the brain's response millisecond by millisecond. The infant N170-like response to faces, mismatch responses to unexpected speech sounds, and sleep spindles have all been mapped this way. Kuhl's speech-tuning work, for instance, paired behavioral discrimination with brain responses to native and foreign syllables.

The trade-off is location: EEG says when with precision but where only coarsely, because the skull smears electrical signals. Reading that infants' brains detect a sound change is strong; claims about which precise region fired are much softer.
