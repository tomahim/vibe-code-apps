The complement of sensitive periods is plasticity, and the infant brain has it in unusual degree. When injury or malformation strikes early, young brains reorganize dramatically: children who lose an entire hemisphere in early childhood can develop surprisingly typical language, an outcome unimaginable for adults after similar injury.

Everyday plasticity is quieter but constant. Every mechanism in this course is plasticity at work: reflexes integrating into voluntary control, motor calibration rebuilt per posture, speech perception retuning to the native language, flavor preferences shaped by milk. Experience-expectant plasticity is the term for development that counts on universal inputs - faces, voices, movement - while experience-dependent plasticity tunes the brain to the particular environment.

The balanced view avoids two errors: the brain is not infinitely reprogrammable at any age, and it is not fixed on a rigid clock either. The first year is special because so many systems are simultaneously open for calibration.
