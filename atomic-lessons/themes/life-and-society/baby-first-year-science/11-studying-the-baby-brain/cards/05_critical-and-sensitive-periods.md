title: "Sensitive periods: windows, not cliffs"

---
The idea that experience must arrive on time has a real but softer form in modern science. Critical periods - strict now-or-never windows - are well established for a few systems: the classic case is visual development, where congenital cataracts removed too late leave permanent deficits despite restored optics.

For most of development, scientists prefer sensitive periods: windows when experience has outsized effects but the window closes gradually and incompletely. Perceptual narrowing for speech sounds across the second half of the first year is the course's clearest case: tuning begins early and powerfully, yet later language learning remains entirely possible, if harder.

The distinction matters for reading headlines. Brain-based urgency claims - talk to your baby or lose the chance forever - overshoot the evidence. The defensible version is gentler: early experience lays foundations that make later learning easier, and some specific systems genuinely have deadlines, while most have preferences.
