title: "fNIRS: light on the brain"

Functional near-infrared spectroscopy is the newcomer that transformed awake-infant imaging. A headband shines harmless near-infrared light into the scalp; the light returning carries the signature of oxygenated blood, revealing which cortical areas were just active. Like EEG it works on a baby sitting upright on a lap, tolerating some movement, but it localizes activity to rough regions of cortex.

fNIRS has let researchers watch the cortex during naturalistic situations EEG handles awkwardly: babies listening to live speakers, watching faces, or interacting with caregivers. Studies using it showed, for example, that infant and adult brain activity synchronizes during shared play, giving neural substance to the serve-and-return idea.

Its limits mirror its strengths: light penetrates only the outer cortex, so deep structures stay invisible, and spatial resolution remains coarse. fNIRS maps neighborhoods, not streets - but for social neuroscience with awake babies, those neighborhoods were previously dark.
