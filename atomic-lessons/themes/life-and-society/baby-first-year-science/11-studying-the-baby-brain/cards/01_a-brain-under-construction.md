The first-year brain changes faster than at any later age. At birth it is about a third of adult volume; by twelve months it has roughly doubled. The growth is not just size: neurons form connections (synapses) at extraordinary rates, axons acquire myelin - the insulation that speeds signaling - and the dense, noisy network of the newborn is progressively tuned by experience.

Two opposite processes run together. Overproduction floods the young brain with more synapses than it will keep, then pruning removes underused connections, sculpting efficient circuits. Different systems run on different schedules: basic sensory areas mature earlier, prefrontal circuits for planning far later.

This is the biological backdrop for everything earlier in the course: experience does not write on a blank brain, but it decides which of the overbuilt connections survive. Serve-and-return, tummy time, conversation - their proposed leverage is exactly this use-dependent sculpting.
