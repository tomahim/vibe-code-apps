title: "Gesture: the bridge to words"

Between cooing and first words, babies develop a third channel: deliberate gesture. Around nine to ten months, pointing appears - first to request (give me that), then to declare (look at that!). Declarative pointing is a major event: the baby demonstrates they understand that another mind can be directed, a foundation of all later communication.

Gesture does real communicative work. Reaching to be picked up, pushing food away, waving bye-bye, showing a toy - each is a sentence before sentences. Research by Susan Goldin-Meadow and others shows that the things children say with gesture often lead their speech: a child who combines a pointing gesture with one word (pointing at a dog while saying big) tends to produce that two-word combination in speech weeks later.

Gesture is also the first channel in which comprehension visibly exceeds production: babies reliably point, wave and understand routines months before their vocabularies catch up.
