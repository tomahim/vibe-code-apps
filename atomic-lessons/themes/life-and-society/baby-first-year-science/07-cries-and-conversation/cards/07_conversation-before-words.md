Put the pieces together and the first year already contains the full skeleton of conversation. Crying broadcasts need; cooing sustains exchange; serve-and-return supplies turn-taking structure; gesture adds deliberate reference; and comprehension quietly accumulates meaning. Only the words are missing, and they are on their way.

Each piece is also a joint production. None of it develops well in a vacuum: the crying curve is universal, but the responses it meets shape regulation; babble needs listeners; pointing requires someone whose attention is worth directing. Communication is built between two people from the start.

The next two lessons follow the sound side of this story - how babbling turns vocal play into the raw material of speech - and then the meaning side, when first words finally attach sounds to things.
