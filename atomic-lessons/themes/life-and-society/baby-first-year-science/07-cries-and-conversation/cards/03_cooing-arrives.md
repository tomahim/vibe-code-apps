title: "Cooing: the sound of contentment"

Around six to eight weeks, a new kind of sound appears: cooing - soft, musical, vowel-like sounds such as oo and ah, usually produced when the baby is calm, alert and engaged with someone. Where crying mobilizes, cooing socializes.

Cooing is vocal practice with a social engine. The baby is learning to coordinate the vocal folds, tongue and breath in the same apparatus used for speech, and caregivers answer with smiles, imitation and their own sounds. Around the same age, the social smile arrives, and the pair of them - coo and smile - transform one-way care into exchange.

Researchers treat this as the opening of protoconversation: short, turn-taking bouts in which baby and caregiver alternate sounds and expressions with the rhythm of dialogue, months before any word exists. The structure of conversation is rehearsed before its content.
