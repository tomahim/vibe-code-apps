title: "Understanding before speaking"

One of the most robust facts of the first year is that comprehension runs ahead of production. Long before a baby says a recognizable word, they demonstrate understanding: turning when their name is called (commonly around five to six months), looking at the right person for mama or dada, responding to no, finding a named object or person in the room.

The gap can be enormous. Estimates from parent-report inventories like the MacArthur-Bates CDI suggest many one-year-olds understand dozens of words and phrases while saying almost none. Receptive language is quietly compounding while productive language is still learning to control the mouth.

This asymmetry has a practical reading: a preverbal baby is a listening baby. Talking through the day - naming objects, narrating routines - feeds a comprehension system that is already storing inventory for the speech explosion to come in lesson 9.
