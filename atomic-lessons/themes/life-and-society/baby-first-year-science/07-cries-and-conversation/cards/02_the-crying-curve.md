The peak of normal crying is so predictable that pediatricians give families a map of it in advance. The Period PURPLE Crying program, developed by developmental researcher Ronald Barr, spells out the features: Peak of crying around two months, Unexpected onset and stopping, Resistance to soothing, a Pain-like face, Long bouts, and Evening clustering. Each letter describes typical, time-limited behavior of an otherwise healthy baby.

The practical payload of this research is safety. Because normal crying can reach hours per day and resist all soothing, exhausted caregivers are at risk of losing control; shaken baby syndrome cases cluster exactly around the crying peak. Knowing that the peak is universal and temporary is a protective factor in itself.

This is the science lesson: a behavior that looks like malfunction is a normal developmental phase. What caregivers supply is not a cure but safe endurance - soothing attempts, breaks, and handovers - until the curve descends on its own.
