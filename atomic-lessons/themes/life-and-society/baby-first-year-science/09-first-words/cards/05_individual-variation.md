title: "Late talkers, early talkers, and the range of normal"
archived: false
---
Vocabulary at age two varies enormously among typical children: from a handful of words to several hundred. The MacArthur-Bates Communicative Development Inventories, built from large parent-report samples, exist precisely to map this spread - and their percentile tables are wide.

Some late talkers are simply on the slow end of normal and catch up on their own; research following such children finds many indistinguishable from peers within a few years. A subset benefit from early support, and because no one can perfectly sort the two groups in advance, persistent delay is a standard reason to consult a professional - while remembering that variation itself is not pathology.

Bilingual babies deserve special mention: their vocabulary splits across two languages, so each single-language count underestimates what they know. Total conceptual vocabulary - words known in either language - is the fair measure, and bilingual development within it is normal and healthy.
