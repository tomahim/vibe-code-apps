title: "What counts as a first word"

When does a sound become a word? Researchers apply a simple test: the sound must be used consistently for a stable meaning, somewhat independent of context. A baby who says ba whenever the ball appears, and only then, has a word; a baby who says bababa at everything is still babbling.

By that test, first words typically land around the first birthday, with a wide normal range of roughly 10 to 15 months. The CDC checklist, built so that at least 75% of children show each skill, places tries to say one or two words besides mama or dada at 15 months - a deliberately conservative line.

First words everywhere draw on the same small sound stock rehearsed in babbling - mama, dada, baba, papa - and name the same kinds of things: important people, food, animals, body parts and objects that move or make noise. A first vocabulary is a portrait of a baby's life.
