If word learning is inference from input, input matters. The famous and much-debated word gap studies of the 1990s claimed millions-of-words differences between children by age three along socioeconomic lines; later work refined the numbers but confirmed the core: the quantity and, more importantly, the quality and responsiveness of talk directed at children predicts their vocabulary growth.

Quality means conversational turns more than monologues, questions and expansions more than corrections, and shared book reading more than background television. Live, contingent speech is what the system is built to learn from; Kuhl's video experiment from lesson 5 showed recorded speech failing to teach where live speech succeeded.

The science here doubles as reassurance rather than pressure: no products, flashcards or apps outperform ordinary responsive conversation, songs and stories delivered across the day.
