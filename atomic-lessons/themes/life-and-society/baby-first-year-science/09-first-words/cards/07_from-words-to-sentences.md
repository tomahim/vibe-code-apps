The first year ends with single words; the second begins combining them. Somewhere around 18 to 24 months, children who command roughly 50 words start producing two-word mini-sentences: more juice, daddy go, big doggie. Word combinations are a genuine threshold - the child is now doing grammar, not just labeling.

The content of these first sentences is wonderfully consistent across children and languages: agents and actions, possessions, locations, recurrences, refusals. No is among the most practiced words of the era, and it does real grammatical work.

This is where this course's subtheme ends and a longer story begins: from two-word combinations, children's sentences lengthen and regularize through the preschool years. The first year supplied everything needed - tuned perception, practiced syllables, mapped meanings and a partner who answers.
