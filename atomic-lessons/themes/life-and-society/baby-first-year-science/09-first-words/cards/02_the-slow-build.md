title: "The slow build: words ten to fifty"

After the first word, growth is gradual at first. The climb from 1 to about 50 words often takes months, and this slow phase is where the real cracking of the code happens: the baby works out that things have names, that names are shared, and that the same word picks out the same kind of thing tomorrow.

This insight has a name: the naming insight or the realization that everything has a label. Once it lands, word learning changes character - from occasional lucky pairings to a general appetite for what things are called.

During this phase, comprehension still far exceeds production, and errors are charmingly systematic. Overextension is common: dog applied to all four-legged animals, ball to anything round. These are not confusions so much as first-draft theories of categories, corrected as vocabulary fills in.
