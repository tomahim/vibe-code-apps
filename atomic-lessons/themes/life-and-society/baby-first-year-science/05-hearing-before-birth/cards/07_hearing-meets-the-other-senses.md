title: "Hearing meets the other senses"

Hearing does not develop in isolation. By about four months, babies begin matching speech sounds to the lip movements that produce them - the foundation of the McGurk effect, where seeing one syllable mouthed while hearing another changes what adults perceive. Babies also link voices to faces and learn to expect that a familiar voice belongs to a familiar person.

This cross-sense binding is part of a broader pattern: the senses develop together and check each other. A sounding object invites looking; looking invites reaching; reaching invites mouthing. Each sense supplies evidence the others cannot, and the brain assembles a coherent world from the combination.

Hearing's special role is social. It delivers voices before birth, carries the melody of emotion, and provides the raw signal from which language will be carved - the subject of the next subtheme.
