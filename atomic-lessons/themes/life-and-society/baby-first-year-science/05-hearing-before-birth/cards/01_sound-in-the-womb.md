title: "Sound in the womb"

---
The womb is not silent. By the third trimester, the fetal auditory system is functional, and the developing baby lives inside a soundscape: the mother's heartbeat, blood flow, digestion - and, filtered through tissue and fluid, her voice. Studies using heart-rate and movement responses show fetuses react to sound from roughly 25 to 27 weeks of gestation.

The mother's voice passes through her own body, which changes it: low frequencies carry best, and the melody and rhythm of her speech survive far better than fine acoustic detail. External sounds arrive muffled, like listening through a wall.

This early listening is not idle. The auditory system is already learning, and the proof arrives at birth: newborns show measurable preferences for sounds they heard repeatedly before they were born. Hearing is the sense with the longest head start, and it shapes the very first days of life.
