title: "The voice they already know"

In 1980, Anthony DeCasper and William Fifer published a landmark experiment. Newborns, days old, could control which recording played by adjusting their sucking rhythm on a special pacifier - and they sucked in the pattern that produced their own mother's voice over another woman's. Three-day-old babies already knew and preferred the voice they had heard from the womb.

Later work pushed the finding further: newborns prefer a story their mother read aloud repeatedly in the last weeks of pregnancy over a new story, and they prefer the rhythm of their native language over a foreign one. Prenatal hearing leaves real memory traces.

This is why hearing, unlike vision, arrives at birth already tuned to the baby's specific environment. The construction started months before anyone could see it ([DeCasper & Fifer, 1980](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.7375928)).
