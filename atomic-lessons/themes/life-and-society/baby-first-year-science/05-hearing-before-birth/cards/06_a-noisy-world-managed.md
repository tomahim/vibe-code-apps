The modern environment adds a wrinkle: babies now grow up amid recordings and screens in addition to voices. Research consistently finds that infant language learning thrives on live, responsive human speech. Kuhl's lab showed that foreign-language exposure kept non-native discrimination alive at nine months only when delivered by a live person; identical exposure by video or audio alone produced no measurable learning.

Sound also interacts with sleep, which dominates infant life. Newborns sleep through moderate noise but are calmed by rhythmic, womb-like sounds - one reason shushing, humming and white noise work. These imitate the filtered acoustic world the baby knew before birth.

The practical science is simple: a baby's auditory diet is built from responsive voices, songs and everyday household sound. No special products required; the tuning system runs on ordinary conversation.
