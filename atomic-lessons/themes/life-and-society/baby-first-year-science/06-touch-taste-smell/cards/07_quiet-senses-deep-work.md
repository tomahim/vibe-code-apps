Touch, taste and smell rarely headline developmental science, yet they do some of its deepest work. They are the first senses online, they regulate the newborn's unstable physiology, and they carry the earliest social recognition. Long before a baby reads a face, they know their caregiver by feel and scent.

They also demonstrate this course's recurring pattern: built-in beginnings shaped by experience. Sweet preference and bitter aversion come preloaded, but the menu of accepted flavors is written by prenatal and milk exposure. Touch receptors are born ready, but the stress system's calibration depends on caregiving input.

With the senses surveyed, the course turns to their most famous product: the slow, astonishing assembly of communication, from the first cry to the first word.
