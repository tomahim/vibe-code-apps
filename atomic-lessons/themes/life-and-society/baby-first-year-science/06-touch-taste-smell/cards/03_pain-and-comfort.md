title: "Pain, stress and comfort"

---
For much of the twentieth century, medicine doubted that newborns really felt pain. That view is dead. Newborns show the full physiological signature of pain - stress hormone surges, heart-rate changes, distinctive crying and facial responses - and their pain pathways are functional, even if still maturing. Modern neonatal care treats pain management in infants as a requirement, not an option.

The complementary finding is that comfort works. Sweet-tasting solutions, sucking, and being held or contained measurably reduce pain responses during minor procedures like heel sticks. Skin-to-skin contact during such procedures lowers pain scores, a finding now embedded in neonatal practice.

The developmental lesson is broad: the systems for distress and for soothing are both present from birth, and caregiving is one of the main inputs into how the stress system calibrates over the first year.
