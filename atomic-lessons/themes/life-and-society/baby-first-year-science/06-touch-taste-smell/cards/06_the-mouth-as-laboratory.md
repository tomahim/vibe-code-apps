From around three to four months, hands gain enough control to deliver objects reliably to the mouth, and the mouth takes over as the baby's main measurement instrument. Mouthing is systematic exploration: gums, tongue and lips carry dense touch receptors, and oral exploration gives detailed data on texture, hardness and shape that hands and eyes cannot.

This phase unsettles parents but delights researchers, because it marks a cognitive step: the baby is deliberately gathering information, not just reacting. Combined with improving vision, mouthing is part of the cross-sensory mapping described in the last lesson - feeling what you see, seeing what you feel.

The same mouth is also running feeding experiments. As solid foods arrive around the middle of the first year, the flavor education from card 4 meets texture learning, gag-reflex calibration and chewing practice. Eating is a skill assembled from all the quiet senses at once.
