title: "Taste and smell before birth"

Flavor learning begins prenatally. Aromatic compounds from the mother's diet - garlic, vanilla, carrot, anise - reach the amniotic fluid, which the fetus swallows in the third trimester. In Julie Mennella's studies, infants whose mothers drank carrot juice during pregnancy showed greater acceptance of carrot-flavored cereal months later at weaning, compared with infants without that exposure.

After birth, the learning continues through milk. Breast milk carries flavors from the mother's meals, varying day to day, while formula tastes constant - one hypothesized reason breastfed babies often accept a wider variety of foods at weaning. The flavor bridge runs from womb to milk to the family table.

Newborns also arrive with built-in biases: a ready preference for sweetness and an aversion to strong bitterness, probably ancient protections for seeking energy and avoiding toxins. Everything else is educated taste ([Mennella et al., 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1351272/)).
