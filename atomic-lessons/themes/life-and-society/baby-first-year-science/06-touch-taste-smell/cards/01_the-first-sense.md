Touch is the first sense to develop. Responses to touch around the mouth appear by about eight weeks of gestation, spreading across the body over the following months, long before hearing or vision come online. At birth, touch is already a mature, high-bandwidth channel.

Its jobs are immediate: the rooting reflex from lesson 1 is touch-triggered, and a newborn's mouth and hands are dense with sensors, which is one reason everything eventually goes into the mouth. Mouthing is not just exploration for fun; it is a primary way of measuring an object's texture, shape and temperature.

Touch is also the most social of the early senses. Long before a baby can interpret a face or understand a word, a hand on the back communicates safety. The nervous system treats gentle stroking and holding as fundamental signals, not luxuries.
