title: "Smell: the intimate sense"

Smell is a newborn's social compass. Within the first days, breastfed babies turn preferentially toward the odor of their own mother's breast and can distinguish it from another mother's, using smell to find the nipple. The learning starts with amniotic fluid, which shares aromatic notes with the maternal environment the baby will be born into.

The traffic is two-way: mothers can reliably identify their own newborn's odor within hours to days of birth, and familiar body odor calms babies - infants settle faster and cry less when wrapped in something carrying their mother's scent.

Smell never gets the attention vision and hearing receive, because adults mostly use it in the background. For a baby lying in arms, unable to see far or understand words, odor is a primary identity signal: this is my person, this is home, this is food.
