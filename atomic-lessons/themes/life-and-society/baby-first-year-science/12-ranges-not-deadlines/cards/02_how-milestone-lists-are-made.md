title: "How milestone lists are actually made"

Milestone checklists feel authoritative, so it is worth knowing their recipe. For the CDC's 2022 revision, a team led by developmental-behavioral pediatrician Jennifer Zubler reviewed the published evidence for each milestone and placed it at the well-child-visit age by which at least 75% of children would be expected to show it. The explicit goal: make a missing milestone actionable rather than average.

The 75% choice is a design decision with consequences. Under old lists, a milestone at its average age meant half of children had not yet shown it, which invited wait and see. At 75%, absence is more informative - but it still labels a substantial minority of perfectly typical children as not yet there.

The WHO windows take the complementary approach: instead of one actionable line, they publish the full 1st-to-99th-percentile range from 816 children in five countries. Two tools, two philosophies: a screening conversation starter versus a description of normal spread ([CDC key points](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/key-points.html)).
