If single milestones mislead, what does healthy development look like? The research answer is pattern over points. Across domains, is the child adding skills over weeks - new sounds, new movements, new ways of engaging? Trajectory matters more than position. Across senses, does the baby use looking, listening, touching and mouthing to explore? Across relationships, does the baby serve - looks, coos, gestures - and delight in returned answers?

Loss of skills is the one pattern professionals treat as urgent at any age: a child who reliably did something and stops - words, social engagement, a motor skill - is different in kind from a child who has not yet done it. That asymmetry is one of the most robust rules in developmental surveillance.

The rest is the course's recurring theme: ranges are wide, plateaus are normal, and the next skill is usually being quietly assembled out of view, the way babbling rehearses speech before anyone hears a word.
