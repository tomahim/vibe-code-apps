A baby arrives with a toolkit: reflexes for feeding and protection, hearing already tuned by the womb, touch ready to regulate, and a brain doubling in size with connections to spare. Over twelve months, practice converts the toolkit into skills - head to toe, with thousands of steps and dozens of falls a day once walking begins. Senses calibrate: vision sharpens from blur to binocular detail, hearing specializes for the home language, flavors are learned from milk. Communication assembles: cries, coos, babble rehearsed in the accent of home, first words around the first birthday.

Science watches all this through clever proxies - looking, sucking, surprise, electrical rhythms - and corrects itself through replication. And its deepest finding is the one this lesson is built on: the architecture is universal, the timetable is not.

Ranges, not deadlines. That is the first year through science.
