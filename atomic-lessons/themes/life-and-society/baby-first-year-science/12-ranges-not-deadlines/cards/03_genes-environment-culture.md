Where does the variation come from? Twin studies show that the timing of milestones is partly heritable - early-walking families exist - but the effects are modest and entangled with shared environments. Body proportions, muscle-to-weight ratios and temperament all contribute, as lesson 2 detailed.

Environment contributes at least as much. Practice opportunity shifts timing: tummy time predicts earlier prone milestones; cultural handling routines in some Jamaican and African communities, with daily stretching and stepping exercises, associate with earlier sitting and walking; extended time in containers like walkers and exersaucers associates with later ones.

Culture also decides what counts as a milestone at all. Western lists prize independent walking; communities with different caregiving goals prize different skills. The WHO study deliberately sampled five countries to describe human variation rather than one culture's schedule - a reminder that every milestone list embeds assumptions about what babies do all day.
