Every subtheme in this course landed on the same discovery. Movement: WHO windows spanning months, with 4.3% of healthy children skipping crawling. Senses: vision sharpening over a year, hearing tuned by whatever language happened to surround the baby. Language: first words anywhere from about 10 to 15 months, vocabularies at age two ranging from a handful to hundreds.

The pattern is that development has an architecture but not a timetable. The sequence is broadly ordered - head control before walking, cooing before babbling, babbling before words - because each skill is assembled from components that mature and practice in order. But the assembly speed varies with body, brain, temperament, opportunity and culture.

Variation is not noise around a true schedule. Variation is the normal product of a system that builds itself in a particular body, family and world. This lesson turns that pattern into a way of reading any milestone claim.
