title: "Screening, surveillance, diagnosis: three different words"
archived: false

Reading milestone lists well requires three distinctions the public conversation blurs. Surveillance is the ongoing watch: at each visit, professionals ask, observe and elicit concerns - milestone checklists live here, as communication tools. Screening is a periodic standardized test given to all children at set ages (commonly 9, 18 and 30 months in US guidance), designed to flag who deserves a closer look. Diagnosis is the full evaluation that follows a flag, and only it concludes anything.

The CDC is unusually explicit that its checklists are surveillance tools and must not be used to diagnose. That is not legal caution; it reflects how development works. A single observation on one afternoon, measured against a 75% line, cannot separate a late-but-typical child from one who benefits from support. Trajectory across time matters more than any point.

This is also the boundary of this course: it describes how the science works, and deliberately leaves individual questions to professionals who examine individual children.
