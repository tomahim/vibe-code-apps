title: "Jargon: babbling with opinions"

Late in the first year and into the second, many babies produce jargon babbling (also called conversational babble): long, fluent streams of syllables delivered with convincing intonation - questions rise, statements fall, scoldings sound scolding. Adults often describe it as the baby speaking a private language.

Linguistically, that is close to the truth. Jargon babble shows the baby has captured the melody of conversation - the prosody that carries turn-taking, emotion and intent - before stocking it with words. The baby is performing conversation with the words still missing.

This stage often overlaps with first real words, and the two coexist happily: streams of jargon punctuated by a recognizable mama or ball. It is a visible reminder that language is more than vocabulary; the music of speech is a learnable layer of its own, and babies master it early.
