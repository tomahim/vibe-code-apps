The boundary between babbling and speech is pleasantly blurry. Late babble and early words share sounds, and some forms sit right on the border: a baby's consistent ba used only for ball is arguably already a word - a stable sound-meaning pairing, which is the definition that matters.

Caregivers accelerate the transition by treating babble as conversation. When a baby says ba while reaching for a ball and the adult answers yes, ball!, the loop supplies exactly what word learning needs: the baby's own sound, a shared focus of attention, and the adult's label. This is serve and return doing vocabulary work.

So the arc of the first year completes: reflexive cries become social coos, coos become syllables, syllables become jargon, and jargon becomes first words. The next lesson takes up what those first words do once they land.
