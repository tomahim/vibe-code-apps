Babbling looks like play because it is play - but play with a function. Producing syllables lets the baby practice the motor routines of speech: coordinating jaw, tongue, lips and breath at conversational speed. Like the motor skills of lessons 2 and 3, speech movement is assembled through massive daily repetition.

Babbling is also social. Babies babble most when engaged with responsive partners, and studies show caregivers respond to babble as if it were speech - answering, imitating, expanding. Those responses shape what happens next: infants whose babbling gets contingent replies develop more mature vocalizations, because the social loop tells the baby which sounds do communicative work.

In this sense babbling is the vocal version of serve and return from lesson 7. The baby serves a syllable; the caregiver returns a word; and the baby's sound system gradually bends toward the language it hears answered.
