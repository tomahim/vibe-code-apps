Because canonical babbling arrives on a comparatively tight schedule, its timing carries information. The absence of canonical babble - few or no well-formed consonant-vowel syllables by about ten months - is one of the early markers associated with hearing loss and with later speech and language difficulties, which is why pediatric surveillance asks about babbling specifically.

As always in this course, a marker is a prompt, not a verdict. Late babbling has many possible explanations, and assessment belongs to professionals with hearing tests and full history. The scientific point is that babble's predictability makes it useful: tightly timed behaviors are the ones where deviation is informative.

Equally informative is what follows babble. Children who babble richly tend to say their first words with the very syllables they practiced, and the size and sound inventory of babble relates to early vocabulary. Rehearsal, it turns out, predicts repertoire.
