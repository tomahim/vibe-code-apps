Early babbling sounds similar across the world, drawing on a common stock of easy syllables. Then something subtle happens: over the second half of the first year, babble begins to drift. The baby's productions increasingly match the rhythm, melody and sound inventory of the surrounding language - French-learning babies babble with French-ish intonation, Japanese-learning babies with Japanese-ish timing.

This drift is perceptual narrowing made audible. Lesson 5 described how perception tunes to the native language in the second half of the first year; babbling is where the production system follows. The baby is averaging the speech they hear into the speech they attempt.

The practical consequence is that late-first-year babble already contains the accent of home. When first words arrive, they are built from exactly the syllables the baby has been rehearsing - which is why early words across languages lean on the same easy sounds: mama, dada, baba, papa.
