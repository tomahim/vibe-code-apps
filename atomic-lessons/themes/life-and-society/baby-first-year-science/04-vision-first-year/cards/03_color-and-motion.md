title: "Color, motion and the moving world"

Newborns are not colorblind, but their color vision is limited at first. By about two to four months, infants discriminate colors in ways resembling adult categories - reds, greens, blues and yellows group together for them much as they do for us - as the cone cells and their neural wiring mature.

Motion is a separate superpower. Even very young babies track slow-moving objects with their eyes, though the pursuit is jerky at first, a series of catch-up saccades rather than a smooth glide. Smooth pursuit improves steadily over the first months, which is why a two-week-old loses a moving toy while a four-month-old follows it across the room.

Motion perception matters beyond entertainment. Looming - the rapid expansion of an approaching object - triggers blinking and defensive responses early on, one of the first signs that the baby is extracting depth and trajectory from the visual flow.
