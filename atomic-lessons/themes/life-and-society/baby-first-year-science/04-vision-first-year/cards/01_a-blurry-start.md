A newborn's view of the world is famously fuzzy. Standard estimates put newborn acuity around 20/400: what an adult sees clearly at 400 feet, a newborn resolves at about 20. Details, fine patterns and distant faces are simply not available yet. What is available is the world at feeding distance - roughly the gap between a baby's eyes and a caregiver's face while held.

The limits are partly optical and partly neural. The eye's structures are still maturing, and so is the visual cortex that interprets the signal. Both improve rapidly: acuity climbs steeply over the first year, though adult-like precision takes years to fully arrive.

This blurriness is not a defect to fix; it is a starting point matched to a baby's actual life. High contrast and close range are exactly what a newborn's environment provides, and the visual system develops fastest where stimulation is richest.
