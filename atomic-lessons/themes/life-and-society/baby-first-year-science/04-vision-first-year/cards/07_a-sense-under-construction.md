title: "A sense under construction"

Vision is the sense adults trust most, which makes its slow construction striking. At birth it is the least finished major sense: blurry, close-range, drawn to contrast and faces. Within twelve months it supports tracking, depth, accurate reaching and the beginnings of reading the social world.

The construction is driven from both ends. Maturation opens possibilities - better optics, binocular wiring, faster processing - and experience fills them, as the baby chooses what to look at and practices interpreting the stream. Deprivation studies, including children treated late for dense congenital cataracts, show that early visual input matters for normal development, while also revealing surprising recovery capacity when input arrives later.

The senses that follow, hearing and the chemical senses, tell the same story with a twist: they start far more mature, because their construction began before birth.
