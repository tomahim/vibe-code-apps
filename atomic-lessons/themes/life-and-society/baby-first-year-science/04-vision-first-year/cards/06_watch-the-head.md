Because vision matures on a loose schedule, certain behaviors reliably mark progress: fixing on a face in the first weeks, tracking a moving object by about three months, reaching accurately for what is seen, and noticing small objects like crumbs in the second half of the year. Each behavior reflects a different piece of the visual system coming online.

Eye contact deserves a special word because parents watch it so closely. Gaze develops gradually: fleeting in the first weeks, then increasingly sustained and mutual by two to three months, when social smiling arrives alongside it. Brief or variable eye contact in the early weeks is normal; what matters is the trajectory toward shared attention.

As throughout this course, single observations diagnose nothing. Professionals assess vision with structured tests and the full developmental picture; educational content like this describes the science, not a screening tool.
