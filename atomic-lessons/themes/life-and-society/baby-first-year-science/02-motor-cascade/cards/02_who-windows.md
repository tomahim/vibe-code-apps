How wide is normal? The WHO Motor Development Study followed 816 children in Ghana, India, Norway, Oman and the United States, checking six gross motor milestones from 4 to 24 months. The result was a set of windows of achievement bounded by the 1st and 99th percentiles, meaning the range in which healthy children actually reach each skill.

The numbers are striking. Sitting without support: 3.8 to 9.2 months. Hands-and-knees crawling: 5.2 to 13.5. Standing alone: 6.9 to 16.9. Walking alone: 8.2 to 17.6 months. For walking, the window of normal variation spans nearly a year.

These windows exist precisely because healthy babies vary so much. The study describes what typically developing children across very different settings do; it does not set deadlines. A baby at either edge of a window can be completely typical, which is why professionals look at patterns and trajectories rather than single dates ([WHO Motor Development Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16817682/)).
