Walking rarely arrives as a single breakthrough. First babies pull to stand using furniture, then cruise sideways while holding on, then stand alone for a few wobbly seconds, and only then come the first independent steps. Each phase can last weeks or months, and babies often move back and forth between them.

New walkers practice on an industrial scale. Researcher Karen Adolph's lab has shown that toddlers just starting to walk take thousands of steps and fall dozens of times per day, treating carpets, slopes and stairs as a full-time laboratory. Falling is not a failure of the system; it is how the system calibrates balance.

Even after the celebrated first steps, walking stays unsteady for months. Steps are wide, arms held high, turns negotiated carefully. The WHO window for walking alone runs from 8.2 to 17.6 months, and both edges are normal.
