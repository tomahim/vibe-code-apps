title: "The hidden skill: calibration"

Underneath every milestone sits a less visible skill: calibration. Each new posture changes the physics of the body. A baby who has learned to judge what is reachable while sitting must partly relearn those judgments when standing, because the center of gravity, the sway pattern and the view have all changed.

This is why progress can look uneven. A baby who confidently judges slopes as a crawler may refuse the same slopes as a new walker, not from fearfulness but because the old calibration does not transfer to the new posture. Adolph's research shows each posture demands its own weeks of practice before judgments become accurate.

Seen this way, the motor cascade is not a row of trophies but a series of new problems the baby solves in turn. Head control, sitting, crawling and walking are less like items on a checklist and more like learning to play related but distinct instruments.
