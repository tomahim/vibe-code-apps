title: "Why the ranges are so wide"
archived: false
---
If development were a fixed program, babies would hit milestones on schedule. Instead, the WHO windows stretch across months, because many ingredients vary. Body build matters: heavier babies sometimes sit and walk slightly later because they have more mass to control against gravity. Temperament matters too: bold babies attempt risky moves earlier, cautious ones perfect each stage before advancing.

Opportunity is a real factor. Babies who spend more waking time free on the floor get more practice than babies often held or kept in containers. Culture shapes these opportunities, as the next lesson shows. For babies born early, pediatricians count from the due date rather than the birth date, called corrected age, until about two years.

None of these ingredients works alone, and none predicts a child's future abilities. Wide ranges are the signature of a healthy, variable system, not a ranking table.
