The classic sequence is common but not compulsory. In the WHO study, about 90% of children achieved five of the six milestones in the expected order, yet 4.3% never crawled on hands and knees at all - and went on to walk normally. Some babies bottom-shuffle, some commando-crawl on the belly, and some simply stand up one day.

Crawling itself comes in dialects: hands-and-knees, belly-dragging, bear-walking on hands and feet, rolling as transport. Each style solves the same problem - getting to something interesting - with the body's current strengths.

The practical lesson is that the sequence describes a tendency, not a law. Skipping a textbook stage is a well-documented normal variant. What researchers and clinicians watch for is steady progress across the whole picture: is the baby gaining new ways to move and explore over weeks, whatever the style?
