Watch a newborn and then a nine-month-old, and you can read the direction of development. Newborns can barely lift their heads; older babies sit, crawl and pull to stand. Motor control spreads from head to tail, a pattern researchers call cephalocaudal: babies stabilize the head first, then the trunk, then the legs.

A second pattern runs from center to edge, called proximodistal. Control of the shoulders arrives before control of the hands, and whole-arm swiping comes before the fingertip pincer grasp. This is why a baby can bat at a toy months before picking up a crumb.

These two rules explain the famous sequence: head control, rolling, sitting, crawling, standing, walking. The order is not arbitrary decoration; it reflects which parts of the body and nervous system come online first. What the rules do not dictate is timing, and that is where the next cards go.
