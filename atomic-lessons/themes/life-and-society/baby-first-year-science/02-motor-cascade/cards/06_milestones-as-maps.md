title: "Milestones are maps, not deadlines"

Milestone lists feel like due dates, but their own designers say otherwise. When the CDC revised its developmental checklists in 2022, each milestone was placed at an age by which at least 75% of children would be expected to show the skill. The goal was to make a missing milestone actionable - a prompt to look closer - rather than to describe an average baby.

The CDC is explicit about what these checklists are: communication tools to support conversations between families and professionals. They are not screening or diagnostic tools, and they do not contain every milestone a child might show.

Read that way, the lists are a map of what most children do, drawn from published evidence, with wide individual variation built into the terrain. A baby outside a line is a reason for a conversation, not a verdict.
