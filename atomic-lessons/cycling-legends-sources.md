# Cycling legends: sources and review notes

Main sources: Wikipedia profiles and race articles (fetched 2026-10-30) as starting points, cross-checked against official and dated sports reporting for every load-bearing or recent claim, plus the sources linked on each lesson's seventh card, listed below. Results and records are as of 2026-10-30 and should be re-checked before edits.

Review notes (fact-check pass, 2026-10-30):
- Tadej Pogačar's 2026 Tour de France win, his record-equalling fifth title, was confirmed with Reuters (26 July 2026), Guinness World Records and ESPN/AP reporting.
- Pogačar's 2026 Vuelta stage-8 crash (29 August 2026), his C7 neck fracture, collarbone surgery and season ending, plus his contract running to 2032, were confirmed with BBC Sport, Cyclingnews and Olympics.com.
- Lance Armstrong's sanctions come from primary sources: the USADA reasoned decision (10 October 2012), the USADA sanction of 24 August 2012 (life ban, results annulled from 1 August 1998 including all seven Tour titles), and the UCI's confirmation of 26 October 2012. The lesson covers the doping case factually and avoids invented detail.
- The 2025 Kigali Road Worlds as the first in Africa, and Pogačar's 77 km solo defence, were confirmed with UCI and BBC reporting.
- Two draft errors were caught and fixed during verification: the 2022 Milan-San Remo winner is Matej Mohorič (not Biniam Girmay), and early drafts of the 2026 season facts were corrected against the sources above.
- The Triple Crown club (Merckx 1974, Roche 1987, Pogačar 2024) and Sean Kelly's seven straight Paris-Nice wins (1982-1988) were checked against race history sources; the Jeannie Longo five road world titles figure is the widely documented record.
